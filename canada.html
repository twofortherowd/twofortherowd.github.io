Canada — two adventures,
fifteen years apart.

Canada has given us two completely different trips and some of our greatest travel memories. In 2009 we crossed Eastern Canada from Toronto to Newfoundland. Fifteen years later we came back for Calgary, the Stampede, the Canadian Rockies and Vancouver — before sailing north to Alaska.

Canada at a glance

One country. Two very different journeys.

🍁 Eastern Canada

Our 2009 road trip took us from Toronto through Montréal, Québec City, Ottawa and Niagara Falls, then east to Prince Edward Island, Nova Scotia and Newfoundland.

⛰️ Western Canada

In 2024 we returned for Calgary and the Stampede, a three-day Rockies adventure through Yoho, Banff and Jasper, then finished in Vancouver.

🇮🇪 Biggest surprise

Newfoundland. The Irish accents, music, place names and the Irish Loop on the Avalon Peninsula stopped us in our tracks.

🧊 Biggest wow moment

Walking on the Athabasca Glacier, surrounded by the Canadian Rockies, was one of those travel moments we'll never forget.

A country shaped by two languages

🇫🇷 Why is part of Canada French-speaking?

French roots in Canada stretch back more than four centuries, to the settlements of New France in the early 1600s. After Britain took control of New France in the 18th century, French-speaking communities remained — especially along the St Lawrence River — and their language, culture and identity endured.

Today, English and French are Canada's two official languages at federal level. Québec is the heartland of French-speaking Canada, while Francophone and Acadian communities are found well beyond the province too. Travelling through Montréal and Québec City made that side of Canada especially vivid for us: the language, architecture and atmosphere can feel dramatically different from Toronto just a few hours away.

Road trip · 2009

Eastern Canada — Toronto to Newfoundland

In 2009 we embarked on one of our greatest road trips — starting in Toronto and travelling all the way to Newfoundland. It gave us cosmopolitan cities, French and British heritage, Niagara Falls, the Atlantic provinces and one of the strongest Irish connections we've ever encountered abroad.

“We knew about Newfoundland's Irish heritage. We weren't prepared for how familiar it would actually feel.”
🏙️ Toronto→🇫🇷 Montréal→⚜️ Québec City→🥁 Ottawa→💦 Niagara→🏝️ PEI→⚓ Nova Scotia→🇮🇪 Newfoundland

🏙️ Toronto

Our starting point for the 2009 trip: a huge, energetic, multicultural city and the launch pad for the journey east.

🇫🇷 Montréal

French-Canadian culture, Old Montréal, food, music and a city with a character completely its own.

⚜️ Québec City

One of our favourite parts of the entire 2009 trip. The old streets, French language, historic architecture and views around Château Frontenac gave Québec City a character unlike anywhere else we visited in Canada.

🥁 Ottawa

Canada's capital surprised us. Parliament, museums and seeing traditional Pow Wow dancing became one of the trip's most memorable cultural experiences.

💦 Niagara Falls

No photograph prepares you for the noise, spray and sheer volume of water. It exceeded the hype for us.

🏝️ Prince Edward Island

Red-soiled farmland, beaches, fishing villages and a gentler pace — a completely different side of Canada.

⚓ Nova Scotia

Atlantic coastline, seafood culture, colourful communities and strong Celtic connections.

🇮🇪 Newfoundland & the Irish Loop

This was the extraordinary surprise. The accents, place names, music and warmth felt uncannily familiar. Driving the Irish Loop on the Avalon Peninsula became one of the most unexpectedly emotional travel experiences we've ever had.

04
Rockies adventure · 2024

Western Canada — Calgary, the Rockies & Vancouver

Fifteen years after Eastern Canada, we came back for a completely different adventure: Calgary for the world-famous Stampede, three packed days through the Canadian Rockies, the Columbia Icefield and Jasper, then Vancouver before boarding our Alaskan cruise.

🤠 Calgary→🏔️ Yoho & Banff→🧊 Columbia Icefield→🦌 Jasper→🌊 Vancouver

🤠 Calgary & the Stampede

The Stampede was one of the greatest events we've attended anywhere: rodeo, chuckwagon racing, live music, fairground energy and an entire city getting involved. Calgary was also our gateway to the mountains.

⛰️ Our 3-day Canadian Rockies itinerary

We did a guided three-day tour, which let us cover an enormous amount without having to organise every stop ourselves.

Day 1 — Calgary → Yoho → Lake Louise → Moraine Lake → Banff

  • Moraine Lake
  • Natural Bridge
  • Emerald Lake
  • Lower Spiral Tunnels
Day 2 — Banff → Bow Lake → Columbia Icefield → Athabasca Falls → Jasper
  • Bow Lake
  • Icefields Parkway
  • Columbia Icefield & Athabasca Glacier
  • Athabasca Falls
  • Jasper
Day 3 — Jasper → Maligne Lake → Peyto Lake → Calgary
  • Maligne Lake
  • Maligne Canyon
  • Medicine Lake
  • Peyto Lake
  • Crowfoot Glacier

🧊 The Icefield Explorer

One of the most unusual experiences of the trip. The Icefield Explorer took us onto the Athabasca Glacier, where we stepped out and actually walked on the ice surrounded by mountains.

💎 The lakes

Lake Louise, Moraine Lake, Peyto Lake, Bow Lake, Emerald Lake and Maligne Lake — the turquoise and jade colours were every bit as spectacular as we'd hoped.

🌊 Vancouver

After the mountains, Vancouver felt like another world: glass towers, ocean, mountains on the horizon. The Panorama Seaplane tour was a standout, and the hop-on hop-off bus gave us an easy way to cover the city before our cruise.

Before you go

What we'd keep in mind for Canada

🗺️ Don't underestimate the scale

Canada is enormous. Our two trips worked because each focused on a region rather than trying to cross the whole country at once.

🤠 Time Calgary carefully

If the Stampede is part of your plan, build the western itinerary around those dates rather than trying to squeeze it in.

⛰️ A guided Rockies tour worked for us

We covered a huge number of major stops in three days without having to manage the driving and logistics ourselves.

🚢 Vancouver pairs brilliantly with Alaska

We used Vancouver as the final Canadian stop before sailing north. Read the separate Alaska diary for that part of the journey.

Common questions

Canada FAQ

Where did your 2009 Eastern Canada trip start?

Toronto. From there we travelled through Montréal, Québec City, Ottawa and Niagara Falls before continuing to Prince Edward Island, Nova Scotia and Newfoundland.

What were your favourite parts of Eastern Canada?

Québec City was one of our favourite stops of the whole trip, while Newfoundland surprised us with its strong Irish connection and the unforgettable drive around the Irish Loop.

Was the Calgary Stampede worth it?

For us, absolutely. It was one of the greatest events we've attended anywhere, and the whole city atmosphere was part of the experience.

Was the three-day Canadian Rockies tour worth it?

Yes. It let us see Lake Louise, Moraine Lake, Yoho, the Columbia Icefield, Jasper, Maligne Lake, Peyto Lake and much more without having to organise every leg ourselves.

What was a standout experience in Vancouver?

The Panorama Seaplane tour. Taking off from the downtown waterfront and seeing Vancouver, Burrard Inlet and the surrounding mountains from the air was spectacular.
