🌴 Our Caribbean Story

A year in Martinique, and a region worth exploring further

Living in Martinique gave us the chance to experience Caribbean life beyond a normal holiday. Instead of a week in a resort, we had a base — the Island of Flowers — from which to properly get to know the wider region, one island at a time.

What struck us most was how different neighbouring islands can be, sometimes after only a short ferry ride or a quick flight. French, British, Dutch and independent Caribbean influences all sit within easy reach of one another, and each brings its own language, food, music and pace of life. Martinique and Guadeloupe feel distinctly French. Anguilla and Dominica feel distinctly their own. Sint Maarten and Saint Martin manage to be two countries on one small island. Jamaica is its own world entirely.

Where transport allowed, we travelled by ferry or short flight rather than sticking to package tours, and we tried to discover places independently — hiring cars, stopping wherever looked interesting, finding beaches and villages and bars that most visitors staying in a single resort would never see. That approach is what made our Caribbean travels so memorable, and it's the thread running through every one of the guides below.

🧳 Before You Go

Before you go — the Caribbean isn't one country

A handful of practical things worth knowing before island-hopping around the region. Requirements and conditions change, so always check the latest information for your specific destination before you travel.

💱
Currencies vary

The Euro is used in the French territories, the US and Eastern Caribbean dollars are common elsewhere, and Jamaica has its own currency.

🗣️
Languages vary

French in Martinique, Guadeloupe, St Barths and Saint Martin; English across most of the rest of the islands we've visited, with Dutch also spoken in Sint Maarten.

🇫🇷🇬🇧🇳🇱
Different jurisdictions

French, British, Dutch and independent Caribbean nations all sit within the region, each with their own rules, currency and administration.

📋
Entry requirements vary

Requirements differ by island and can change — always check the current entry requirements for your specific destination before travelling.

⛴️
Ferries and flights

Some neighbouring islands are connected by short ferry crossings, while others need a flight — transport links vary a lot across the region.

🌦️
Hurricane season

Tropical weather and hurricane season run roughly from June to November — worth factoring into when you plan to travel.

🚗
Driving rules can vary

Which side of the road you drive on, licence requirements and road conditions all vary from island to island.

🌍
Local cultures vary enormously

Pace of life, food, music and everyday culture change dramatically as you move between islands, even ones close together.

🍽️ Food & Culture

Food and culture across the islands

One of the things our Caribbean travels made clear is that there is no single "Caribbean food" — the region's cuisine and culture change island to island. In Martinique, Guadeloupe and St Barths, French influence runs through everything, from the food to the language to the general atmosphere, alongside distinct Creole traditions of their own. Move to Anguilla, Dominica, St Lucia or Jamaica and the flavours, music and pace of life shift again, shaped by British, independent and other Caribbean traditions.

That contrast — French-Caribbean sophistication one island over from rainforest villages or reggae-filled beach bars — is part of what made exploring the region so rewarding. It's a theme that runs through each of our individual guides above.

Common questions

Caribbean FAQ

Which Caribbean islands have Two for the Row'd visited?
We've explored eight Caribbean destinations so far: Martinique (where we lived for a year), St Barths, St Lucia, Sint Maarten & Saint Martin, Anguilla, Guadeloupe, Dominica and Jamaica. Each has its own guide with our honest experiences, photos and practical tips.
Can you island-hop around the Caribbean?
Yes, to an extent — some neighbouring islands have short ferry crossings or quick flights between them, which made it possible for us to base ourselves in Martinique and explore further afield. Transport links vary a lot from island to island though, so it's worth checking routes and schedules carefully before you plan a trip.
Are all Caribbean islands similar?
Not at all. The Caribbean is not one homogeneous destination — French-Caribbean islands like Martinique, Guadeloupe and St Barths feel completely different to independent nations like Jamaica or British territories like Anguilla. Language, currency, food, music and pace of life all change from island to island, sometimes within a very short ferry ride.
When is the best time to visit the Caribbean?
Generally the drier season from around December to April is the most popular time to visit, while hurricane season runs roughly from June to November. That said, weather patterns vary by island and by year, so it's always worth checking the current forecast and advisories for your specific destination before booking.
Which Caribbean island should I choose?
It depends entirely on what you're after — extraordinary beaches, rainforest and waterfalls, French-Caribbean culture, music and nightlife, dramatic volcanic landscapes or independent self-drive exploring.
