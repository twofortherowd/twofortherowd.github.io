Nicola has her own unforgettable memories of a 2004 Grand Prix weekend.

🏁 A weekend that became a memory

August 2004 — Nicola's Grand Prix weekend

The story of a 2004 Belgian Grand Prix weekend.

"I had pit passes for the weekend. You don't get those often. The energy of the Paddock, the focus of the teams, the roar of the cars — it got into my blood. And Jacques Villeneuve was my F1 hero. I was hoping against hope I might see him. It didn't happen, but the weekend stayed with me." — Nicola

In August 2004, Nicola attended the Belgian Grand Prix at Spa-Francorchamps with pit passes for the weekend. This was not a casual sporting event — this was behind-the-scenes access to one of the world's greatest Formula 1 circuits, to the Paddock where teams work, where drivers prepare, where the real racing machinery lives.

For any motorsport fan, Spa is sacred ground. The circuit is considered one of the greatest in the world — fast, demanding, dramatic. The sweep through Eau Rouge and Raidillon, the fast sections through the Ardennes and the circuit's famously changeable weather all contribute to Spa-Francorchamps' reputation.

Behind the scenes

Having pit passes for the weekend made the trip especially memorable, giving Nicola a much closer look at the world of Formula 1 than a normal race ticket would.

Jacques Villeneuve and the moment that didn't happen

Jacques Villeneuve, the 1997 World Champion, was Nicola's Formula 1 hero. With pit passes for the weekend, she hoped she might finally get the chance to meet him. It never happened — but it remains one of the stories she remembers from the trip.

Le Grand Maur

One of the other memories from the trip was dinner at Le Grand Maur in Spa. The historic stone setting, warm interior and multi-course dining made it feel like a proper occasion after a day immersed in Formula 1.

The meal was a gastronomic, multi-course experience and became part of the memory of the weekend alongside Spa-Francorchamps, the pit passes and the hope of spotting Villeneuve.

The weekend was Spa and Formula 1 and motorsport passion. But it was also the Ardennes landscape, the thermal heritage of the town, the gastronomy of Belgium. It was the whole experience layered together.

🏛️ Spa the town

Beyond the circuit — the thermal heritage

Spa-Francorchamps is legendary motorsport, but the town of Spa itself has centuries of history as a destination for thermal cures. The mineral-rich thermal springs attracted visitors as early as the 14th century, when people believed the waters had healing properties. That heritage remains — there are thermal facilities and a spa culture in the town independent of Formula 1.

The Ardennes setting is beautiful. Rolling hills, forests, the landscape is distinctly different from the cities of Flanders. Spa sits in this landscape with character and charm. It's a proper destination, not just a circuit venue.

If you're visiting Spa and not attending a Grand Prix weekend, you'll find a pleasant historic town with thermal facilities, hiking in the Ardennes, cafés and restaurants, and the story of Belgium's thermal heritage. If you're visiting during the Grand Prix — that's when the town becomes electric with motorsport passion.

🚂 Getting to Spa

Reaching Spa

From Brussels: Spa can be reached by rail, usually with a connection. Check current SNCB/NMBS journey times and connections for your travel date.

From Liège: Liège is a useful gateway for reaching Spa by public transport, and the two work well together on a Wallonia itinerary.

Day trip or stay over? The distance from Brussels makes Spa better as an overnight destination. If you're visiting Spa-Francorchamps during the Grand Prix, you'll definitely stay overnight (or longer). Even visiting the circuit outside race week, 1–2 nights allows you to experience the town and landscape properly.

🏎️ Visiting Spa-Francorchamps

The circuit experience

During the Belgian Grand Prix

The Belgian Grand Prix transforms Spa. Tickets are competitive. Hospitality packages offer access. Pit pass experiences like Nicola had are rare but possible. The energy is extraordinary. This is where serious F1 fans make their pilgrimage.

Outside race week

Outside race weekends, the circuit hosts a busy programme of events and experiences. Check the circuit's current visitor information before travelling, as access varies according to the calendar.

The circuit itself

Spa-Francorchamps is considered one of the greatest F1 tracks in the world. Fast, technical, dramatic in all weather. Its combination of elevation changes, fast corners and Ardennes setting makes it one of the most distinctive circuits on the Formula 1 calendar.

Common questions

Spa FAQ

Is Spa worth visiting if you're not an F1 fan?
Absolutely. The town of Spa itself has historic thermal spa heritage, beautiful Ardennes setting, and charm completely independent of Formula 1. The thermal baths and spa facilities are excellent. The historic centre is pleasant. It's a proper destination, not just a motorsport venue. F1 fans will love Spa-Francorchamps; non-fans will appreciate the town and Ardennes landscape.
What is Spa-Francorchamps?
Spa-Francorchamps is a legendary Formula 1 circuit in the Ardennes, home to the Belgian Grand Prix. It's considered one of the greatest racing circuits in the world — fast, technical, dramatic in all weather. The lap takes drivers through the town and across public roads modified for racing. It's the heartbeat of Belgian motorsport.
Can you visit Spa-Francorchamps?
Yes. You can visit the circuit, walk parts of it, and visit the museum. During Belgian Grand Prix week, the full hospitality experience requires tickets. Outside race week, you can explore the circuit, stand on corners, understand the layout and history. It's a pilgrimage for motorsport fans.
How long should you spend in Spa?
A full day or overnight. If you're visiting Spa-Francorchamps, allow several hours to walk the circuit, visit the museum, and soak up the motorsport history. Add time for the town itself, the thermal facilities if interested, and exploring the Ardennes landscape. 1–2 nights is ideal.
What's the thermal spa heritage?
Spa has been a destination for thermal water cures since the 14th century. The mineral-rich thermal springs attracted visitors seeking health benefits. While modern spa science has evolved, the town maintains its spa heritage with thermal facilities and the character of a historic spa destination. It's a charming aspect of the town independent of F1.
