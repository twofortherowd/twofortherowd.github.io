where medieval history meets creative energy

🏙️ Medieval meets modern

Ghent's own character

Ghent has a character entirely of its own.

"Ghent has the medieval architecture and canals of Bruges, but the energy is completely different. This is a city that feels alive. Young, creative, independent. The restaurants are excellent. The streets have energy. The people are proud of their city in a way that feels genuine, not touristic."

The Graslei and Korenlei waterfronts, with their historic guild houses reflected in the water, are among Ghent's great postcard views. But the city is more than its medieval centre: step away from the obvious sights and you find a lively contemporary place with galleries, cafés, shops and street art.

Street art covers the buildings. Independent boutiques and galleries fill the side streets. The food scene is world-class — Ghent has thriving vegetarian and vegan culture alongside traditional Flemish restaurants. The nightlife is lively. Cafés and bars are packed with locals, not just tourists. This is where Ghent differentiates itself from Bruges.

Graslei & Korenlei

These are the show-piece waterfront areas where historic guild houses line the canals. Perfect for a walk, perfect for a canal boat tour, perfect for sitting at a café and watching the city move. But there's so much more to Ghent than the postcard views.

Beyond the main sights

The Gravensteen (castle) is impressive. St. Bavo's Cathedral has the famous Ghent Altarpiece. But the real Ghent is in the independent galleries, the design shops, the restaurants where locals eat, the cafés where people sit for hours. Ghent rewards those who wander and explore.

The food scene

Ghent has an excellent food culture. Belgian beer and traditional Flemish food, yes. But also an innovative restaurant scene, excellent vegetarian and vegan options, street food, cafés where quality matters. Eating in Ghent is a key part of the experience.

This is a city that knows what it is and doesn't apologise for it. It's not trying to be Bruges. It's proudly, defiantly itself.

🎯 Essential Ghent

The key sights and experiences

💧
Graslei Waterfront

Historic guild houses reflected in the canal. One of Belgium's most beautiful views. Walk it, photograph it, sit at a café on it.

🏰
Gravensteen

Medieval castle with towers offering views over Ghent. Historical significance and architectural importance.

⛪
St. Bavo's Cathedral

Contains the famous Ghent Altarpiece by Van Eyck. One of the greatest paintings of the medieval period.

🎨
Street Art & Galleries

Independent galleries, design shops and street art throughout. A thriving creative scene you won't find in guidebooks.

🍷
Café Culture

Belgian beer in historic cafés, modern bars, street-side seating. Ghent's social life revolves around its cafés.

🍽️
Restaurant Scene

Excellent food — traditional, innovative, vegetarian. Ghent is a food destination with world-class restaurants.

🚂 Getting to Ghent

Ghent is easy to reach from Brussels

From Brussels: Ghent is straightforward to reach by direct train, making it an easy day trip or overnight destination.

From Bruges: Ghent and Bruges are well connected by rail, so combining the two cities is straightforward.

Day trip or stay over? You can see the main sights as a day trip from Brussels. But Ghent comes alive in the evening — the restaurants fill up, the bars get lively, the city reveals itself as a living place, not a museum. Staying overnight is worth it.

Common questions

Ghent FAQ

Is Ghent worth visiting?
Absolutely. Ghent is one of Europe's most underrated cities. It has medieval architecture and canals like Bruges, but with a distinctly vibrant, artistic and independent character. The food scene is excellent, the atmosphere is lively, and there's a creative energy that makes Ghent feel alive and modern alongside the history.
What is Ghent known for?
Ghent is known for: the Graslei waterfront with historic buildings, medieval canals and bridges, the Gravensteen (castle), St. Bavo's Cathedral with the famous Ghent Altarpiece, vibrant street art and independent character, excellent restaurants and food scene, vegetarian/vegan food culture, lively nightlife and café culture.
How long should you spend in Ghent?
A day trip from Brussels gives you time to explore the main sights. But Ghent is better with a full day or overnight stay — it allows you to experience the nightlife, eat in the restaurants properly, and feel the city's independent creative energy. 1–2 nights is ideal.
Is Ghent just 'Bruges for younger people'?
No. While Ghent has medieval architecture and canals like Bruges, it's a distinctly different city with its own character. Ghent is more vibrant, gritty, artistic and modern. The food scene is excellent. There's street art and independent culture. Ghent rewards those who explore beyond the obvious sights.
What should you eat in Ghent?
Ghent has an excellent food culture. Belgian beer, street food, restaurants ranging from casual to fine dining. Ghent has a thriving vegetarian/vegan scene. Take time to eat properly — this is where you experience the city's creative energy.
Can you visit Ghent as a day trip from Brussels?
Yes — Ghent is easy to reach by direct train from Brussels and works well as a day trip. But staying overnight is better if you can — Ghent comes alive in the evening with restaurants, cafés and nightlife.
