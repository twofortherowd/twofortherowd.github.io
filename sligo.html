Some counties have beautiful beaches. Others have spectacular mountains. A few are steeped in fascinating history. County Sligo decided it wanted the lot!

Tucked away in the north-west, Sligo manages to feel both wild and welcoming: dramatic landscapes, lively seaside villages, prehistoric monuments, and mountains that look like they belong in a fantasy film.

It's Yeats Country, too, and whether you're into surfing, hiking, history, poetry or just a great view with a decent cup of coffee, there's plenty here, with quiet corners where you can escape the crowds.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Sligo, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🌊 Beach safety in Sligo

Swimming is prohibited at Strandhill because of dangerous currents. Elsewhere, strong currents, big waves and fast tides are real hazards, and a beautiful beach isn't always a safe one for swimming. Check local signs and lifeguard information.

Benbulben, Strandhill & Yeats Country
Mountain · Viewpoints

⛰️ Benbulben

Few mountains in Ireland are as instantly recognisable. Benbulben's flat-topped limestone profile dominates the landscape north of Sligo town, part of the Dartry Mountains.

You don't need to climb it. The views from the roads around Drumcliff and the lower-level walks are spectacular.

Why visit? Photography, landscapes and walking.

⚠️ The upper slopes and cliffs are dangerous. Only experienced hillwalkers with the right gear and good conditions should attempt mountain routes.
Surf village · No swimming

🏄 Strandhill

One of Ireland's best-known surf spots: a lively coastal village with Atlantic waves, cafés and a relaxed atmosphere. Get in the surf with a qualified instructor, or stay on dry land with a coffee and watch.

Why visit? Surfing, coastal walks and food.

🚫 Swimming is prohibited at Strandhill beach because of dangerous currents. Only surf with a qualified school or local guidance.
Walk · Legend

🪨 Knocknarea & Queen Maeve's Cairn

Knocknarea rises above the coast west of Sligo, crowned by a huge prehistoric cairn that tradition links with Queen Maeve of Connacht. The Queen Maeve Trail climbs to the summit for sweeping views.

Why visit? Walking, mythology and panoramic views.

⛰️ The summit path is steep in places. Please don't climb on or take stones from the cairn.

Prehistory · Seasonal

🌀 Carrowmore Megalithic Cemetery

One of Ireland's most important prehistoric burial landscapes, with dozens of ancient tombs and Knocknarea rising in the distance.

Why visit? Archaeology and prehistory.

📅 The visitor centre opens seasonally, so check before you go.
Literature · Free

✒️ Drumcliff & W.B. Yeats's grave

Beneath Benbulben, the churchyard at Drumcliff is where W.B. Yeats is buried, under his famous self-written epitaph: "Cast a cold eye / On life, on death. / Horseman, pass by!" There's an early monastic high cross and round tower stump here too.

Why visit? Literature, history and a beautiful setting.

Seaside village

🌅 Rosses Point

A seaside village on Sligo Bay with sandy beaches, maritime history and views to the mountains. It has strong links with the painter Jack B. Yeats.

Why visit? Seaside walks, photography and families.

Around the county
Abbeys, lakes, beaches & tombs
Medieval · Town

⛪ Sligo Abbey

The remains of a 13th-century Dominican friary in the heart of Sligo town, with carved tombs, cloisters and a rare surviving medieval high altar.

Why visit? Medieval history and architecture.

Beach · Seaweed baths

🏖️ Enniscrone

A long golden beach in west Sligo, known for walks, surfing, golf and traditional seaweed baths.

Why visit? Families and relaxed seaside days.

Wildlife · Family

🦅 Eagles Flying, Ballymote

A bird-of-prey and animal sanctuary with flying demonstrations that families love.

Why visit? Families and wildlife lovers.

📅 Check current opening dates and demonstration times.
Lake · Literature

🛶 Lough Gill

A beautiful wooded lake just east of Sligo town. Its tiny island of Innisfree inspired Yeats's poem The Lake Isle of Innisfree.

Why visit? Nature, literature and peaceful scenery.

Prehistory · Remote

🗻 Carrowkeel Passage Tombs

High in the Bricklieve Mountains, these passage tombs sit in a dramatic, remote setting with huge views.

Why visit? Archaeology and experienced walkers.

⚠️ The tombs are extremely fragile. Never enter restricted chambers or climb on the monuments, and check trail conditions first.
Harbour village

🌊 Mullaghmore

A pretty harbour village with wonderful coastal views. The waves offshore are famous among the world's big-wave surfers.

Why visit? Coastal scenery and photography.

⚠️ Big-wave surfing here is for highly trained professionals, not visitors.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Legend

👑 Queen Maeve of Knocknarea

Local tradition says the great cairn on Knocknarea is the grave of Queen Maeve, the fierce warrior queen of the Táin, buried standing up and facing her enemies in Ulster.

The cairn itself is prehistoric and far older than any story about Maeve. The link with her is a much later legend.

Legend

🐗 Diarmuid and Gráinne

Gráinne was promised to the ageing Fionn Mac Cumhaill but ran away with the young warrior Diarmuid. In many versions, Diarmuid is killed by a wild boar on Benbulben, and Fionn lets him die rather than save him.

Literature

🛶 The Lake Isle of Innisfree

Yeats wrote about longing for the tiny island in Lough Gill while homesick in London. It's literature rather than folklore, but it's as much a part of Sligo's identity as any legend.

History

⚓ The Spanish Armada at Streedagh

In September 1588, three ships of the retreating Spanish Armada were wrecked off Streedagh, with huge loss of life. Captain Francisco de Cuéllar survived and later wrote a remarkable account of his escape through the north-west.

Archaeology

🌀 The tombs of Carrowmore

The cemetery at Carrowmore is among the oldest in Ireland, showing this landscape mattered to people thousands of years before the legends of Maeve and Fionn were told.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚆 Getting around
Sligo town has train and bus links. A car helps for beaches, mountains and remote sites.
🗓️ Best time
May to September for long days. Autumn is especially atmospheric.
⏳ How long
Two or three days for an introduction, four or five at a relaxed pace.
🥾 Walking
Mountain weather changes fast. Check routes and conditions before you set off.
🌊 Beaches
Strong currents, big waves and changing tides are serious hazards.
🗺️ County lines
Glencar Waterfall is in County Leitrim, not Sligo.

Opening times and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Visit Sligo, Sligo Walks, Heritage Ireland and Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Sligo FAQ
Can you swim at Strandhill?
No. Swimming is prohibited at Strandhill beach because of dangerous currents. It's a surf beach, and you should only go in with a qualified surf school.
Is Glencar Waterfall in Sligo?
No, it's just over the border in County Leitrim, although lots of Sligo trips include it because it's so close.
Can you climb Benbulben?
Experienced hillwalkers do, in good conditions, but the upper slopes and cliffs are dangerous. Most visitors enjoy it from the viewpoints and lower walks around Drumcliff.
How many days do you need in Sligo?
Two or three days for an introduction, and four or five to explore at a relaxed pace.
