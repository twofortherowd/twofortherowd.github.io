More than 1,000 years
Trade, revolution and German history

A city with a serious place in history

Leipzig was first recorded in 1015 and grew where two important European trade routes — the Via Regia and Via Imperii — crossed. That location helped turn it into a major market and trade-fair city, a tradition that shaped the passages and courtyards you still see in the centre today.

Its history is not all medieval. The enormous Monument to the Battle of the Nations commemorates the Battle of Leipzig in 1813, when Napoleon's forces faced a coalition of European armies in one of the defining battles of the Napoleonic Wars.

And then there is 1989. Peace prayers at St Nicholas Church fed into the Monday demonstrations that became central to the Peaceful Revolution in East Germany. On 9 October 1989, around 70,000 people demonstrated peacefully in Leipzig. One month later, the Berlin Wall fell.

03
Music city
Bach, St Thomas Church & Leipzig's musical soul

Johann Sebastian Bach's Leipzig

Leipzig is inseparable from Johann Sebastian Bach. He worked here as Thomaskantor from 1723 until his death in 1750, directing the St Thomas Boys Choir and composing some of his greatest music during his years in the city.

St Thomas Church is still one of Leipzig's essential places for music lovers — Bach worked there for 27 years and is buried inside the church. Nearby, the Bach Museum and Archive keep that extraordinary musical legacy alive.

A taste of Saxony
What to eat & drink in Leipzig

Leipzig has its own specialities

Leipziger Allerlei is probably the best-known local dish — traditionally a mixture of young vegetables, although versions vary considerably.

For something sweet, look out for Leipziger Lerche, a small pastry associated with the city. Its name means “Leipzig lark”, but modern versions are sweet pastries rather than anything involving the bird.

Then there's Leipziger Gose, Leipzig's distinctive sour beer style. And for literary history with your food, Auerbachs Keller is one of the city's best-known historic restaurants, famously associated with Goethe's Faust.

These are general Leipzig food traditions rather than dishes Nicola remembers eating on the 2005 trip.

Common questions
Leipzig FAQ
Is Leipzig worth visiting?
Yes. Leipzig combines a beautiful historic centre with major musical and modern German history, and Nicola still remembers it as one of the loveliest stops on her 2005 Interrail trip.
How many days do you need in Leipzig?
Two days is a good amount of time for a first visit, giving you time for the historic centre, Bach sites, St Nicholas Church and some of the city's wider cultural attractions.
Why is Leipzig important in German history?
Leipzig played a central role in the Peaceful Revolution of 1989. Peace prayers at St Nicholas Church and the Monday demonstrations helped build momentum toward the fall of the East German regime and German reunification.
What is Leipzig famous for?
Leipzig is famous for Johann Sebastian Bach, its long trade-fair tradition, the Peaceful Revolution of 1989, the Monument to the Battle of the Nations and its lively cultural scene.
What food and drink should you try in Leipzig?
Traditional Leipzig specialities include Leipziger Allerlei, Leipziger Lerche and the city's distinctive sour beer style, Leipziger Gose.
Is Leipzig easy to explore without a car?
Yes. The historic centre is compact and walkable, while trams and other public transport make it easy to reach sights farther out.
