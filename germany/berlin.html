Nicola's Interrail trip
Berlin, 2005

A city shaped by division and reunification

The remnants of the Berlin Wall, Checkpoint Charlie and the Brandenburg Gate all made a lasting impression — three landmarks that between them tell the story of a city split in two and then stitched back together within living memory. The scale of the architecture and the sense of a place still working through its own history made Berlin one of the most striking stops on the whole trip.

It's the kind of city where you don't need to be a history buff to feel the significance of where you're standing — and that's exactly what stayed with Nicola nearly two decades on.

Common questions
Berlin FAQ
Did Nicola visit Berlin on the 2005 interrail trip?
Yes — Berlin was one of four stops on Nicola's 2005 interrail trip through Germany, alongside Leipzig, Cologne and Munich.
What makes Berlin different from other European capitals?
Berlin's recent history is unusually visible — remnants of the Wall, Checkpoint Charlie and the Brandenburg Gate all sit within a city that was literally divided and then reunited within living memory.
How many days do you need in Berlin?
For a first visit, three days gives you a much better chance to explore Berlin's major historic sights without trying to squeeze everything into one rushed day.
Is Berlin a good city for history lovers?
Very much so. Berlin's twentieth-century history is unusually visible in the city itself, from remnants of the Wall to Checkpoint Charlie and the Brandenburg Gate.
Can you still see the Berlin Wall?
Yes. Sections and traces of the former Wall remain around Berlin, making it possible to explore the story of the divided city on the ground rather than only in museums.
Is Berlin easy to explore without a car?
Yes. Berlin has extensive public transport, and many visitors combine trains, trams, buses and walking to get between its neighbourhoods and major sights.
