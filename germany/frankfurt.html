Frankfurt — one night in Germany's skyline city

Frankfurt was a flying visit for Nicola — just one night, many years ago. It wasn't enough time to claim to know the city inside out, but Frankfurt is one of those places a lot of travellers find themselves passing through, thanks to its enormous international airport and rail connections. So this guide combines Nicola's brief experience of the city with practical, researched ideas for making the most of a short Frankfurt stay.

02
Old meets new
Historic Frankfurt & the skyline

Two very different Frankfurts

Frankfurt has a very different identity to Cologne, Heidelberg, Munich or Berlin. It's home to one of Europe's most distinctive skylines — the nickname "Mainhattan" isn't an exaggeration — while a short walk away sits a carefully reconstructed historic old town around Römerberg. Few German cities put centuries-old, half-timbered buildings and glass-and-steel skyscrapers quite so close together.

03
Short-stay guide
If you have one day in Frankfurt

General visitor information, researched rather than drawn from personal experience.

Römerberg & the Römer

Frankfurt's historic central square, framed by reconstructed medieval and Renaissance-style buildings and the city's iconic step-gabled town hall, the Römer.

Frankfurt Cathedral

The Kaiserdom sits just off Römerberg — a red sandstone church with centuries of imperial history and a tower you can climb for city views.

The Main & Eiserner Steg

The pedestrian iron bridge across the river Main links the old town to Sachsenhausen, with good skyline views in both directions.

Museumsufer

A run of museums along the riverbank on the Sachsenhausen side, covering everything from film to applied art to world cultures.

Main Tower

One of the few Frankfurt skyscrapers with a public observation deck, giving a proper overview of the skyline the city is known for.

Goethe House

The reconstructed birthplace of Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, a short walk from Römerberg.

Palmengarten

A large botanical garden with glasshouses, popular for a quieter break from the city centre.

Sachsenhausen

The district across the Main known for its traditional apple-wine taverns and a more local, less corporate atmosphere.

04
Passing through
Frankfurt as a stopover

Making the most of a layover or a single night

Frankfurt Airport is one of Europe's busiest, and it has its own train stations built in, both regional and long-distance. Regional trains connect the airport to Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof (the main station) in roughly 10 to 15 minutes, which makes the city centre a genuinely realistic option even on a fairly short layover, provided you allow properly for security and immigration on the way back.

From the Hauptbahnhof it's a short tram or walkable distance into the old town around Römerberg. With a few hours you could reasonably manage Römerberg, the Cathedral and a walk along the Main; with a full night, adding the Main Tower or a wander through Sachsenhausen is realistic too. Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof is also one of the country's key rail hubs, with fast connections onward to Cologne, Munich, Berlin and beyond — part of why so many visitors end up passing through.

Always check current Deutsche Bahn and airport timetables before you travel, since schedules and journey times can change.

05
Local flavour
Food & drink

What Frankfurt is known for

Frankfurt has its own distinct food culture, most famously Apfelwein (apple wine), traditionally served in Sachsenhausen's taverns. Frankfurter Grüne Soße — a cold green herb sauce usually served with potatoes and egg — is a local specialty worth knowing about, along with the city's own style of Frankfurter sausage. This is general visitor information rather than a first-hand recommendation.

Common questions
Frankfurt FAQ
Is Frankfurt worth visiting or just a stopover?
Frankfurt is best known as a transport hub, but its reconstructed old town around Römerberg and its striking modern skyline make it a genuinely worthwhile stop for a day or a night, not just a place to change trains.
How long does it take to get from Frankfurt Airport to the city centre?
Frankfurt Airport has its own regional and long-distance train stations, and regional trains reach Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof (the main station) in roughly 10 to 15 minutes, making the city centre very accessible even on a short layover.
Did Nicola spend much time in Frankfurt?
No — Nicola's own visit to Frankfurt was a single night, years ago, so this guide combines that brief personal experience with researched practical information for visitors.
