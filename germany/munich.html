Munich — biergartens, Bavaria and history

The last stop on Nicola's 2005 interrail trip through Germany, and the one that delivered on every promise of Bavarian hospitality. Munich was the biergarten capital of the world and everything it claimed to be — long tables, chestnut trees, cold beer and locals who actually wanted to talk to you.

Nicola's Interrail trip
Munich, 2005

Marienplatz, the Glockenspiel and the English Garden

Marienplatz and the Glockenspiel were the obvious starting point — the scale and theatre of the square gives you an immediate sense of the city. From there, the English Garden was the real highlight: one of the great biergartens of the world under the chestnut trees, the sort of genuinely joyful, sociable travel experience that's hard to replicate anywhere else.

The sheer scale of Munich, and the Bavarian atmosphere running through it, made it a fitting final stop on the trip.

Bavaria — big squares, biergartens and brilliant memories ✨

A place of remembrance
The Dachau Memorial Site

Dachau

On the same trip, Nicola visited the Dachau Memorial Site — the first Nazi concentration camp, now preserved as a memorial and place of remembrance. It is an easy day trip from Munich, and it left a lasting impression.

Dachau is deeply moving and important. It is sobering, educational and deeply humanising, and it is a place we would encourage anyone visiting Munich to make time for. This isn't a typical tourist stop, and it shouldn't be approached as one — but it is, in its own way, essential.

Common questions
Munich FAQ
Is Munich good for biergartens?
Munich is the home of biergarten culture — cold beer, long tables, chestnut trees, locals and tourists mixing together in the most convivial atmosphere imaginable. The English Garden's biergarten is iconic but there are brilliant options all over the city.
Is Dachau worth visiting from Munich?
Yes. Dachau is a deeply moving and important place that everyone should experience at least once. It is sobering, educational and deeply humanising, and it is an easy day trip from Munich.
How many days do you need in Munich?
Three days is a good starting point for a first visit, especially if you want time for the historic centre and English Garden without rushing. Allow extra time if you plan a day trip.
Is Munich a good first city in Bavaria?
Yes. Munich combines grand city sights with unmistakably Bavarian traditions, making it an easy introduction to the region.
Is Munich easy to explore without a car?
Yes. The central sights are straightforward to combine with Munich's public transport, so a car isn't necessary for a typical city break.
