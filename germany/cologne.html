Cologne — the German city Nicola called home

This isn't just another city break in our collection. In 2015, Nicola moved to Cologne to work in a school for a year — and it changed everything. She learned German properly during her time there, adding it to her growing repertoire of languages alongside French, and fell completely in love with the city and its people. Everything on this page comes from that year, not a weekend visit.

A year in Cologne

The city that got under Nicola's skin

The Kölners have a reputation throughout Germany for being warm, laid-back and welcoming — and every bit of that reputation is earned. Cologne has a distinct identity and a fierce civic pride that you feel as soon as you arrive, and a year living there was more than enough time for Nicola to feel like a local rather than a visitor.

Working in a school gave her a rhythm to the city that most travellers never get — the everyday version of Cologne, not just the postcard one. And the German she picked up during that year has stuck with her ever since.

What not to miss
Cologne highlights

The Kölner Dom

Cologne Cathedral — the Dom — is one of the great Gothic buildings in Europe. It dominates the skyline from every angle, and up close it is genuinely awe-inspiring. The detail in the stonework, the sheer scale, the centuries of history embedded in every wall — it is unmissable, and Nicola never tired of walking past it.

🍫

The Chocolate Museum

The Schokoladenmuseum is brilliant fun — the story of chocolate from bean to bar, a chocolate fountain you can dip a wafer into, and more tastings than you can handle. Perfect for a rainy afternoon on the Rhine.

✨

4711 on Glockengasse

The 4711 shop is a Cologne institution — home of Eau de Cologne, the original fragrance that gave a whole category of perfume its name. The beautiful historic shop and free spritz at the door are part of the experience.

🌊

The Rhine

The Rhine promenade is one of the best things about the city — a proper stretch of riverside life running right through central Cologne and an easy place to simply wander.

🍀

A little bit of Ireland

Cologne's warm Irish pub scene became something of a home away from home during Nicola's year there. The friendliness of the Kölners and the familiarity of a good Irish pub proved a very good combination.

Cologne FAQ

Is Cologne worth visiting?
Absolutely — Cologne is one of Germany's most liveable and visitor-friendly cities. The Cathedral is one of the great Gothic buildings in the world, the Rhine promenade is beautiful, the Chocolate Museum is brilliant fun, and the Kölners are famously warm and welcoming. Nicola lived there for a year and loved every minute of it.
Did Nicola live in Cologne?
Yes — Nicola moved to Cologne in 2015 to work in a school for a year. She learned German properly during her time there and came away with a real fondness for the city and its people.
What is 4711 in Cologne?
4711 is the original Eau de Cologne — the fragrance that gave the whole category its name. The historic shop on Glockengasse is a Cologne institution, with a lovely free spritz on the way in.
How many days do you need in Cologne?
Two full days is a lovely amount of time for a first visit. It gives you time for the Cathedral and Old Town, a walk along the Rhine, the Chocolate Museum and 4711 without racing around. Add another day if you want a slower pace or a day trip nearby.
What is Cologne best known for?
The Kölner Dom is the city's great landmark, but Cologne is also famous for Kölsch beer, Eau de Cologne, its huge Carnival celebrations, its position on the Rhine and a famously open, friendly local character.
Is Cologne a walkable city?
Yes. Many of the places most visitors want to see are concentrated around the Cathedral, Old Town and Rhine, so central Cologne is very easy to explore on foot. Public transport is useful when you want to go farther out.
What is Kölsch?
Kölsch is Cologne's local beer style and part of the city's identity. Traditionally it is served in small, slim glasses called Stangen, often brought to the table one after another in Cologne's brewhouses.
Is Cologne good for a Christmas market trip?
Yes — Cologne is a very popular Christmas-market city, with several markets spread around the centre rather than just one. The Cathedral market has the most dramatic setting, but there are other themed markets within easy reach too.
Can you visit Cologne without speaking German?
Absolutely. Visitors can get around comfortably using English in most hotels, attractions and central restaurants. Learning a few German phrases is always appreciated, but you do not need fluent German for a city break.
