Our Marrakech

Why Marrakech gets under your skin

Marrakech is one of those cities that doesn't need any warming up. It arrives at you all at once — the heat, the pinks and ochres of the walls, the smell of spices and grilling meat, the constant negotiation of the souks, a hundred sounds at once from mopeds to muezzins to street sellers. It is disorienting on purpose. And once you find your feet in it, it's genuinely wonderful.

We were there for three nights in February 2026 — the front end of a longer Morocco trip that carried on into the Atlas Mountains and out into the Sahara. Marrakech was where the trip started and where it ended, and we're glad we gave it proper time rather than treating it as a jumping-off point.

This page is a Marrakech-specific city guide — where we stayed, what we'd point you at, the practical stuff we wish we'd known before we went. If you want the day-by-day story of the wider Morocco trip — the argan co-op, the Atlas passes, the Sahara camp under the stars — that's on the full Morocco tour review instead.

Before you go

Before you go — the practical bits

✈️ Getting there
Ryanair flies direct Dublin to Marrakech, roughly three and a half hours. One of the easiest long-haul-feeling destinations from Ireland with no connection needed.
🌙 We stayed
3 nights — enough to see Marrakech properly if you're focused. More would be lovely; fewer would feel rushed.
🏨 Where we stayed
Grand Mogador Menara for arrival, Riad Zoraida in the medina for the return. Two very different stays — we'd recommend the split.
💵 Cash
Bring dirhams and take out more than you think. Card machines are rare in the souks, and you'll want to buy things — the leather, the lanterns, the spices, the rug you didn't know you were coming for.
🍵 Mint tea, always
It's offered constantly. Say yes every time. It's essentially the national drink and it's genuinely brilliant — sweet, strong, poured from a height.
🍷 Alcohol is scarce
Mostly hotel-bar-only outside of licensed restaurants. If a pint after dinner is your thing, plan accordingly or make peace with the mint tea.
👟 Wear proper shoes
Medina cobbles are uneven and unrelenting. Broken-in walking shoes are not optional. Your feet will thank you at the end of day one.
🌾 Gluten-free notes
Nicola found Marrakech more gluten-free-friendly than expected — tagines, grilled meats, rice-based dishes and lots of naturally GF Moroccan cooking. As always: ask before you order, especially about sauces and coatings.
🚗 Explore virtually
Our partners at Drive From Home let you virtually explore Marrakech's streets — useful for getting a feel for the medina before you visit, or remembering the atmosphere after.
💡 One thing we wish we'd known
Book a walking tour on day one. The medina makes sense with a guide and swallows you without one. Do this first and everything else in your Marrakech trip gets better.
🌡️ When to go
February (when we visited) is lovely — cool mornings, warm afternoons, few crowds. Spring and autumn are ideal. Avoid July and August — the heat is genuinely punishing.

What to do in Marrakech

Things worth doing — from our three nights

Not an encyclopaedic list. What we actually did, what we'd point you at, and what we'd prioritise if you're doing Marrakech in a short window.

🕌 The medina and souks

The medina is the reason to come to Marrakech. Souks are broken down by trade — leather here, spices there, lanterns down that alley, textiles somewhere you'll never find again. It's chaotic, gorgeous, and much more navigable with a guide. Which is our next point.

🚶 Ali's walking tour (do this first)

We booked a walking tour with Ali from Get Marrakech through TourRadar. It's the single best decision we made on the whole trip — the medina makes sense, the souks make sense, the food stops are better than anything you'd find yourself. If you do one paid experience in Marrakech, make it this.

🌆 Jemaa el-Fnaa at dusk

The main square is fine during the day and extraordinary as the sun goes down — food stalls set up, storytellers and musicians appear, snake charmers, orange juice sellers, acrobats. It's not curated for tourists, it's actually where Marrakech does its evening. Best experienced from the ground rather than from a rooftop terrace, though the terraces are lovely too.

🐐 The tanneries

Fascinating if you're interested in traditional crafts, extremely fragrant if you're standing downwind. A guide will point you at the good vantage points and explain what you're looking at. Bring a sprig of mint — it's the traditional workaround for the smell.

🫖 Tagines and mint tea

Order tagines. Order them repeatedly. Chicken with preserved lemon, lamb with prunes, kefta with egg — they are as good as you've been told. And the mint tea is not garnish, it is the point.

🏛️ Rooftops and riads

Some of the loveliest spaces in Marrakech are behind doors you would walk past without noticing. Riad restaurants, rooftop terraces, hidden courtyards — a guide (or a local recommendation) is how you find them.

Common questions

Marrakech FAQ

How many days do you need in Marrakech?
Two to three nights is enough to see Marrakech properly if you're focused — the medina, the souks, Jemaa el-Fnaa, a few of the palaces and gardens, and enough tagines to feel you've made your point. We spent three nights there in February 2026 and felt we'd done it justice. Any less and you'll feel rushed.
Do you need a guide for the Marrakech medina?
Honestly — don't skip this. The Marrakech medina is beautifully disorienting and Google Maps genuinely gives up after a few turns. A local walking guide unlocks the whole thing — the souks make sense, the food stops are better, and you understand what you're looking at. We used Ali from Get Marrakech, booked through TourRadar, and it was the single best decision of our whole trip.
Is Marrakech safe for tourists?
Yes. We felt safe throughout our three nights in Marrakech — day and evening, in the medina and outside it. Standard travel sense applies: keep an eye on your bag in busy souks, don't wave large amounts of cash around, and if a stranger is very insistent on "guiding" you somewhere for free, they'll usually want payment at the end. But it's a welcoming city and violent crime against tourists is rare.
What is the food like in Marrakech?
Excellent — heavier on aromatic spice and slow-cooked richness than most people expect. Tagines are everywhere and worth ordering, harira soup is a classic starter, and mint tea is essentially the national drink (it comes out constantly and it's genuinely good). The food stalls at Jemaa el-Fnaa in the evening are an experience in themselves. Fresh orange juice from the square stalls is unreasonably cheap and delicious.
Is Marrakech easy for gluten-free travellers?
Better than you might expect. Tagines, grilled meats, rice dishes, harira in some forms, and lots of naturally gluten-free Moroccan cooking make it manageable. Do be careful with sauces and coatings and always ask before ordering — cross-contamination is possible especially in busier tourist spots. Nicola managed fine throughout.
When is the best time to visit Marrakech?
Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are ideal — warm days, cooler evenings, manageable crowds. February (when we visited) is also lovely — cool mornings, pleasant afternoons, few crowds. Avoid July and August unless you like being cooked; temperatures regularly hit the mid-40s Celsius and much of the medina becomes unwalkable in the middle of the day.
Can you fly direct to Marrakech from Ireland?
Yes — Ryanair operates direct Dublin to Marrakech flights, roughly three and a half hours. It's one of the easiest long-haul-feeling destinations you can reach from Ireland without a connecting flight.
