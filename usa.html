What to know before you go
  • ESTA is essential. Irish citizens don't need a visa for short tourism trips, but everyone must apply for an ESTA online in advance under the Visa Waiver Program. Do this at least 72 hours before you fly.
  • Tipping is a much bigger deal than at home. Budget for 18–20% at sit-down restaurants, $1–2 per drink at bars, 15–20% for taxi and ride-share drivers, and a few dollars per night for hotel housekeeping. It's not optional in the way it can be in Ireland.
  • You'll need a plug adapter. The USA uses Type A/B plugs and runs on 110V. Most phone, laptop and camera chargers are dual-voltage and just need an adapter. Hair dryers and straighteners often are not — check the label or buy a travel version.
  • Driving is on the right — opposite side to Ireland. For a road trip like our Deep South drive, that's the whole point. For cities like New York and Washington DC, public transport is often much better than driving and parking.
  • ATMs are universal, cards are king. Contactless is now widespread. You rarely need lots of cash — but keep a small amount for tips.
  • Best time to visit varies enormously. There is no single "best time" for the USA — pick your destination first, then the season. See our destination guides for region-specific advice.

Common questions
USA travel FAQ
Do Irish citizens need a visa for the USA?
Irish citizens don't need a visa for tourism or short business trips of up to 90 days, but do need to apply for an ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization) online in advance under the Visa Waiver Program. Check the official U.S. government site for the latest requirements before you travel.
How long does an ESTA last?
An approved ESTA is generally valid for up to two years from the date of approval, or until your passport expires — whichever comes first. It allows multiple trips to the USA within that period, each up to 90 days. If you get a new passport, you'll need to apply for a new ESTA. Always confirm the current rules before travelling.
What's the best time to visit the USA?
It depends enormously on the region. For the Deep South and Florida, spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) avoid the worst humidity and hurricane season. Hawaii is a year-round destination but drier April–October. Alaska cruise season runs roughly May to September. New York and Los Angeles are enjoyable much of the year but especially good in spring and autumn. Pick your destination first, then the season.
Do I need to tip in the USA?
Yes — tipping is a much bigger part of the culture in the USA than it is at home. At sit-down restaurants, 18–20% is standard for good service. Bartenders typically expect $1–2 per drink or 15–20% of the tab. Tip taxi and ride-share drivers around 15–20%, and it's normal to leave a few dollars per night for hotel housekeeping. Factor tipping into your budget, particularly for eating out.
Do I need a car in the USA?
For road trips like our New York-to-Miami Deep South journey, absolutely — the freedom and coverage a car gives you is the whole point. For city stays like New York, Washington DC or (parts of) Los Angeles, public transport, walking and ride-share often work better than driving and parking. Hawaii and Alaska cruise ports each have their own patterns. Plan by destination.
What plug adapter do I need for the USA from Ireland?
The USA uses Type A and Type B plugs, and runs on 110V / 60Hz — different from Ireland's Type G plug and 220V. You'll need a plug adapter, and check the label on each device (most modern phone, laptop and camera chargers are dual-voltage and work fine with just an adapter — but appliances like hair dryers or hair straighteners often are not, and may need a voltage converter or a travel version).
