Why this county is part of our story
Our Kildare

Kildare is where Nicola goes to work, with Kilcullen becoming the part of the county she knows through real life rather than a once-off visit. It is a small town built around the River Liffey, with its bridge, valley walks and familiar everyday rhythm.

Away from the daily routine, Kildare opens out into horse country, ancient religious heritage and country houses. During Heritage Week we visited Burtown House, a historic Quaker home near Athy, and found exactly the kind of place Two for the Row'd loves: a story-rich house, gardens and a corner of Ireland that rewards looking beyond the obvious.

This page combines the Kildare we genuinely know with the places we want to explore properly next. We will keep moving places into the first-hand sections as we visit them.

Kildare is close enough to feel familiar — and varied enough to keep surprising us.

The part Nicola knows best
Kilcullen and Old Kilcullen
Everyday Kildare

🌉 Kilcullen

A compact town gathered around the River Liffey and its landmark bridge. Kilcullen is Nicola's working base, so it feels less like a sightseeing stop and more like part of ordinary life in Kildare.

A quiet wander

🌳 Kilcullen Valley

The riverside is the town's natural breathing space. It is ideal for a short walk that lets you see Kilcullen away from the traffic and appreciate how closely the town is tied to the Liffey.

Ancient hilltop

🗼 Old Kilcullen

On the hill outside the modern town, Old Kilcullen preserves the remains of an early monastic settlement, including a round tower and high-cross fragments, with broad views over the surrounding countryside.

Make time to stop

☕ Independent Kilcullen

Kilcullen works best as more than a drive-through. Pair the river and heritage with one of the town's independent cafés or shops and it becomes an easy, unhurried half-day stop.

04
Our Heritage Week discovery
Burtown House & Gardens

Burtown House, near Athy, has been home to the same Quaker family for generations. We visited during Heritage Week and found a place where the house, gardens and family story all belong together — much more personal than a polished stately-home experience.

The gardens are a major reason to visit in their own right, with woodland, water, old orchards and productive areas supplying the on-site food offering. Opening days and access to the house can vary, so check Burtown's current arrangements before setting off.

The county's big stories
Horse country, St Brigid & Kildare Town
Open Kildare landscape

🐎 The Curragh

Thousands of acres of open grassland and the landscape most closely associated with Kildare's racing tradition. Even without attending a race meeting, the sheer openness makes the Curragh feel unlike almost anywhere else nearby.

Gardens and thoroughbreds

🌸 Irish National Stud

One of Kildare's best-known attractions, bringing together working thoroughbred breeding, the Japanese Gardens and St Fiachra's Garden in one visit.

A saint at the centre

🔥 Kildare Town

St Brigid's Cathedral, its round tower and the wider Brigidine story give Kildare Town one of the county's strongest heritage identities. It deserves time beyond a shopping stop.

Racing heritage

🏇 Newbridge & the Curragh

Newbridge makes a practical base for exploring horse country, with the Curragh Racecourse nearby and the Museum of Style Icons at Newbridge Silverware adding a completely different kind of stop.

💎 Why Kildare works as a day trip

You can combine a historic town, open countryside, gardens and a good food stop without covering huge distances. The trick is to choose one area and explore it properly instead of trying to race along the whole county.

What we'd tell a friend
Our practical Kildare tips
🚗 A car unlocks the county.
The main towns have rail and bus links, but smaller heritage sites and gardens are easiest to combine by car.
🐎 Check race days.
A meeting at the Curragh, Naas or Punchestown can be the highlight of a trip — and can also affect traffic and accommodation.
🌿 Confirm seasonal opening.
Country houses, gardens and smaller heritage attractions may not open every day of the year.
🔥 Give Kildare Town time.
St Brigid's story is spread across several sites, so it is worth planning rather than simply arriving.
🥾 Bring shoes for mixed ground.
The Curragh, woodland trails and riverside walks can all be muddy after Irish rain.
💎 Look beyond the big names.
Our own Kildare connection begins with everyday Kilcullen and a Heritage Week discovery — not only the headline attractions.

Common questions
County Kildare FAQ
What is County Kildare best known for?
Kildare is especially known for horse racing and breeding, the Curragh, the Irish National Stud, St Brigid's heritage and attractive towns including Kildare, Naas and Kilcullen.
Is Kilcullen worth visiting?
Yes. It is a compact riverside town with an attractive bridge, independent businesses and Old Kilcullen's early-monastic remains just outside town.
What can you see at Burtown House?
Burtown is a historic Quaker house with gardens, woodland and a strong food connection. Opening arrangements can be seasonal, so check directly before travelling.
How far is Kildare from Dublin?
Much of the county is roughly 30 to 60 minutes from Dublin by road or rail, depending on your starting point and destination.
Have you visited everywhere in this guide?
No. We clearly distinguish Nicola's everyday Kildare connection and our visit to Burtown House from attractions that are still on our list.
Do you have a Kildare guide I can use?
Yes — our printable Kildare guide appears further up this page and collects attractions, food, coffee, pubs, shopping, hidden gems and day-trip ideas.
