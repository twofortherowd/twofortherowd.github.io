First-hand reviews

The five Christmas markets we've actually done

All five are worth visiting. They are absolutely not all the same.

01 · Germany · visited 2015

Cologne

The proper, traditional Christmas-market experience.

Cologne isn't just somewhere Nicola visited for a weekend — she lived here for a year. So when we say the city works at Christmas, we know it beyond the postcard version. The Dom looming over the lights, mugs of glühwein, multiple markets dotted around the city and Kölners who are naturally warm and sociable: this is the one we'd recommend if you've never done a German Christmas market before.

Our verdict: If you want the Christmas market you've always pictured in your head, start here.

02 · Denmark · visited 2022

Copenhagen

The one where winter itself is part of the attraction.

Copenhagen understands cosy. Candlelit cafés, beautifully lit streets, Nyhavn in the winter darkness and Christmas at Tivoli make the whole city feel festive, not just one square. We spent four nights here and had plenty to fill them — this is a Christmas break for people who want a brilliant city as much as they want a market.

Our verdict: Beautiful, atmospheric and very easy to fall for. Your bank card may need counselling afterwards.

03 · Italy · visited 2023

Turin

The market wasn't the star — and we'd still go back tomorrow.

Turin is the useful reminder that a Christmas-market break doesn't have to live or die by the market itself. The festive atmosphere was lovely, but what really stole us were the grand baroque piazzas, the coffee bars, aperitivo and some genuinely brilliant food. We had outstanding gluten-free pizza here too, which is enough to put a city high on Nicola's list all by itself.

Our verdict: Pick Turin if you want a cracking Italian city break with Christmas wrapped around it.

04 · Poland · visited 2024

Bydgoszcz

The hidden gem we now won't shut up about.

Bydgoszcz wasn't on the standard Christmas-market conveyor belt — and that's exactly why we loved it. The market felt properly festive rather than overly commercial, the blue light arches and waterfront looked spectacular after dark, the food and glühwein were great value, and the locals we met made the whole trip something special.

Our verdict: Probably the biggest surprise of the five. Go before everybody else discovers it.

05 · Poland · visited 2025

Wrocław

The full Christmas-card version.

Wrocław gave us the big one: a gorgeous Old Town, a huge market on the Rynek, festive lights, brilliant Polish food and the city's hundreds of tiny bronze dwarfs sending you off exploring every side street. It was cold — properly cold — but that's almost part of the point when the whole square is glowing and you've something hot in your hands.

Our verdict: If you want maximum festive impact without western-European prices, Wrocław is very hard to beat.

A few things we'd do before booking

✈️ Price the whole weekend

A €29 flight is not a bargain if the only decent room left is €240 a night. Check flights and accommodation together before committing.

📅 Sunday–Tuesday can win

If you can travel outside the obvious Friday–Sunday slot, flights and rooms can be dramatically kinder.

🌙 Stay central if you can

Christmas markets are at their best after dark. Being able to walk back to your hotel instead of figuring out late-night transport is worth paying a little extra for.

🧤 Assume it'll feel colder

Standing around outside with a drink is very different from walking briskly through a city. Proper layers make the difference between magical and miserable.

🎄 Don't make the market the only plan

Our best festive trips were cities we'd happily visit without Christmas. That gives you museums, food, cafés and sightseeing if the weather is rotten.

🍷 Pace the glühwein

It tastes like Christmas in a mug and goes down suspiciously easily. This is information acquired through extensive field research.

Common questions

Christmas market FAQ

Which Christmas market is best for a first trip?

Cologne. It gives you the traditional German experience most people imagine when they think “European Christmas market”, and there are multiple markets across the city rather than one small festive square.

Which is the best value?

Bydgoszcz and Wrocław were the standouts for value for us. Poland gives you excellent food, drink and festive atmosphere without the prices you can run into in Copenhagen or some of Europe's bigger-name Christmas destinations.

Which one is the prettiest?

Wrocław probably wins for the full Christmas-card look, but Bydgoszcz's lighting — especially those blue arches — was genuinely spectacular and far more impressive than we expected.

How many nights should I book?

Two or three nights is ideal for most Christmas-market city breaks. We gave Copenhagen four nights and had plenty to do. For Cologne, Turin, Bydgoszcz and Wrocław, a good long weekend works beautifully.

Do Christmas markets run after Christmas Day?

Some do and some don't. Copenhagen's Tivoli Christmas season and Wrocław's market extend beyond Christmas in the published 2026 schedules, while Cologne's main Cathedral market finishes before Christmas. Always check the exact market you want before booking.

When should I book from Ireland?

For a late-November or December weekend, September and October are a sensible time to get organised. The biggest risk in waiting isn't necessarily the market itself — it's flight and hotel prices on the popular weekends.

Our pick if we were booking tomorrow?

For the classic: Cologne. For sheer Christmas magic: Wrocław. For value and something different: Bydgoszcz. For atmosphere: Copenhagen. And if the food matters just as much as the fairy lights, Turin.
