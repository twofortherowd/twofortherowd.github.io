County Clare —
Cliffs, Castles & a Little Irish Magic

Wild Atlantic waves, ancient landscapes, traditional music and stories around every corner.

There's something wonderfully dramatic about County Clare.

Maybe it's the huge cliffs rising out of the Atlantic, the eerie limestone of the Burren or the sound of trad music drifting out of a pub in Doolin. Whatever it is, Clare knows how to leave an impression.

It's home to some of Ireland's best-known natural sights, but also to plenty of lesser-known treasures: ancient castles, underground caves, colourful villages, spectacular coastal drives and enough history and folklore to keep anyone busy. Whether it's a weekend away or a Wild Atlantic Way road trip, Clare deserves a place on your list.

The big hitters
Must-see attractions
Cliffs · Iconic

🌊 The Cliffs of Moher

Few places capture the drama of Ireland's Atlantic coast like the Cliffs of Moher. They rise more than 200 metres above the ocean at their highest point, and on a wild day the weather adds plenty of extra theatre.

The main visitor area and O'Brien's Tower give you the classic views without needing to go anywhere near the edge.

Why visit? Coastal scenery, photography and one of Ireland's most famous landmarks.

⚠️ Coastal walk update: the southern section from Liscannor and Hag's Head is fully closed for safety reasons, and the Doolin section is only partly open. Stay on designated paths, never cross walls or barriers, and check the official site for the latest status before you go.

Landscape · Nature

🪨 The Burren

The Burren looks unlike anywhere else in Ireland: a vast limestone landscape of cracked pavements, strange rock formations and ancient monuments.

What looks like bare stone is actually bursting with life. Arctic, alpine and Mediterranean plants grow side by side here, and spring brings a remarkable show of wildflowers. Follow the marked trails, stop at the viewpoints and take your time.

Why visit? Geology, archaeology, nature and otherworldly landscapes.

🌿 Stick to marked trails. The limestone is uneven and the plants are protected.
Castle · Folk park

🏰 Bunratty Castle & Folk Park

Bunratty's mighty 15th-century tower house is one of the best-known castles in Ireland. Beside it, the folk park recreates village streets, farmhouses and cottages to show how people lived in rural Ireland in times gone by.

Why visit? Medieval castles, Irish social history and family days out.

Village · Music · Ferries

🎶 Doolin

Doolin is famous for traditional music and makes a great base for North Clare, close to both the Cliffs of Moher and the Burren.

Doolin Pier is also a departure point for ferries to the Aran Islands, especially Inis Oírr, the closest of the three.

Why visit? Traditional music, coastal scenery and island trips.

⛴️ Ferries are seasonal and depend on the weather.
Cave · Guided tour

🦇 Aillwee Cave

Hidden beneath the Burren's limestone is a remarkable underground world. Guided tours of Aillwee lead you through its passages and explain how water carved them over thousands of years.

Why visit? Caves, geology and family adventures.

ℹ️ Check current tour times and any combined tickets before you go.
Cave · Guided tour

💧 Doolin Cave

Doolin Cave is home to the Great Stalactite, reputed to be the longest free-hanging stalactite in Europe. A guided descent shows you one of the Burren's most unusual sights.

Why visit? Geology and a genuinely unusual experience.

♿ The descent involves steps, so check accessibility information before you book.
Seaside · Surfing

🏄 Lahinch

A lively seaside town with a long sandy beach and serious Atlantic surf. Book a lesson, take a bracing stroll along the prom or just watch the surfers from somewhere warm.

Why visit? Beaches, surfing and seaside breaks.

Scenic drive · Lighthouse

💡 Loop Head Peninsula

If you like dramatic coastal roads and quiet corners, Loop Head is a treat. Big cliffs, sweeping Atlantic views and the famous Loop Head Lighthouse at the tip, with the Shannon Estuary on one side and the open ocean on the other.

Why visit? Scenic driving, photography and coastal landscapes.

📅 The lighthouse opens for visits seasonally.
Seaside town

🐚 Kilkee

A classic seaside town wrapped around a horseshoe-shaped bay, with clifftop walks that look out over the Atlantic.

Why visit? Seaside holidays, coastal walks and photography.

⚠️ The cliff walks are exposed, so stay well back from the edge, especially in wind.
Town · Music · Seasonal friary

🎻 Ennis

Clare's county town has narrow streets, colourful shopfronts and a strong traditional music scene, and it's lovely to explore on foot.

The medieval Ennis Friary, founded in the 13th century, is famous for its carved sculptures.

Why visit? Town breaks, history, music and independent shops.

📅 Ennis Friary opens seasonally, roughly March to November.

Town · Lough Derg

👑 Killaloe

Killaloe sits beside the River Shannon at the southern tip of Lough Derg, linked by an old stone bridge to Ballina on the Tipperary side.

The town is closely tied to Brian Boru, Ireland's most famous high king, whose royal seat at Kincora is traditionally placed here.

Why visit? Riverside scenery, history and relaxing days out.

Worth the detour
Hidden gems & unusual places

A mix of free outdoor places and seasonal attractions, labelled so you can plan around them.

Prehistory · Free

🪦 Poulnabrone Dolmen

One of Ireland's best-known portal tombs, standing alone on the Burren limestone. It's more than 5,000 years old.

🙏 Please don't climb on or touch the monument.
Beach · Free

🏖️ Fanore Beach

A beautiful Atlantic beach with dunes, framed by the grey hills of the Burren.

Island · Seasonal boat

⛪ Scattery Island

An island in the Shannon Estuary with a round tower and the remains of an early Christian monastery linked to St Senan.

⛴️ Boats run from Kilrush in the summer season, and the island opens to visitors seasonally. Check before you go.
Coast · History

⚓ Spanish Point

A seaside spot whose name recalls the ships of the Spanish Armada wrecked along the Clare coast in 1588. See the legends section for the story.

Village · Maritime

🏰 Carrigaholt

A picturesque village on the Shannon Estuary, with a castle tower overlooking the harbour and a long maritime heritage.

Woodland · Nature reserve

🌳 Dromore Woods

Woodland walks, lakes and riverside paths in a nature reserve near Ennis, rich in wildlife.

Ruins · Free

🏚️ Quin Abbey

Atmospheric ruins of a 15th-century Franciscan friary in the village of Quin, with a beautiful cloister.

Coast · Birdwatching

🌉 The Bridges of Ross

A natural sea arch on the Loop Head Peninsula, and one of Ireland's top spots for seabird watching, especially in autumn.

⚠️ The rocks are exposed to Atlantic swells, so stay well back from the edges.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

We've kept history and legend clearly labelled, because the best Irish stories are usually a bit of both.

Legend

🧙‍♀️ The Hag's Head

At the southern end of the Cliffs of Moher, a rock formation is said to look like the head of a woman gazing out to sea. Legend tells of Mal, a hag who fell for the hero Cú Chulainn and chased him across Ireland. In one version, she leapt after him across the sea and fell to her death at this spot, leaving her face in the rock forever.

It's a legend, of course, but it's hard not to see her once you know the story.

History

🪦 The ancient tombs of the Burren

The Burren is packed with prehistoric monuments, from wedge tombs to ring forts. Poulnabrone, the most famous, is a portal tomb more than 5,000 years old. When it was excavated, archaeologists found the remains of many people buried over hundreds of years.

These tombs are reminders that people have been farming and living on this stony landscape for thousands of years.

History

👑 Brian Boru and Killaloe

Brian Boru was born in the area in the 10th century and rose to become High King of Ireland. His royal seat at Kincora is traditionally placed at Killaloe, beside the Shannon.

He died at the Battle of Clontarf in 1014, a battle that became one of the most famous in Irish history.

History & legend

⚓ The Spanish Armada

In 1588, after the failed invasion of England, storms drove ships of the Spanish Armada onto the west coast of Ireland, and several were wrecked off Clare. Spanish Point takes its name from those wrecks.

Many dark stories grew up around what happened to the survivors. The details vary from one telling to the next, so we'd treat the more dramatic versions as legend rather than proven history.

Ready-made plans
Suggested days out

Realistic days out, each focused on one part of the county.

🌊 Day 1: Classic North Clare

Ideal for: first-time visitors
  1. Morning: the Cliffs of Moher, early if you can, to beat the crowds.
  2. Lunch: in Doolin.
  3. Afternoon: on to Lahinch for the beach.
  4. Tip: leave time for viewpoints and a warm drink.

🪨 Day 2: The Burren

Ideal for: landscape and archaeology
  1. Morning: Poulnabrone Dolmen.
  2. Midday: Burren scenic stops and a short marked walk.
  3. Afternoon: Aillwee Cave, then Ballyvaughan.

💡 Day 3: West Clare coast

Ideal for: scenic drivers
  1. Morning: Kilkee and its clifftop views.
  2. Midday: the Bridges of Ross.
  3. Afternoon: Loop Head Lighthouse at the tip of the peninsula.

🏰 Alternative: family heritage day

Ideal for: families and a gentler pace
  1. Main event: Bunratty Castle and Folk Park. Give it most of the day.
  2. Then: a relaxed stop nearby, rather than racing across the county.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚗 Getting around
A car gives you the most freedom for the Burren, Loop Head and rural Clare, though some of the main sights have public transport connections.
🗓️ How long to stay
Two to four days for a good selection of highlights.
☀️ Best time to visit
Late spring to early autumn for long days and ferries. The Burren's wildflowers are at their best in late spring.
🌧️ Check before you go
Weather, cliff access, cave tour times and boat schedules can all change.
🥾 Cliff safety
Stay on designated paths and behind barriers, especially at the Cliffs of Moher, Kilkee and the Bridges of Ross.
☔ Rainy-day options
Aillwee and Doolin caves, Bunratty and the town of Ennis.

Opening times, seasons and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. For general visitor information, see Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Clare FAQ
Can you walk the Cliffs of Moher coastal walk?
Only parts of it at the moment. The southern section from Liscannor and Hag's Head is fully closed for safety reasons, and the Doolin section is only partly open. Check the official Cliffs of Moher website for the latest status, and always stay on designated paths.
Are the Cliffs of Moher in Clare or Galway?
County Clare. They're a popular day trip from Galway city, but they're firmly in Clare.
How many days do you need in Clare?
Two to four days lets you see the Cliffs, the Burren, a cave and either Loop Head or Bunratty without rushing.
Can you get to the Aran Islands from Clare?
Yes. Ferries run from Doolin Pier, especially to Inis Oírr, but they're seasonal and weather-dependent. Check sailings before you travel.
Have Two for the Row'd visited everywhere on this page?
Not yet, and we'd rather be upfront about it. This Clare guide is researched rather than first-hand. When we visit and film, our own videos and tips will be added here.
