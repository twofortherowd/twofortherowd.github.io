🏛️ Butrint 🇺🇳 UNESCO World Heritage Ancient city near Sarandë

Butrint —
an ancient city between lake and sea

Our guide to Albania's greatest archaeological site — how to visit from Sarandë.

Butrint sits on a peninsula between Lake Butrint and the Vivari Channel, about 20 kilometres south of Sarandë. It's Albania's first UNESCO World Heritage Site — a layered ancient city where Greek, Roman, Byzantine and Venetian remains sit inside a wild wetland landscape. We visited on our 2018 guided day trip from Corfu and it stopped us in our tracks. This is everything we'd tell a friend heading down for the day.

❤️ Our Visit to Butrint

Ruins in a landscape you don't expect

"We came to Butrint expecting a set of ancient ruins. What we didn't expect was the setting — the way the stone works its way through wetland, forest and water, so that walking through it feels less like visiting a monument and more like walking through a place that never quite stopped being lived in."

Butrint was one of the anchors of our 2018 day trip out of Sarandë. We joined a guided tour that morning after crossing over from Corfu, and Butrint was our first proper stop of the day. The drive down along the coast is beautiful in itself, past Ksamil and along the water, and then the ruins appear on a peninsula wrapped in green.

What surprised us most was how alive the site felt — not in a manicured, roped-off way, but in the sense of a place that people had been walking through for thousands of years and were still walking through. The Roman theatre, the traces of the baptistery, the layers of walls where you can see where the Byzantines picked up what the Romans left behind, and then the Venetians did the same later — it all feels remarkably close to the surface.

We climbed up to the small acropolis at the top of the hill where there's a museum and one of the best views of the whole peninsula. The panorama over Lake Butrint on one side and the channel on the other is genuinely one of the most beautiful archaeology-plus-landscape combinations we've seen anywhere.

Practical note: we were on a guided tour, so a lot of the timing and logistics got taken care of. If you're going independently, factor in more time — Butrint is a walk-around site rather than a quick photo stop.

🇺🇳 Why Butrint Is Important

Albania's first UNESCO site — and it earns it

Butrint was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1992 — the first archaeological site from Albania to be recognised. The listing was later extended to include the wider landscape, and today the surrounding Butrint National Park acts as a buffer zone protecting both the ruins and the natural environment around them. The Butrint wetlands are also recognised as a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance.

What makes Butrint stand out isn't any single monument. It's the layering. Most great Mediterranean archaeological sites belong to a single civilisation — this one is Greek, or that one is Roman. Butrint is nearly all of them, in the same place, on top of each other. It grew as a Greek colony, expanded as a Roman city, kept going as a Byzantine bishopric, was later occupied briefly by the Venetians, and eventually declined and was gradually reclaimed by the marshes around it. That gradual, non-violent decline is part of why the archaeology has survived so well.

And uniquely for a Mediterranean site of this scale, Butrint has never been swallowed by modern development. The setting — water, wetland and forest — is a huge part of what makes it feel the way it does.

🏛️ What You'll See at Butrint
🎭
The Roman Theatre

The centrepiece of the site — a beautifully preserved Roman theatre carved into the hillside, often with a pool of water at its base. One of the most photographed spots at Butrint.

✝️
Baptistery & Basilica

Traces of Butrint's early Christian era, including a baptistery known for its intricate mosaic floor (often covered for protection) and the ruins of a large basilica.

🦁
The Lion Gate

A stone gateway named for a striking carved relief above it — a lion attacking a bull. Part of the ancient city's fortifications.

🏰
Venetian Acropolis & Museum

At the top of the hill, a Venetian-era fortress houses a small archaeological museum — well worth the climb for both the artefacts and the panoramic views.

🧱
City walls & layers

Walking the paths, you'll see how successive civilisations reused and built on top of each other — Greek foundations, Roman upper courses, Byzantine and later additions.

🌿
The wetland setting

Butrint isn't just a ruins site — it's a landscape. Between the ancient stones you're surrounded by forest, water and birdlife, which is part of what earned the wider Ramsar wetland status.

📜 A Short History of Butrint

Butrint's story stretches over two and a half thousand years. Very briefly:

  • Ancient Greek period: Butrint began as a Greek colony, growing into a small but strategically important settlement on the trade routes between the Adriatic and Ionian.
  • Roman city: Under Roman rule the town expanded significantly — the Roman theatre and much of the surviving urban fabric dates from this era.
  • Byzantine bishopric: After the fall of Rome, Butrint continued as a Byzantine centre of some importance, and this is the era of the basilica and baptistery.
  • Venetian occupation: The Venetians took control in the medieval period, adding fortifications including the acropolis fortress at the top of the site.
  • Decline & rediscovery: Butrint was gradually abandoned as the surrounding land turned to marsh. That slow retreat is part of why so much has survived — the site was never demolished for later cities. Modern excavation and conservation has continued into the present day.
🚗 How to Visit Butrint from Sarandë

Four ways to do it, plus a note on timing

Bus: There's a direct local bus from Sarandë to Butrint, running roughly every hour, that stops in Ksamil along the way. Cheap and easy — the go-to option for independent travellers.

Guided tour: This is what we did — a guided tour from Sarandë (or from Corfu) that typically pairs Butrint with Ksamil and/or the Blue Eye. Least stressful, especially if you only have one day.

Taxi: Fine for a small group. Agree the price and any return/wait arrangement before you get in the car.

Hire car: Gives you the most freedom — Butrint is easy to combine with Ksamil beaches and stops along the coast.

💡 When to arrive

Butrint gets very busy in summer, especially with tour groups arriving from Corfu on the mid-morning ferries. If you're on the ground independently, going early in the morning or later in the afternoon makes for a much calmer visit and better light for photos. Bring water — there's not a lot of shade in the height of summer.

❓ Butrint FAQ
Where is Butrint?
Butrint is in southern Albania, roughly 17–20km south of the coastal city of Sarandë, on a peninsula between Lake Butrint and the Vivari Channel. It sits within Butrint National Park, close to the Albanian–Greek border.
Is Butrint a UNESCO World Heritage Site?
Yes — Butrint was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1992 as the first archaeological site from Albania to be recognised. The wider Butrint National Park, which acts as a buffer zone, was established later. The site is also recognised as a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance.
What civilisations built Butrint?
Butrint's ruins span thousands of years and multiple civilisations — including Greek, Roman, early Christian, Byzantine and Venetian periods, with later Ottoman-era additions nearby. That layered history is exactly what makes the site special and is central to its UNESCO listing.
How do you get to Butrint from Sarandë?
Butrint is easy to reach from Sarandë — options include a direct local bus (relatively cheap, runs roughly every hour and passes through Ksamil on the way), a taxi (agree the price and any return arrangement upfront), a hire car, or an organised guided tour that typically includes hotel pickup and often combines Butrint with Ksamil and the Blue Eye.
How long do you need at Butrint?
Allow at least 1.5 to 3 hours to walk the site properly — longer if you love archaeology and want to visit the acropolis museum at the top. Going early or later in the afternoon avoids the busiest window when tour groups from Corfu are on site.
Is Butrint worth visiting?
Absolutely — it's the most important archaeological site in Albania and one of the most atmospheric in the Balkans. What makes Butrint special isn't just the ruins themselves but the setting: forest, wetlands and water surrounding stone that has been layered by civilisation after civilisation for millennia. Very much unmissable if you're in southern Albania.
