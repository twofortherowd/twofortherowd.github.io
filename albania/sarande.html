❤️ Our Sarandë Experience

A ferry, a guided tour and a day that changed our minds

"We were staying in Corfu and decided to cross over to Albania for the day. It turned into one of those travel days that completely changed our perception of a destination."

We were on holiday in Corfu in 2018 when we made the short crossing to Sarandë on the ferry. Standing at Corfu port beforehand, most of what we knew about Albania fitted on a postcard. A short crossing of the Ionian later, that started falling apart in the best way.

Sarandë itself is an easygoing coastal city — a long waterfront promenade curving around a beautiful bay, clear Ionian water, and views back across to Corfu on the horizon. It felt smaller and quieter than we'd expected, with the shape of a proper working town rather than a purpose-built resort. That was 2018, mind you — every account we've seen since suggests Sarandë has grown a lot as a tourist destination.

Once ashore we joined a guided day tour, which was easily the smartest call we made. It took us out to Butrint — the UNESCO archaeological site south of Sarandë — and then to the Blue Eye, a stunningly vivid natural spring in the woods. Both are proper highlight-of-your-year sights and doing them in one guided loop meant we didn't have to worry about buses, timings or the ferry back.

Throughout the day, one thing that stayed with us was the warmth of everyone we met. Guides, café staff, shopkeepers — genuinely friendly, proud of their country and happy to see visitors. Albania's tourism profile has changed a lot since our visit, but that human warmth is one of the things people still consistently mention, and it matches exactly what we experienced.

⭐ Best Things to Do in & Around Sarandë
🌊
1. Walk the Sarandë waterfront

The promenade curves around the bay and is the heart of the town — lovely to walk at any time of day, with clear Ionian views, cafés along the way and Corfu visible on the horizon.

🏛️
2. Day trip to Butrint (UNESCO)

Around 20km south of Sarandë — the UNESCO-listed ancient city of Butrint layers Greek, Roman, Byzantine and Venetian ruins on a peninsula between lake and sea. Unmissable. See our Butrint guide →

💧
3. Visit the Blue Eye (Syri i Kaltër)

Roughly 22km east — a natural spring whose colour genuinely has to be seen to be believed. A short forest walk from the car park. See our Blue Eye guide →

🏖️
4. Ksamil beaches

Between Sarandë and Butrint — turquoise water, small islands you can swim to, and famously beautiful beaches. We didn't get to Ksamil ourselves on our 2018 day trip, but it's one of the best-known coastal spots in southern Albania and easy to fold in.

🍽️
5. Waterfront food & coffee

Sarandë is well set up for eating and drinking outside — the waterfront and streets just back from it have lots of options, from casual seafood spots to café terraces good for a long slow coffee.

🚗
6. Base for the Albanian Riviera

Sarandë is the gateway to the wider Albanian Riviera stretching north — dramatic coastline, beach villages and mountain roads. Worth staying a few nights for if this is your first taste of Albania.

⛴️ How to Get from Corfu to Sarandë

The ferry is the way — here's what to know

Who runs the ferry: Three main operators work the Corfu–Sarandë route — Finikas Lines, Ionian Seaways and Albania Luxury Ferries. Between them there are frequent sailings in summer, tapering to fewer in winter.

How long: Fast hydrofoil/high-speed services can take around 30 minutes. Conventional ferries take longer, so check the journey time for the exact sailing you book.

Travel documents: This is a proper international border crossing — Greece is in the EU and Schengen Area, while Albania is not. Document checks apply, so bring the travel document required for your nationality and allow extra time at both ports.

Time zone trick: Corfu (Greece) runs one hour ahead of Sarandë (Albania). So a 09:00 departure from Corfu arrives roughly 10:30 local Corfu time — which is 09:30 in Sarandë. Check whether your ticket time is Greek or Albanian time and don't get caught out at the terminal.

Booking: Book ahead in peak season and follow the check-in time shown by your operator. Timetables and check-in requirements can change, so use the latest information for your sailing.

Where you arrive: Sarandë's ferry terminal is at the western end of the waterfront promenade — an easy walk into the centre of town.

💡 Nicola & Podge's Ferry Tip

Our day trip started at Corfu port with a guided tour that included the ferry, transport in Albania, Butrint, the Blue Eye and lunch. If you've only got one day, booking a guided tour from Corfu is by far the least stressful way to do it — you're just following someone else's plan and can enjoy the scenery. If you've got two or three nights in Sarandë, doing it yourself opens up more freedom.

🗺️ One Day in Sarandë — What We Did

This is roughly the shape of our 2018 day trip from Corfu. If you're doing it independently rather than on a guided tour, the timings will shift, but the sequence still works.

Morning — Ferry across from Corfu

  1. Arrive at Corfu port: Early sailing — allow at least an hour for boarding and passport formalities.
  2. 30-minute crossing: The Ionian views alone make this a lovely start to the day.
  3. Sarandë port: Passport control, then straight into your tour pickup or into the town.

Late Morning — Butrint (UNESCO)

  1. Drive south: Roughly 20km through the coastline to Butrint National Park.
  2. Explore the ruins: The Roman theatre, the baptistery mosaic (usually covered but the site itself is remarkable), the Venetian acropolis with panoramic views.
  3. Allow 2–3 hours: More if you love archaeology; a rushed hour is possible but does the site a disservice.

Lunch

  1. Where: Either back in Sarandë, or one of the villages en route between Butrint and the Blue Eye — plenty of local options wherever your tour stops.

Afternoon — The Blue Eye

  1. Drive east: Roughly 22km inland from Sarandë, into the forest around the spring.
  2. Short walk in: A forest walk from the car park to the spring itself — comfortable shoes matter.
  3. Take it in: The colour is genuinely startling in person — spend some time here.

Late Afternoon — Back to Sarandë & the ferry home

  1. Sarandë waterfront: A quick coffee or drink on the promenade before heading back.
  2. Passport control & ferry: Same routine in reverse — get to the terminal in good time.
  3. Sunset crossing: If you time it right, the run back to Corfu at golden hour is beautiful.

❓ Sarandë FAQ
Where is Sarandë?
Sarandë (also spelled Saranda) is a coastal city in southern Albania, on the Ionian Sea. It sits directly across the water from the Greek island of Corfu, which makes it easily reachable by ferry — the crossing takes around 30 minutes.
How do you get from Corfu to Sarandë?
Finikas Lines, Ionian Seaways and Albania Luxury Ferries serve the Corfu–Sarandë route. Fast services can take around 30 minutes, while conventional ferries take longer, and schedules vary by season. This is an international border crossing: Greece is in the EU and Schengen Area, while Albania is not, so document checks apply. Corfu is one hour ahead of Sarandë, so pay close attention to the local time shown on your ticket. Check the latest operator timetable before travelling.
Is Sarandë worth visiting?
Yes — Sarandë is a lively, easygoing Ionian coastal city with a lovely waterfront, clear sea and a very useful location. It's the main base for visiting Butrint (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and the Blue Eye natural spring, and it works both as a day trip from Corfu and as a base for several nights exploring southern Albania.
How long do you need in Sarandë?
A day trip from Corfu is enough for a quick taste plus one of the big day-trip attractions like Butrint or the Blue Eye — but two or three nights lets you see everything without rushing: the town itself, Butrint, the Blue Eye, and often Ksamil and nearby beaches too.
What is there to do near Sarandë?
The big three near Sarandë are Butrint (UNESCO archaeological site, around 20km south), the Blue Eye spring (roughly 22km east) and the beaches of Ksamil (between Sarandë and Butrint). The wider Albanian Riviera stretches north from Sarandë along a stunning stretch of Ionian coastline.
What currency and language does Sarandë use?
The local currency is the Albanian lek (ALL). Euros are often accepted in tourist businesses in Sarandë but you shouldn't assume they'll be accepted everywhere — carry some lek. The official language is Albanian, but English is widely spoken in tourism-facing businesses.
