The Blue Eye —
a colour you have to see to believe

Our guide to Syri i Kaltër — a natural spring in the forest near Sarandë.

If someone showed you a photo of the Blue Eye you'd probably assume it had been edited. It hasn't — the water really is that colour. We visited on our 2018 guided day trip out of Sarandë and it was one of the most memorable stops of the whole day. This is our guide to what it is, how to get there and what to know before you go.

❤️ Our Visit to the Blue Eye

The one that had us wondering if our eyes were working properly

"We walked through the forest thinking we knew what to expect — we'd seen the photos. Nothing prepared us for actually seeing the water in real life. That blue does not look natural. And yet there it is, bubbling up out of the ground in the middle of a forest."

The Blue Eye was the afternoon stop on our 2018 guided day trip from Sarandë. Our tour had taken us to Butrint in the morning; after lunch, we drove inland toward the mountains, turning off the main Sarandë–Gjirokastër road onto a smaller access route that led to the spring.

The walk in from the car park is short and easy through a shaded forest. Then, quite suddenly, the pool appears — small, deep, and an almost unreal colour. A vivid turquoise ring around the edges, dropping into a startling deep blue at the centre where the spring rises. It genuinely stops you in your tracks.

We spent a good while just standing on the platform above the water, watching. Practical advice from our visit: the crowd factor is real. Even in 2018, we had company. Everything we've read since suggests the site has become much busier as Albania's tourism has grown — going early in the morning or later in the afternoon is much calmer.

We didn't swim on our visit. The rules on that seem to have changed at various points, so if swimming matters to you, check current signage and instructions on site — see the note further down.

💧 What Is the Blue Eye?

A very deep natural spring — with a colour to match

The Blue Eye — Syri i Kaltër in Albanian, which literally translates as "the blue eye" — is a karst spring in southern Albania. Water rises up from deep underground, filling a roughly circular pool with a remarkable colour gradient: a deep, almost saturated blue at the centre where the spring emerges, ringed by turquoise around the edges. That combination is where the "eye" in the name comes from.

The site sits within a small protected natural area of forest and small watercourses, and the spring itself feeds a stream that eventually joins the Bistricë river system, flowing down toward the Ionian coast. From the viewing platform, you can watch the water bubbling gently up from the depths.

🔬 Why Is the Water So Blue?

Two things combine to give the Blue Eye its extraordinary colour:

  • The depth of the spring. The Blue Eye is famously deep — divers have explored it to significant depths, but its full depth is still uncertain. The dark centre contrasts dramatically with the shallower water around the edges.
  • Exceptionally clear water. The spring water is remarkably transparent, which lets you see the colour contrast between the deep centre and the shallower edges particularly clearly.
  • The turquoise ring. Around the edges the water is shallower and the pale bed is more visible, creating the vivid turquoise ring around the darker centre.

Put simply: it's not a filter. It's physics.

🚗 How to Visit the Blue Eye from Sarandë

Four ways to do it

Guided tour: Almost certainly the easiest option, especially if you're already on a day trip out of Sarandë or Corfu. Blue Eye + Butrint (often + Ksamil) is a very common combined itinerary — this is what we did.

Hire car: Gives you complete flexibility on timing and lets you go early or late to avoid the busiest window.

Taxi: Fine for a small group. Agree the price and any return/wait arrangement upfront before you get in the car.

Public bus: Buses running between Sarandë and Gjirokastër pass the turn-off for the Blue Eye. You'll need to cover the last stretch from the main road to the spring — walking, hitching, or a shuttle depending on what's running when you visit. Doable but the least predictable option.

💡 Nicola & Podge's Timing Tip

Try to arrive early in the morning or in the late afternoon to avoid the peak busy window. The Blue Eye is small — even a modest crowd can turn it into a queue at the viewing platform. Softer light at either end of the day also brings the colour of the water out beautifully in photos.

🚶 What the Visit Is Like

The basic shape of a visit is straightforward:

  • Arrive at the site entrance: Park at the car park and pay the small entry/access fee on the way in.
  • Short walk through the forest: A shaded path leads from the entrance area to the spring itself. Comfortable shoes are all you need; it's not a serious hike.
  • The viewing platform: A small wooden platform gives you a view down over the spring. This is the classic Blue Eye photo spot.
  • Take it in: Give yourself time. The colour is more remarkable the longer you look at it, and the light shifts through the day.
  • Café / rest area: There are typically some small refreshment options in the wider area around the spring — good for a coffee or a snack before heading back to the car.

⚠️ Please verify on the day

Access arrangements, entry fees, opening hours and swimming rules at the Blue Eye have changed at various points over the years to protect the site. Always check current signage on arrival and any recent visitor information before travelling — don't assume older blog posts (including ours) reflect the current situation exactly.

❓ Blue Eye FAQ
What is the Blue Eye in Albania?
The Blue Eye — Syri i Kaltër in Albanian — is a natural spring in southern Albania, deep in a forested area between Sarandë and Gjirokastër. Water rises up from the earth at an extraordinary depth, creating a circular pool with a startling colour gradient from deep blue at the centre to turquoise around the edges. It's one of the most striking natural sights in the country.
Where is the Blue Eye located?
The Blue Eye is in southern Albania, roughly 22 km east of Sarandë off the road between Sarandë and Gjirokastër. From the main road, a short access road leads to a car park, from which it's a short walk through the forest to the spring itself.
How do you get to the Blue Eye from Sarandë?
Most visitors reach the Blue Eye from Sarandë either by guided tour (often combined with Butrint and Ksamil), by hire car, or by taxi. There's also a public bus running between Sarandë and Gjirokastër that passes the turn-off, from where you'll need to cover the last stretch to the spring. Confirm the current pickup and access details before you travel.
How deep is the Blue Eye?
The Blue Eye is famously deep — divers have explored it to significant depths, but its full depth is still considered uncertain. Its striking appearance comes from exceptionally clear water, the depth of the spring and the way light interacts with the water and pale surroundings, creating the deep-blue centre and turquoise edges.
Can you swim in the Blue Eye?
Swimming rules at the Blue Eye have changed over the years — at times swimming has been permitted, at other times it has been restricted to protect the spring. Check the current rules and any signage on site before entering the water. If you do swim, be aware that the water is extremely cold year-round because it comes straight from underground.
Is the Blue Eye worth visiting?
Yes — it is one of the most striking natural sights in Albania and the colour is genuinely unlike anything you'll have seen in photos. It's most commonly done as part of a day trip that also includes Butrint, and pairs beautifully with a Sarandë base.
