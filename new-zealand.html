Logistics
Getting around New Zealand

How to explore New Zealand

New Zealand is large and getting around independently requires careful planning. These are your best options:

🚌 Guided tours

The best option for first-timers. Companies like Haka Tours operate 10–21 day circuits covering both islands with accommodation and transport included.

🚗 Car rental

Driving yourself gives maximum flexibility. Left-hand traffic. Good roads, but plan drives carefully — distances are vast. An InterIslander ferry connects North and South islands.

✈️ Domestic flights

Useful for covering long distances quickly (e.g. Auckland to Queenstown). Air New Zealand and Jetstar are the main carriers.

🚆 Coach/bus

Budget-friendly but slower. Intercity and NakedBus connect major cities and towns.

🌍
Planning
Best time to visit New Zealand
🌞 December–February (summer)

Best for: Long daylight, warm weather, beach time, hiking. Busiest season — book accommodation well ahead.

🍂 March–May (autumn)

Best for: Mild weather, fewer crowds, stunning foliage. Ideal sweet spot for visiting.

❄️ June–August (winter)

Best for: Skiing, Southern Lights viewing (from June), fewer tourists. Can be cold and rainy.

🌸 September–November (spring)

Best for: Warming weather, spring wildflowers, active for adventure sports. Building crowds.

Our tip: We visited in shoulder season (May) and had excellent weather and manageable crowds. New Zealand is good to visit year-round — it just depends what you want to do and whether you're comfortable with weather variability.

📝
Travel essentials
Visas & entry requirements
🇮🇪 Irish citizens

Visa-free entry: Up to 6 months. You'll get a NZeTA (electronic travel authority) automatically on arrival or apply online beforehand.

🌐 Other EU/UK citizens

Visa-free entry: 3–6 months depending on citizenship. Many nationalities need an NZeTA. Check immigration.govt.nz for your specific country.

💳 What you'll need

Passport (valid 3+ months past travel), proof of onward travel, proof of funds, travel insurance (highly recommended). Some nationalities may need additional documents.

💰 Currency & costs

New Zealand Dollar (NZD $). Widely accepted cards everywhere. Budget NZD $80–150/day for budget travel, NZD $150–250/day for mid-range comfort.

Travel info: Visit immigration.govt.nz for the most up-to-date visa requirements. Requirements can change.

Prepare for the trip
New Zealand packing essentials

New Zealand can mean beaches, boats, glacier walks and freezing cold mountain summits all in one trip. These are the things we'd pack without hesitation.

🧥
Waterproof jacket

NZ weather changes fast — especially on the South Island. A good waterproof layer is essential.

🥾
Hiking boots

Essential for walking trails, Cathedral Cove, glacier visits and adventure days.

🔥
Warm layers

For Lake Tekapo stargazing especially — it's genuinely very cold on that summit.

🦟
Insect repellent

NZ sandflies are famously persistent — especially near water and in forested areas.

☀️
High SPF sunscreen

NZ has some of the strongest UV radiation in the world — you can burn badly even when it feels cool.

🔌
Travel adaptor

NZ uses Type I plugs — same as Australia but different to Ireland.

Common questions
New Zealand FAQ
Is New Zealand worth visiting?
Absolutely — New Zealand is firmly in our top five countries in the world and we'd go back tomorrow. The scenery genuinely does not look real in places, the people are some of the nicest we've encountered anywhere, and the variety of experiences — from Māori culture to glaciers to glowworm caves to the best adventure sports on earth — is extraordinary.
Is a guided tour the best way to see New Zealand?
For first-time visitors, yes — New Zealand is large and the highlights are spread across both islands. A guided tour like the Haka Tours Amplified means you cover far more ground without the stress of organising transport, ferries and accommodation yourself. It is the most efficient and enjoyable way to experience both islands properly in a limited time.
How long do you need in New Zealand?
To do both islands justice allow at least two to three weeks. We did 19 days on the Haka Tours Amplified and felt we got a brilliant but still fast-moving overview of both islands. If you can give it longer, do. Rushing New Zealand would be a real mistake.
What is a Māori hāngī?
A hāngī is a traditional Māori method of cooking — food is slow-cooked in an underground pit using heated rocks. The result is smoky, tender and completely unlike anything you've had before. Experiencing a hāngī meal as part of a Māori cultural evening is one of the most memorable experiences available in New Zealand.
Do I need a visa to visit New Zealand?
Irish and most EU/UK citizens get visa-free entry for 3–6 months depending on nationality. You'll typically receive a visitor visa on arrival or need to apply for an NZeTA online beforehand. Check immigration.govt.nz for your specific country's requirements.
What's the currency and how much will it cost?
New Zealand uses the New Zealand Dollar (NZD $). Budget around NZD $80–150 per day for budget travel, or NZD $150–250 per day for mid-range comfort. Most places accept cards, though it's useful to carry some cash for smaller establishments and tips (tipping is appreciated but not obligatory).

Ready to plan your New Zealand trip?

New Zealand is one of those trips you plan for years and it exceeds every single expectation. Don't rush it — give both islands the time they deserve. You won't regret a single second of it.
