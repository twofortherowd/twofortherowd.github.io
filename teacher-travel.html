Travelling on a Teacher's Calendar when you can only go when it costs the most

Nicola is a teacher. That means no cheap week in May and no quiet trip in early October. We travel when the schools are off, exactly when every airline and hotel puts its prices up. We've still made it to more than 60 countries and territories, and this is how we make the school calendar work for us.

The problem every teacher knows

The moment the school holidays start, so does the price hike. The same flight can cost far more on the first Saturday of mid-term than it did the week before. If you're a teacher, an SNA or a parent of school-age kids, you can't just "travel off-peak" like the budget travel tips tell you to.

So we stopped fighting the calendar and started planning around it. The trick isn't one big secret. It's using every kind of time off in the right way.

Use the weekends. Plan the holidays. Save the big trips for summer.

🍎 How we make it work
1

Make the weekends count

Weekends during term are travel time that most people overlook. Our Liverpool extreme day trip was a single Saturday: first flight out of Dublin, last flight home, about €170 each, and back in time for Monday. Our Christmas market trips were weekend breaks too: one night in Turin, Saturday to Sunday, and Friday to Sunday in Bydgoszcz and Wrocław, at around €250–€270 each including flights and hotel.

2

Pick the city everyone else isn't going to

When the whole country is off at the same time, the obvious destinations fill up and get pricey. Bydgoszcz instead of the famous Christmas markets, or Turin instead of Rome, gives you a brilliant break without the crowds.

3

Book holiday weeks early, and watch the sales

Holiday-week prices only go one way as the dates get closer. Once you have next year's school calendar, the big sales like Black Friday are a good time to lock in a mid-term or Easter trip.

Our Black Friday travel deals →
4

Save the big trips for summer

Summer is the one time you have weeks rather than days, so that's when we go far and go long. We spent ten days in Prague one August. A group tour can make a big summer trip easier to budget, because so much is included in one price.

Our 10 days in Prague →
5

Don't forget home

Some of our favourite days off have been right here in Ireland, like our Heritage Week road trip. No flights, no peak-season fares, and a whole lot of country most of us have never properly seen.

Our Ireland guides →
6

Get the most out of every hour

When your time off is fixed, the cheapest trip is the one you don't waste. Fly out early, fly home late, travel light so there's no waiting at the carousel, and book the one thing you'd hate to miss before you go.

❓ Teacher travel FAQ
How can teachers travel cheaply if they can only go during school holidays?
Make weekends count with day trips and Friday-to-Sunday city breaks during term, pick less obvious destinations for the holiday weeks, book holiday-week trips early, and save the big, long trips for the summer, when you have the most time.
Can you travel abroad on a weekend during the school term?
Yes. Our Liverpool extreme day trip was a single Saturday, flying out of Dublin early and home late, and our Christmas market trips were weekend breaks too: one night in Turin, Saturday to Sunday, and Friday to Sunday in Bydgoszcz and Wrocław.
Where can I find Irish school holiday dates?
The Department of Education publishes the standardised mid-term, Christmas and Easter breaks on gov.ie. Summer holidays aren't standardised, so check the exact dates with your school.
