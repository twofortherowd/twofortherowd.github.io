County Tipperary —
Castles, Kings and a Few Hidden Treasures

From the Rock of Cashel to peaceful river valleys, there's far more to Tipp than the famous song.

It's a long way to Tipperary, apparently. But once you arrive, you'll find plenty of reasons to stay.

County Tipperary is packed with castles, ancient religious sites, attractive towns, rolling countryside and fascinating slices of Irish history. Its most famous landmark, the Rock of Cashel, draws visitors from all over the world, but that's only the start. From Cahir Castle on its river island to the glorious Glen of Aherlow and the quieter shores of Lough Derg, there's an enormous variety to discover.

Whether you fancy a proper castle-hopping adventure, a scenic drive or a relaxed weekend of historic towns, Tipperary deserves a place on your Irish travel list.

Kings, Butlers & abbeys
Castles & heritage
History · Ticketed

👑 The Rock of Cashel

One of Ireland's most iconic sights, the Rock of Cashel rises dramatically out of the surrounding countryside. This limestone outcrop was the seat of the kings of Munster, and Brian Boru was crowned High King here in 978.

In 1101 it was granted to the Church, and it became a major religious centre. Today you'll find a cathedral, a round tower and the remarkable Cormac's Chapel, all in a setting that's spectacular in any weather.

Why visit? Medieval architecture, Irish history and dramatic views.

⏱️ Allow 60–90 minutes. Cormac's Chapel is by guided tour only, with limited tickets sold on site. The Rock is very exposed and can close at short notice in bad weather, so check before you go.

History · Ticketed

🏰 Cahir Castle

Standing on a rocky island in the River Suir, Cahir Castle is one of Ireland's largest and best-preserved medieval castles. It was a stronghold of the powerful Butler family and was once thought almost impregnable, until the Earl of Essex took it with heavy artillery in 1599.

Film fans may recognise it from Excalibur and The Tudors. It sits right in the centre of Cahir, so it's easy to pair with a walk around the town.

Why visit? Castle architecture, history, filming locations and riverside scenery.

History · Seasonal

🏡 The Swiss Cottage

Just outside Cahir, the Swiss Cottage looks like it has stepped out of a storybook. It was built in the early 1800s for Richard Butler, 1st Earl of Glengall, as a decorative cottage orné: a romantic countryside retreat for picnics, parties and hunting trips.

Its quirky architecture and carefully designed interiors are a lovely contrast to the stern walls of Cahir Castle, and you can walk between the two along the riverside Coronation Walk.

Why visit? Unusual architecture, gardens and Regency history.

🎟️ The interior is by guided tour only, in small groups on a first-come basis, and the cottage opens seasonally.
Heritage Ireland ↗
History · Place of worship

⛪ Holycross Abbey

Beside the River Suir, Holycross Abbey is an atmospheric medieval abbey founded in 1182. It's traditionally associated with relics of the True Cross, which made it an important place of pilgrimage for centuries.

The stonework is impressive and the setting peaceful: a rewarding stop if you're interested in Irish religious heritage.

Why visit? Medieval history, architecture and pilgrimage traditions.

🙏 Holycross is an active place of worship, so check visiting arrangements and be respectful during services.

History · Seasonal

🏛️ Ormond Castle, Carrick-on-Suir

Ormond Castle is something different from the usual medieval fortress. Its Elizabethan manor house, built in 1565 alongside an earlier riverside castle, is regarded as the finest example of its kind in Ireland.

It was built by Thomas Butler, the 10th Earl of Ormond, in honour of his distant cousin, Queen Elizabeth I, and it's full of elaborate decorative plasterwork.

Why visit? Tudor history, ornate interiors and architectural heritage.

📅 Opens seasonally. Check current opening and tour arrangements before you go.
Heritage Ireland ↗
History · Town

🗼 Nenagh

Nenagh is an appealing market town with a striking landmark: the tall, circular keep of Nenagh Castle, one of the most recognisable sights in North Tipperary.

Interesting streets and independent businesses make it a handy base for exploring the north of the county and Lough Derg.

Why visit? Historic architecture, local heritage and access to North Tipperary.

History · Seasonal

🏰 Roscrea Castle & heritage

Roscrea is one of Ireland's oldest towns, with a remarkable collection of historic buildings. Its medieval castle dates from the 13th century, and the wider complex includes the later Damer House and historic grounds.

Why visit? Medieval heritage, architecture and a lesser-known historic town.

⚠️ Damer House is closed for the whole of 2026 for works. Roscrea Castle opens seasonally for tours, so check before you visit.

Nature & scenery
Outdoors · Scenic

🌄 The Glen of Aherlow

Between the Galtee Mountains and Slievenamuck, the Glen of Aherlow is one of Tipperary's most beautiful landscapes: rolling countryside, forest trails, mountain views and plenty of quiet spots to pull in and take it all in.

It's the perfect antidote to the busier heritage sites if you want the quieter side of the county.

Why visit? Scenic drives, walking, landscape photography and fresh air.

Lake · Villages

🛶 Lough Derg

The Tipperary shores of Lough Derg are a peaceful change from castles and historic towns. Villages like Dromineer and Terryglass offer lakeside scenery, boating and attractive surroundings.

It's ideal for a relaxed weekend, time on the water, or simply a slower pace.

Why visit? Lakeside views, boating, villages and relaxing escapes.

Mountains

⛰️ The Galtee Mountains

For big landscapes, the Galtees offer some of the most memorable scenery in southern Ireland. Experienced hikers can tackle routes towards Galtymore, the range's highest peak.

Everyone else can enjoy the mountains from the surrounding valleys and viewpoints, including the Glen of Aherlow.

Why visit? Hiking, mountain scenery and outdoor adventure.

⛰️ Galtymore is a serious mountain hike, not a casual walk. It needs preparation, proper gear, navigation skills and a good weather forecast.

Hidden gem · Heritage ruin

🏚️ Athassel Abbey

Near Golden, the sprawling ruins of Athassel Abbey give a striking glimpse of medieval monastic life.

ℹ️ This isn't a managed visitor attraction, so research access, parking and ground conditions before you go, and respect private land.
Hidden gem · Outdoor

💧 St Patrick's Well, Clonmel

A peaceful historic holy well in an attractive setting near Clonmel. A lovely, quiet stop if you're interested in Ireland's religious heritage.

Hidden gem · Scenic drive

🌺 The Vee

A celebrated mountain road through the Knockmealdown Mountains, with sweeping views over the countryside below. It's especially famous for its blaze of flowering rhododendrons in season.

Ticketed · Guided

🦇 Mitchelstown Cave

Despite the name, Mitchelstown Cave is in County Tipperary, at Burncourt between Cahir and Mitchelstown. Guided tours take you through about a kilometre of lit passages into huge limestone chambers full of natural rock formations.

ℹ️ Entry is by guided tour, and there are steps on the way down. Check tour times before you go.

🧱 Fethard medieval town

History, legends & strange stories
History, legends and strange stories

We've kept history and legend clearly labelled, because the best Irish stories are usually a bit of both.

Legend

😈 The Devil's Bit

According to local legend, the devil once took a bite out of a mountain in North Tipperary, leaving the unmistakable gap in the Devil's Bit that you can still see today.

In some versions of the tale, he spat the rock out as he flew south, and it landed at Cashel to become the famous Rock. Whether you believe it or not, it's a memorable explanation for two of the county's most distinctive landmarks.

History & tradition

☘️ St Patrick and the Rock of Cashel

Cashel was the seat of the kings of Munster, and tradition says St Patrick came here to convert King Aenghus to Christianity. The history is more certain from there: Brian Boru was crowned High King at Cashel in 978, and in 1101 the Rock was granted to the Church, beginning centuries as a centre of religious power.

History

🛡️ The Butlers of Ormond

The powerful Butler family played an enormous part in Tipperary's political and architectural history. Two of their buildings show their influence in completely different ways: Cahir Castle, a formidable medieval fortress, and Ormond Castle in Carrick-on-Suir, an elegant Elizabethan manor full of Tudor ambition. The Butlers' later Swiss Cottage adds a playful Regency postscript.

Ready-made plans
Three suggested day trips

Three geographically sensible days out. Tipperary is big, so each one sticks to one part of the county.

👑 A: Castles and kings

Ideal for: first-time visitors and history lovers
  1. Morning: the Rock of Cashel.
  2. Lunch: in Cashel town.
  3. Afternoon: continue to Cahir Castle.
  4. Optional extra: take the riverside walk to the Swiss Cottage, if you have time and it's open for the season.

🏡 B: South Tipperary heritage

Ideal for: historic towns and beautiful architecture
  1. Morning: Cahir and its castle.
  2. Midday: the Swiss Cottage.
  3. Afternoon: continue to Carrick-on-Suir for Ormond Castle, if it's open.
  4. Alternative: swap in St Patrick's Well near Clonmel, adjusting your route to suit.

🛶 C: North Tipperary and Lough Derg

Ideal for: relaxed scenery and quieter attractions
  1. Morning: Nenagh town and its castle.
  2. Lunch: a café in Nenagh or a lakeside village.
  3. Afternoon: head to Dromineer and the shores of Lough Derg.
  4. Optional extra: Terryglass, if time allows.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
👍 Best for
History lovers, castle fans, scenic road trips, countryside walks and weekend escapes.
🚗 Getting around
A car is highly recommended if you want to see several parts of the county. Some larger towns are on public transport, but rural attractions are harder to reach.
🗓️ How long to stay
A day covers Cashel and Cahir. Two to three days lets you add more towns and scenery, and North Tipperary and Lough Derg deserve longer.
☀️ Best time to visit
Spring to autumn for gardens, scenery and longer days. Several heritage sites open seasonally or have shorter winter hours.
💶 Budget tip
Combine one major paid attraction, like Cahir Castle or the Rock of Cashel, with free viewpoints, town walks and historic streets.
♿ Accessibility
Castles, ruins and mountain areas often have steps, uneven ground and low doorways. Check each attraction's access information before you go.

Opening times, seasons and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. For general visitor information, see Heritage Ireland and Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Tipperary FAQ
How long do you need at the Rock of Cashel?
Allow about 60–90 minutes. Cormac's Chapel can only be visited on a guided tour, with limited tickets sold on site, and the Rock can close at short notice in bad weather.
Can you visit Cahir Castle and the Swiss Cottage on the same day?
Yes. They're close together, and you can walk between them along the riverside Coronation Walk. The Swiss Cottage opens seasonally and its interior is by guided tour only, so check before you go.
Is Mitchelstown Cave in Cork or Tipperary?
Despite the name, Mitchelstown Cave is in County Tipperary, at Burncourt between Cahir and Mitchelstown. Visits are by guided tour.
What is the legend of the Devil's Bit?
Local legend says the devil bit a chunk out of the mountain, leaving its distinctive gap. In some versions he dropped the rock at Cashel, creating the Rock of Cashel. It's a legend, of course, not geology!
