Switzerland —
fairytale Bern, polished Zurich and a return to the Rhine in Basel

Our Swiss story started with Interrailing in 2005, when we stayed overnight in both Bern and Zurich — two cities that could not have felt more different. Coming into Switzerland after weeks travelling through other parts of Europe, the first thing that struck us was how unbelievably clean, organised and efficient everything was. The trains ran perfectly, the streets looked spotless and everywhere seemed postcard-perfect — and eye-wateringly expensive on a backpacker budget. Years later we came back to Switzerland for a very different kind of trip, this time to Basel on the Rhine, as the gateway to a few days in Alsace. Two very different eras of Swiss travel, on one page.

🚆 Our Swiss Story

Switzerland felt like Europe had suddenly been polished

"After weeks of Interrailing, Switzerland felt almost too perfect — spotless streets, trains that ran exactly when they said they would, crystal-clear rivers and prices that made young backpackers visibly nervous."

Switzerland made a huge impression because it felt so different from many of the other places on our Interrail route. Everything seemed clean, calm and efficient. Train stations were spotless, streets looked carefully maintained, and even the rivers and lakes seemed impossibly clear.

It was beautiful, but it was also the point in the trip where we fully realised that not all European countries were equally kind to an Interrail budget. Even basic food felt expensive, so Swiss restaurant dreams were quickly replaced with supermarket picnics, bread, cheese and whatever was cheapest near the station.

We stayed overnight in Bern and Zurich, and what made the Swiss section so interesting was how different those two cities felt. Bern was calm, medieval and storybook-like. Zurich was bigger, wealthier, more polished and much more international. Years later, we came back to Switzerland for a completely different kind of trip — a short break in Basel, on the Rhine, as our gateway to Alsace.

🏔️ Swiss Scenery & Budget Reality

The scenery was almost unreal

One of the biggest memories from Switzerland overall was simply the scenery. Even between cities, Switzerland felt like a postcard: crystal-clear rivers, lakes shining in the sun, mountains in the distance and train journeys that felt like sightseeing without needing to pay for a tour.

🌊
Crystal-clear rivers

The Aare River in Bern was one of those colours that did not look real — clean, turquoise and completely Swiss.

🏔️
Mountain views

Even in the cities, the mountains never felt far away. Switzerland always had that alpine backdrop somewhere in the distance.

🚆
Train journeys

Swiss trains were part of the experience: clean, punctual, scenic and almost suspiciously efficient compared with everywhere else.

🌅
Lakes in the sunlight

Lake Zurich and the rivers looked unbelievably clear and bright, especially after moving through busier European cities.

The country was stunning — the prices were terrifying

As young Interrailers in 2005, Switzerland felt almost impossibly organised and wealthy compared with many of the other countries on our route. But it also felt expensive in a way that genuinely shaped how we travelled there.

"Switzerland taught us very quickly that supermarket picnics were not a compromise — they were a survival strategy."

Eating out was not really a casual backpacker option. Even simple food felt pricey, so we became very fond of bakeries, supermarkets, station snacks and anything that could be eaten beside a river or on a train platform. Honestly, with Swiss views, even a budget picnic felt fancy.

🥖
Supermarket picnics

Bread, cheese, snacks and anything affordable became the Switzerland backpacker meal plan.

🚆
Train station food

Not exactly glamorous, but very useful when restaurants felt far too expensive.

💸
Budget shock

Coming from cheaper parts of Europe, Switzerland felt like someone had doubled the price of everything overnight.

❓ Common Questions

Switzerland FAQ

Is Switzerland good for Interrailing?
Yes. Switzerland is excellent for Interrailing because the trains are clean, efficient, scenic and reliable. The downside is that Switzerland is much more expensive than many other European countries, especially for food and accommodation.
Is Bern worth visiting?
Yes. Bern is Switzerland's capital and has a beautiful medieval Old City, sandstone arcades, the Zytglogge clock tower, the Bundeshaus, Bear Park, bridges over the Aare River and a calm fairytale atmosphere.
Is Zurich worth visiting?
Yes. Zurich is polished, wealthy and cosmopolitan, with Lake Zurich, Bahnhofstrasse, Grossmünster, Fraumünster, riverfront old town streets, trams and beautiful lakeside scenery.
Which is better, Bern or Zurich?
Bern is better for medieval charm, a relaxed pace and storybook streets. Zurich is better for lakeside scenery, shopping, modern Swiss city energy and a more international atmosphere. They feel very different and work well together on a Switzerland itinerary.
Is Switzerland expensive for backpackers?
Yes. Switzerland can feel eye-wateringly expensive for backpackers and Interrailers. Supermarket picnics, train station snacks and simple accommodation are usually much kinder to the budget than restaurants.
Is Basel worth visiting?
Yes. Basel sits on the Rhine right on the borders of France and Germany, and it is compact, colourful and easy to get around. Highlights include the Old Town, hopping on and off the trams, a two-hour historical walking tour, and dinner at classic Swiss restaurants like Gifthüttli.
Is Basel a good base for Alsace?
Yes. Basel is one of the best bases for combining Switzerland with Alsace. It has excellent transport links, an international airport and easy onward connections to Colmar and Strasbourg.
How do you travel from Basel to Colmar?
The easiest way to travel from Basel to Colmar is by FlixBus. It is an affordable and straightforward option that makes it very easy to combine Switzerland and Alsace in the same trip without needing to hire a car.

Planning your own Switzerland trip?

Go for the trains, the lakes, Bern's medieval charm, Zurich's polished energy and Basel on the Rhine — where you can wander the Old Town, hop on the trams, and even carry on into Alsace with a straightforward FlixBus. Budget carefully, though: Switzerland does not play around with prices.
