🏙️ City guides

Where we have been in Poland

WarsawSummer 2005 • Nicola & friends

Nicola absolutely loved Poland's remarkable Phoenix City. A city tour brought its destruction and reconstruction to life, while the POLIN Museum, Monument to the Ghetto Heroes, Royal Castle, Castle Square, Museum of Warsaw and Wilanów Palace revealed its many layers.

A rebuilt UNESCO-listed historic centre—and a city that made a huge impression during that first Interrail adventure.
Read our Warsaw guide →
KrakówSummer 2005 • Nicola & friends

Kraków combined eating and drinking around the magnificent Main Market Square with three powerful excursions: Auschwitz-Birkenau, Wieliczka Salt Mine and Wadowice, birthplace of Pope John Paul II.

Auschwitz was profoundly moving; the Main Square gave the trip its lighter evenings and atmosphere.
Read our Kraków guide →
PoznańSummer 2005 • return planned 2026

Colourful, relaxed and affordable, Poznań was another gorgeous stop on the Interrail trip. Its Old Market Square and handsome architecture stayed in Nicola's memory long enough to inspire a planned return for the 2026 Christmas markets.

Already visited in summer; next we want to see it dressed for Christmas.
Read our Poznań guide →
WrocławDecember 2025 • Nicola & Podge

Wrocław delivered a fairy-tale Christmas market, a magnificent Old Town and the endlessly entertaining dwarf hunt. Nicola also climbed the narrow tower at St Elizabeth's Basilica for panoramic views across the city.

One of our favourite European Christmas-market breaks.
Read our Wrocław guide →
BydgoszczWinter trip • Nicola & Podge

Bydgoszcz was the unexpected one: beautiful Mill Island, reflections along the river, Christmas lights and a city that still felt firmly owned by the people who live there. It is exactly the kind of under-the-radar destination Two for the Row'd loves finding.

Poland's hidden gem—and one of the loveliest surprises of our travels.
Read our Bydgoszcz guide →

❓ Practical questions

Poland travel FAQ

What currency does Poland use?

Poland uses the Polish złoty (PLN), not the euro. Cards are widely accepted, but a little cash remains useful.

Which cities suit a first Poland trip?

Warsaw and Kraków make an excellent first combination. Add Wrocław for beautiful architecture and Christmas markets, Poznań for colour and atmosphere, or Bydgoszcz for somewhere quieter and less familiar.

Is Poland affordable?

Poland generally offers very good value compared with Western Europe, although prices have increased in the busiest tourist centres.

Can you travel between Polish cities by train?

Yes. Poland's rail network makes Warsaw, Kraków, Poznań, Wrocław and Bydgoszcz straightforward to combine.

When are Poland's Christmas markets?

Dates vary by city, but most begin in late November and run through December. Always check the official dates before booking flights.
