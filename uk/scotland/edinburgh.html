Edinburgh —
cobbled streets, a castle on the rock, and one of Europe's most beautiful capitals

Edinburgh deserves its own visit entirely. The Old Town spreads across cobbled streets below Edinburgh Castle, which sits perched dramatically on volcanic rock high above the city. The Royal Mile connects the castle to Holyrood Palace, lined with shops, restaurants and galleries. Walk the atmospheric streets, explore the castle, take in the views and understand why Edinburgh is considered one of the great European capital cities.

Our Edinburgh experience

We've explored Edinburgh's Old Town thoroughly, walking the Royal Mile from the castle down to Holyrood Palace, climbing up and down the narrow closes (alleyways) that branch off into hidden courtyards and secret spaces. We've visited Edinburgh Castle, sat on the cobbles watching the city unfold below, and discovered why Edinburgh feels like one of Europe's most atmospheric capital cities.

The energy of the Old Town is palpable — locals and visitors mixing in the pubs and restaurants, street performers on the Royal Mile, the castle looming above everything. It's a city that feels genuinely lived-in rather than preserved as a museum piece.

What to see and do

Edinburgh Castle

Perched dramatically on volcanic rock 130 metres above the city, Edinburgh Castle is the iconic symbol of Edinburgh and Scotland. It's been a fortress since the 11th century and has played a key role in Scottish history. The castle is genuinely impressive — the views over the city from the ramparts are spectacular, and inside there's the Crown Jewels, the Stone of Scone, and centuries of Scottish history to explore.

Give yourself at least two hours to do the castle properly. The steep climb up from the city centre is worth it for the views alone.

The Royal Mile

This famous street runs from Edinburgh Castle down to Holyrood Palace at the foot of the hill — about a mile long (though it actually varies between the Lawnmarket, High Street and Canongate). It's lined with shops, restaurants, galleries and some excellent pubs. But the real magic is in the closes (alleyways) that branch off — narrow passages that open into hidden courtyards and lead to atmospheric bars, shops and galleries.

Spend time wandering, not rushing. Stop for a drink in a hidden courtyard. Chat to the street performers. The Royal Mile reveals itself to those who slow down.

The Old Town's hidden closes and courtyards

Edinburgh's Old Town is built on a slope and the closes create a labyrinth of atmospheric passages. Mary King's Close is famous (and busy) — a preserved 17th-century alley. But there are countless others to explore. Some lead to hidden pubs and restaurants, others to quiet courtyards where locals come to escape tourists. This is where Edinburgh's real character lives.

Grassmarket and surroundings

A historic square at the foot of the castle, originally used for markets and (grimly) public executions. Today it's surrounded by pubs and restaurants and is a great place to sit and watch Edinburgh go by. In summer, there's often a festival atmosphere with street performers and outdoor bars.

Camera Obscura and World of Illusions

A quirky attraction on the Royal Mile that offers unusual views of Edinburgh through a telescope-like device (the camera obscura) and various optical illusions. A bit touristy but genuinely interesting, especially if you like photography or visual tricks.

Calton Hill and the National Monument

A short walk from the city centre, Calton Hill offers 360-degree views over Edinburgh and the surrounding countryside. The National Monument (an incomplete replica of the Parthenon) is the standout landmark. It's a free, less-touristy alternative to climbing the castle.

Arthur's Seat

The highest point in Edinburgh at 251 metres, offering dramatic views over the city and the Firth of Forth beyond. It's a reasonably strenuous hike (about 45 minutes to the top) but worth it for the views. On a clear day you can see for miles. It's surprisingly wild and rugged for being right in the middle of the city.

Getting there and getting around

Getting to Edinburgh

From Ireland: Direct flights from Dublin, Cork and Shannon to Edinburgh Airport are frequent and relatively affordable. The airport is about 8 miles west of the city centre — you can take a bus (Airlink 100), taxi or car rental from there.

From London or other UK cities: Train travel is excellent — the journey from London King's Cross takes about 7 hours and is scenic. Flights from UK airports are also an option.

Getting around Edinburgh

Edinburgh city centre is very walkable — most attractions are within walking distance. The Old Town and city centre can be explored on foot in 2-3 days. Public transport (buses) is good if you need it, but honestly you won't need much beyond your feet.

Don't rent a car for Edinburgh itself — the city centre is compact and has challenging traffic. If you're planning to explore beyond Edinburgh (Stirling, the Highlands), rent after you leave the city.

How much time to spend

Minimum: 2 days gives you time for the castle, a walk down the Royal Mile, explore some closes, and get a feel for the city.

Ideal: 3-4 days lets you explore properly without rushing, climb Arthur's Seat, visit museums and galleries, enjoy the pubs and restaurants, and actually get to know the city rather than just ticking boxes.

Extended: A week in Edinburgh is genuinely possible — there's enough depth here for that, and it's a great base for day trips to Stirling Castle, the Highlands or the Scottish Borders.

Food and drink

Eating in Edinburgh

Edinburgh has a genuinely good food scene — far beyond the stereotypical haggis and deep-fried everything. The city centre has restaurants ranging from fine dining to casual, lots of excellent independent cafes, and pubs serving real Scottish ales.

The Old Town has some slightly touristy restaurants but also authentic local spots if you explore the closes. Leith (the port area north of the city) has become fashionable in recent years with excellent restaurants and bars.

Don't miss trying Scottish whisky — Edinburgh has several whisky bars where you can taste different regions and styles. And if you do want the traditional haggis, neeps and tatties experience, there are plenty of places to do it properly.

Common questions

Edinburgh FAQ

When is the best time to visit Edinburgh?
Late spring through early autumn (May to September) offers the best weather. July and August are busiest (and warmest) but also most expensive. September is often excellent — less crowded than July/August but still warm and with longer days than winter. Winter can be cold and dark but atmospheric, especially at Christmas. Spring (April-May) is lovely with longer days and fewer crowds.
How much does Edinburgh Castle cost?
Prices vary by season — generally £20-£25 for adults. Check ahead as prices change. If you're planning to visit multiple attractions, the Edinburgh Pass might save money depending on what you want to do.
Is Edinburgh expensive?
Edinburgh is more expensive than many other UK cities but less expensive than London. Accommodation, especially in the Old Town, can be pricey in summer. Eating and drinking varies hugely — you can find excellent food at all price points. Using public transport is cheap, and many attractions (walking the Old Town, climbing Calton Hill and Arthur's Seat, exploring closes) are free.
Is Edinburgh safe?
Edinburgh is generally very safe, especially the Old Town and main tourist areas. Like any city, use normal precautions with valuables and be aware of your surroundings, especially late at night. Overall it's one of the safest major cities in the UK.
Can I do a day trip from Edinburgh?
Yes — Edinburgh is a great base for day trips. Stirling Castle is about 45 minutes away by train. The Scottish Highlands are accessible (though a full day trip is better than a rushed few hours). Some people do Glasgow as a day trip, though it's better with 2-3 hours to explore properly.

Ready to explore Edinburgh?

Edinburgh deserves time — at least 3 days to do it justice. Walk the Royal Mile, climb the castle, explore the hidden closes, and discover one of Europe's most atmospheric capital cities.
