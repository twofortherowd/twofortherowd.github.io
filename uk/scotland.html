Scotland —
mountains, history and one of Europe's greatest road trips

We've driven most of the Scottish Highlands and it remains one of our favourite road trips anywhere in the world. The landscape is extraordinary — mountains, lochs, ancient castles and skies that change mood every ten minutes. From Edinburgh's Old Town and Aberdeen's granite streets to Stirling Castle, Loch Ness and Glencoe, Scotland rewards every moment you give it.

Common questions

Scotland FAQ

Is the Scottish Highlands worth driving?
One of the greatest road trips in Europe — the Scottish Highlands are jaw-dropping. Stirling Castle, Loch Ness, Glencoe, Inverness, Fort William — every turn in the road reveals something more dramatic than the last. Give yourself at least a week to do it properly and don't rush it.
How many days do you need in Scotland?
Edinburgh alone deserves two or three days to do it justice. For the Highlands, give yourself at least a week if you want to properly experience the landscape without rushing. A fortnight is better still if you can manage it.
Do I need a car to explore Scotland?
Edinburgh has excellent public transport and is very walkable. For the Highlands, having your own wheels gives you freedom and flexibility to stop wherever you want and explore smaller roads. Bus tours are also available if you prefer not to drive.
When is the best time to visit Scotland?
Late spring through early autumn (May to September) offers the best weather, though it's also busier. The Highlands are spectacular year-round — winter can be dramatic and snowy, summer has long daylight hours. Avoid peak July/August if you prefer fewer crowds.
