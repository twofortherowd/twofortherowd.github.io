Ancient Kings, Castles & Mystical Ireland

Where prehistoric wonders, royal legends and remarkable history are waiting around every corner.

If you like your history ancient, your castles impressive and your Irish legends just a little bit mysterious, County Meath is going to be right up your street.

Known as the Royal County, Meath was linked with the High Kings of Ireland, and its landscape is packed with extraordinary places: monuments older than the pyramids, mighty Norman castles, ruined abbeys and hills that have inspired folklore for thousands of years.

It isn't all ancient stones, either. There are pretty villages, riverside walks, a sandy coast and plenty of places to stop for a bite, and it's close enough to Dublin for a day trip or a lazy weekend.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Meath, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🎟️ Planning Newgrange? Book first

Newgrange and Knowth can only be visited on guided tours from the Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre near Donore, and tickets need to be booked in advance through Heritage Ireland. Don't drive to the monument itself, as there's no public access there.

UNESCO · Book ahead

🌀 Brú na Bóinne: Newgrange & Knowth

A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most important prehistoric landscapes in Europe. Newgrange was built around 3200 BC, making it older than Stonehenge and the Great Pyramid of Giza.

It's famous for its winter solstice alignment, when the rising sun lights up the inner chamber. Nearby Knowth holds the largest collection of passage-tomb art in Western Europe.

Why visit? Archaeology, Irish heritage and a genuinely once-in-a-lifetime visit.

🎟️ You can only reach Newgrange and Knowth on a guided tour from the Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre near Donore, and you need to book in advance. Knowth is seasonal, and winter arrangements differ.
Heritage Ireland ↗
Ancient site · Free

👑 The Hill of Tara

The ceremonial heart of ancient Ireland, linked in tradition with the High Kings. Today you walk among earthworks, burial mounds and the Lia Fáil, with huge views across the Meath countryside.

Why visit? Ancient Ireland, mythology and a leisurely walk.

ℹ️ The open landscape is accessible all year. Visitor centre services and guided tours are seasonal.
Castle · Guided tours

🏰 Trim Castle

The largest Anglo-Norman castle in Ireland, begun by Hugh de Lacy in the 12th century and still dominating the town beside the Boyne. Film fans might recognise it from Braveheart.

Why visit? Medieval history, photography and families.

🎟️ Access to the keep is by guided tour, so check availability before you go.
History · Visitor centre

⚔️ Battle of the Boyne Visitor Centre

At Oldbridge House, on the site of the 1690 battle between King William III and King James II, one of the most consequential days in Irish and British history. The estate has gardens and walks too.

Why visit? History and educational visits.

Village · Castle

🎸 Slane Castle & Slane Village

Slane Castle is world-famous for its open-air concerts, and Slane village has a handsome 18th-century streetscape. Castle and distillery visits run depending on current schedules.

Why visit? Music heritage, architecture and countryside scenery.

Ancient site · Views

🔥 The Hill of Slane

Tradition says St Patrick lit a Paschal fire here in defiance of the High King at Tara. Today there are church and friary ruins, and wide views across the Boyne Valley.

Why visit? Early Christian history and views.

Beyond the Boyne
Loughcrew, Kells, family days & the coast
Prehistory · Uphill

🌄 Loughcrew Cairns

Passage tombs in the hills near Oldcastle, with sweeping views. At the spring and autumn equinoxes, sunlight lights up the carvings inside Cairn T.

Why visit? Archaeology, landscapes and ancient mysteries.

⛰️ Expect a steep, uneven walk up. Check monument access before you travel.
Abbey ruins · Free

🏚️ Bective Abbey

The remains of a medieval Cistercian abbey with atmospheric cloisters and towers. Part of Braveheart was filmed here too.

Why visit? Medieval architecture and photography.

Heritage town

✝️ Kells

The monastery here is closely linked to the Book of Kells, which is now displayed in Dublin. Look out for the high crosses, the round tower and St Columba's House.

Why visit? Early Christian history and heritage trails.

Family · Theme park

🎢 Emerald Park

Near Ashbourne, mixing rides, a zoo and family attractions. A big day out for children and thrill-seekers.

Why visit? Families and thrill-seekers.

🎟️ Check seasonal opening dates, tickets and individual ride restrictions.
River · Walking

🚶 Boyne Valley walks

The Boyne flows through some of Ireland's most historic landscapes. The riverside around Trim is a lovely place to start.

Why visit? Gentle walks with history at every turn.

ℹ️ Check routes before you go. There isn't continuous public access along the river.
Coast · Beach

🏖️ Bettystown & Laytown

Yes, Meath has a coastline! Long sandy beaches for seaside strolls and fresh air, and a very different side of the Royal County.

Why visit? Beach walks and family outings.

🌊 Follow local beach safety signs and check bathing-water information before swimming.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Legend & archaeology

👑 The High Kings of Tara

Tara is woven into Irish tradition as the seat of the High Kings. The Lia Fáil, the Stone of Destiny, was said to roar when the rightful king touched it.

Archaeology tells a more complicated story: Tara was a ceremonial landscape used for thousands of years, rather than a royal palace in the way we'd picture one.

Folklore

🧙 The Cailleach of Loughcrew

One legend says the Cailleach, a powerful hag-goddess, created the Loughcrew cairns by dropping stones from her apron as she leapt from hill to hill.

It's a lovely example of folklore explaining monuments long after their original purpose was forgotten.

Tradition

🔥 St Patrick and the fire at Slane

The story goes that St Patrick lit an Easter fire on the Hill of Slane, in full view of Tara, where no fire was allowed to burn before the High King's. It became one of the founding stories of Christian Ireland.

Archaeology

☀️ The winter solstice at Newgrange

For a few mornings around 21 December, sunlight creeps through the 'roof-box' above the entrance and along the passage to the inner chamber. It was built to do that more than 5,000 years ago.

Places inside the chamber for the solstice are allocated by an annual free lottery run by the OPW.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚗 Getting around
A car is strongly recommended. The historic sites are scattered across the county.
🗓️ Best time
April to September for walks and long days. Winter is wonderfully atmospheric at the ancient sites.
⏳ How long
Two days for an introduction, three for the Boyne Valley and north Meath.
🎟️ Booking
Book Newgrange through Heritage Ireland. Don't turn up without a ticket.
♿ Accessibility
Some ancient sites have steep slopes, uneven ground or restricted access to the monuments.
🗺️ County lines
Drogheda is mainly in County Louth. The Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre is in Meath.

Opening times, tours and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Discover Boyne Valley, Meath County Council, Heritage Ireland and Emerald Park.

Common questions
County Meath FAQ
Do I need to book Newgrange in advance?
Yes. Newgrange is one place where turning up without a ticket is a real risk. Access is only by guided tour from the Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre, and you should book through Heritage Ireland.
Can I go inside Newgrange on the winter solstice?
Places inside the chamber at the solstice are given out through an annual free lottery run by the OPW. You can still visit Newgrange on a normal tour all year round.
Is Drogheda in Meath?
Mostly no. Drogheda is mainly in County Louth, although the Boyne Valley tourism region spans both counties. The Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre is in County Meath.
How many days do you need in Meath?
Two days give a good introduction. Three let you see both the Boyne Valley and the quieter north of the county.
Have Two for the Row'd visited these places?
