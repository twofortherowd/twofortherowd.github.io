biergartens, history and a city Nicola called home

Germany has been part of our travel story since 2005, when Nicola interrailed through Berlin, Leipzig, Cologne and Munich and fell for the country's extraordinary mix of history, culture and craic. A decade later she moved to Cologne for a year, learned German, and found out what it really means to live like a Kölner. Between the interrail trip, the year in Cologne and the day trips it made possible, we've built up a proper collection of German destinations — each with its own page below.

Leipzig, Bonn, Düsseldorf & Aachen

Not every stop needs a full page of its own — yet. These four don't (yet) have enough personal material for a standalone guide, but they're all genuinely worth knowing about.

🎵
Leipzig

A city still finding its post-reunification identity when Nicola passed through in 2005, full of energy and a music scene that felt genuinely alive. Bach was born here, and the city wears its cultural heritage with quiet pride.

Drive Leipzig from home →
🎼
Bonn

Beethoven's birthplace and a charming, manageable city on the Rhine. The old government quarter, the beautiful market square and the lovely riverside make it a perfect half-day trip from Cologne.

Drive Bonn from home →
🍺
Düsseldorf

Cologne's great rival — famous for its Altbier (the dark alternative to Cologne's Kölsch) and its Altstadt, the so-called longest bar in the world. A brilliant city with a distinct personality and well worth a day trip from Cologne.

Drive Düsseldorf from home →
⚜️
Aachen

The seat of Charlemagne and one of the great medieval cities of Europe — the Cathedral is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the oldest in northern Europe. Right on the borders of Belgium and the Netherlands, it feels like three countries in one place.

Germany FAQ

Germany FAQ

Is Germany good for interrailing?
Germany is one of the best interrailing countries in Europe — an excellent rail network connecting cities quickly and efficiently, and a huge variety of destinations from Berlin to Munich to the Rhine Valley. Nicola interrailed through Germany in 2005, visiting Berlin, Leipzig, Cologne and Munich, and it remains one of her favourite travel experiences.
Which German city should I visit first?
It depends what you're after. Cologne is our most personal recommendation since Nicola lived there for a year, Berlin is essential for its history, Munich is unbeatable for biergarten culture and Heidelberg is one of the prettiest small cities in the country. Frankfurt is worth knowing about mainly as a very well-connected stopover.
Is Dachau worth visiting?
Yes — though "worth visiting" is almost the wrong phrase. Dachau is a deeply moving and important memorial site, sobering and educational rather than a typical tourist attraction. It is an easy day trip from Munich and something we would encourage every visitor to Bavaria to consider.
How many days do you need in Germany?
Germany rewards as much time as you can give it. A single city like Cologne or Munich deserves at least two or three days, while a proper interrail-style trip taking in Berlin, Leipzig, Cologne and Munich is easily a fortnight.
