🐠 The Great Barrier Reef

Diving the Great Barrier Reef — one of the best things we've ever done

The Great Barrier Reef is one of those bucket list experiences that people sometimes worry will disappoint — too hyped, too touristy. It does not disappoint. We did two dives from Cairns and it remains one of our absolute favourite experiences from our around-the-world trip. The reef stretches over 2,300km along the Queensland coast and diving it is genuinely like entering another world.

The colour of the coral, the variety of the fish, the clarity of the water and the sheer otherness of spending time beneath the ocean's surface — it is extraordinary. For us, diving was an unforgettable way to experience the reef. If diving is suitable for you, introductory options may be available through local operators; snorkelling is another popular way to experience the reef.

🌆 About Cairns

The gateway to the reef

Cairns is a small, tropical city in Far North Queensland and it is essentially the launch pad for the Great Barrier Reef. Almost every reef trip departs from Cairns Marlin Marina, and the city has grown around that role — a busy waterfront, an easy-going tropical vibe and an outsize backpacker and diver culture given its size.

Beyond the reef itself, Cairns is the natural base for exploring the Daintree Rainforest — one of the oldest continuously surviving tropical rainforests in the world — as well as Kuranda, Port Douglas and the vast wet tropics of North Queensland.

💡 Practical Tips

Diving the Great Barrier Reef — practical info

  • Full-day reef trips typically depart Cairns Marlin Marina around 8am and return late afternoon. Two dives fits comfortably into a standard day trip.
  • Introductory dives: some operators offer supervised introductory dives for people without certification, subject to their health, age and safety requirements. Certified divers have additional dive options.
  • Motion sickness: the boat rides can be choppy — take medication before boarding if you are sensitive.
  • Sun protection: the Queensland sun is strong, so bring high-SPF sunscreen, a hat and suitable cover for the boat.
  • Best time to visit: June to October has the best visibility and calmer seas. Stinger season (jellyfish) runs roughly November to May.

Common questions

FAQ

Is the Great Barrier Reef worth diving?
Absolutely — diving the Great Barrier Reef is one of the great travel experiences on earth. The world beneath the ocean is something else entirely. We did two dives and it remains one of our favourite experiences of the entire trip. The colour, the life, the sheer scale of the reef — no snorkel or glass-bottom boat comes close to diving it properly.
Do you need to be a certified diver?
No — most reef operators offer introductory / resort dives that require no prior certification. You get a briefing, a training session and then dive with an instructor. Certified divers can go deeper and do more dives per day.
How long should you spend in Cairns?
Two to three nights is enough for a reef day trip and a rainforest or Kuranda day. Add another night or two if you want to get out to Port Douglas or spend a full day in the Daintree.
When is the best time to dive the Great Barrier Reef?
June to October generally has the best visibility, calmer seas and no jellyfish. Stinger season (roughly November to May) means operators provide protective suits.
