⛵ Our Whitsundays Experience

Catamaran on the Coral Sea

The Whitsundays were everything they promised. We spent the day on a catamaran gliding through some of the most beautiful stretches of ocean we'd seen — the Coral Sea, dotted with 74 tropical islands, all sitting on the edge of the Great Barrier Reef.

The water, the islands, the light — every bit as stunning as it looks in the brochures. One of those days that reminds you exactly why you got on a long-haul flight in the first place.

🏖️ Whitehaven Beach

Some of the whitest sand in the world

Whitehaven Beach on Whitsunday Island is one of the most celebrated beaches on earth — seven kilometres of impossibly pure, brilliantly white silica sand. The sand is 98% silica, so fine it squeaks underfoot, and so pale it looks almost otherworldly against the turquoise water.

The Hill Inlet lookout at the northern end gives the view that appears on every postcard of the Whitsundays — the swirling patterns of white sand and clear water as the tides mix them together.

💡 Practical Tips

Whitsundays practical info

  • Getting there: Most travellers fly into Hamilton Island Airport or Whitsunday Coast Airport (Proserpine). Airlie Beach on the mainland is the main jumping-off town for day trips.
  • Day trip vs multi-day sail: a full-day catamaran is the easiest introduction. Multi-day sailing trips (2-3 nights) let you stay out on the water and reach quieter corners.
  • Marine stingers: follow current local advice and your operator's guidance about protective suits and seasonal conditions.
  • When to visit: weather, sea conditions and marine-stinger risk vary through the year, so check current local guidance when planning.

Common questions

FAQ

Are the Whitsundays worth visiting?
Absolutely — the Whitsundays are every bit as stunning as they look. 74 tropical islands, the Coral Sea, world-class beaches and easy access to the outer Great Barrier Reef. One of the most beautiful stretches of ocean anywhere.
What is the best way to see the Whitsundays?
We went out on a catamaran for the day, which was a brilliant way for us to experience the islands and Whitehaven Beach. Multi-day sailing trips are the next step up if you want to spend nights on the water.
Why is Whitehaven Beach so white?
The sand at Whitehaven is 98% silica — one of the purest silica beaches in the world. That's what gives it its brilliant white colour and its famously fine, squeaky texture.
Where do you fly into for the Whitsundays?
Hamilton Island Airport for direct access to the islands, or Whitsunday Coast Airport (Proserpine) for Airlie Beach on the mainland, which is where most day tours depart from.
