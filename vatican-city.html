🙏 The Day We Witnessed History

Pope John Paul II's final Easter — St Peter's Square, 2005

"Standing in St Peter's Square for Pope John Paul II's final Easter was one of those moments you never forget. The crowd, the atmosphere, the weight of history — there is nowhere else on earth quite like the Vatican, and no visit we've ever had that could match it."

Of all our visits to Vatican City, one will always stand apart. In 2005, we were in Rome over Easter and made our way to St Peter's Square along with tens of thousands of others for the Easter blessing. Pope John Paul II — by then very frail — appeared, and gave what would be his final Easter blessing before his death only a few days later.

The square was packed. The atmosphere was reverent and deeply emotional — you could feel people around us grasping that this was something they were unlikely to see again. Pilgrims from every corner of the world, all in one square, sharing a single moment. The kind of thing you don't quite process until you look back on it years later and realise you were there.

It is the reason we always say: if you ever have the chance to attend a papal event at the Vatican — a Wednesday audience, a Sunday Angelus, an Easter blessing — take it. You don't need to be religious to feel the weight of the place. The Vatican has a way of making history feel very close.

❤️ Our Vatican Experience

The world's smallest country — and one of its greatest wonders

Vatican City is the world's smallest independent state — just 44 hectares enclosed within Rome — but what it contains is almost impossible to comprehend. The accumulated art, history, architecture and spiritual significance of centuries of the Catholic Church, all in one extraordinary place.

We've been lucky enough to visit multiple times and every visit brings something new. The first time you walk into St Peter's Basilica and your eyes adjust to the scale of it — the soaring dome, the marble, the gold — it genuinely takes your breath away. It is simply one of the most awe-inspiring buildings ever constructed.

The Vatican Museums are a world unto themselves — room after room of sculpture, tapestries, maps, paintings and artefacts gathered from across the globe and across the centuries. And then you turn a corner and you're standing in the Sistine Chapel, looking up at Michelangelo's ceiling — and all words fail you. Nothing quite prepares you for it. Even the fifth time you see it.

⭐ Best Things to Do in Vatican City
⛪
1. St Peter's Basilica

One of the most magnificent buildings ever constructed — the scale, the art, the dome. Entry is free (queue for security). Absolutely essential and completely unforgettable.

🎨
2. The Sistine Chapel

Michelangelo's ceiling is one of the greatest artistic achievements in human history. Smaller than you'd expect, which only makes it more astonishing. Accessed via the Vatican Museums only.

🏛️
3. The Vatican Museums

Centuries of treasures — sculpture, tapestries, maps, paintings, archaeological finds. You could spend a full day here and only begin to see it all. Book in advance, always.

🏟️
4. St Peter's Square

Bernini's colonnade-flanked piazza is one of the great public spaces in the world — whether packed with pilgrims or quiet in the early morning, it is always stunning. Free to visit.

🙏
5. A Papal Event

General Audiences take place on Wednesday mornings when the Pope is in residence; the Angelus is given from a window on Sundays. Free to attend and genuinely moving.

✨
6. The Gallery of Maps

One of the most beautiful rooms in the entire Vatican — a long corridor lined with stunning 16th-century topographical maps of Italy. Often overlooked. Absolutely worth stopping for.

⛪ Basilica vs Museums — What's the Difference?

This is one of the most common sources of confusion at the Vatican — and getting it right in advance will save you hours. The Basilica and the Museums are two completely separate experiences with different entrances, different queues, and different rules.

⛪ St Peter's Basilica

  • Free to enter
  • Entrance via St Peter's Square
  • Queue for security screening
  • Dress code enforced strictly
  • Dome climb costs extra (worth it)
  • Does NOT include the Sistine Chapel

🏛️ Vatican Museums

  • Paid ticket required — book in advance
  • Separate entrance on Viale Vaticano
  • Skip-the-line tickets save hours
  • Includes the Sistine Chapel at the end
  • Includes the Gallery of Maps, Raphael Rooms & more
  • Allow at least 3 hours

Key thing to know: the Sistine Chapel is inside the Museums. You cannot reach it from the Basilica. If seeing the Sistine Chapel matters to you (and it should), you need a Museums ticket.

🗺️ One Day in Vatican City — Our Itinerary

A full day is the honest minimum for Vatican City if you want to do it justice. Here's the order we'd take — Museums first (they're timed entry, so this locks in your booking), Basilica after lunch when the morning crowds have thinned.

Early Morning — The Vatican Museums

  1. Arrive for your pre-booked slot: The earliest morning entry you can get. Non-negotiable — the queue for walk-ups can be 2+ hours.
  2. Work through the Museums: Egyptian rooms, Etruscan halls, sculpture galleries — don't rush past them just to get to the Sistine Chapel.
  3. Gallery of Maps: One of the most beautiful corridors in the Vatican — take your time here.
  4. Raphael Rooms: The frescoes are extraordinary. This is one of the great highlights.
  5. Sistine Chapel: The Museums route ends here. Sit on the side benches if you can, look up, and stay a while. No photos.

Lunch — Step outside the Vatican

  1. Where: The streets just around Borgo Pio (a short walk from the Basilica) have plenty of decent trattorias. Prices are inflated but not crazy.
  2. Tip: Give yourself a proper break — you've just walked several kilometres through the Museums.

Afternoon — St Peter's Square & Basilica

  1. St Peter's Square: Approach via Via della Conciliazione for the full Bernini colonnade reveal — one of the great walks in Europe.
  2. St Peter's Basilica: Free to enter, queue for security. Give yourself at least 90 minutes inside — the scale is genuinely disorienting.
  3. Michelangelo's Pietà: Just inside the Basilica on the right. Don't miss it.

Late Afternoon — The Dome (optional but recommended)

  1. Dome climb: Small fee. Take the lift for the first section, then it's stairs. Panoramic views over St Peter's Square and the whole of Rome from the top — one of the great city views in the world.
  2. Wind down: Wander back into the streets of Rome — you've earned an aperitivo.

💡 Nicola & Podge's Vatican Tip

Don't try to squeeze the Vatican between five other Rome attractions. Give it a proper day. The Museums alone can swallow hours, the Basilica deserves time to sit in, and the whole point of coming is to actually take it in rather than tick it off.

🎟️ Before You Go — Practical Tips

What we wish we'd known the first time

  • Book the Vatican Museums in advance. Always. The walk-up queue can eat two hours of your day.
  • The Basilica and Museums are two separate experiences with different entrances. See the comparison above.
  • Dress code is enforced strictly. Shoulders and knees covered for both men and women — no shorts, no vest tops. Carry a light scarf if you're travelling in summer.
  • Security screening for everything. Both the Basilica and Museums have airport-style security. Small bags only — big bags will slow you down.
  • No photos or video inside the Sistine Chapel. None. Guards will remind anyone who tries.
  • Comfortable shoes are essential. The Museums route alone is around 7km if you walk it end to end.
  • Early morning is best. Book the earliest Museums slot you can get.
  • Papal audiences: Traditionally Wednesday mornings when the Pope is in Rome. Free but often requires a ticket booked in advance. Check the Vatican's official website for current schedules — arrangements can change.

❓ Vatican City FAQ
Is Vatican City worth visiting?
Absolutely — Vatican City is one of the most extraordinary places on earth. The scale, the art, the history and the sheer density of things to see make it a must on any trip to Rome. You could spend hours there and still feel like you've only scratched the surface.
Is St Peter's Basilica free to enter?
Yes — entry to St Peter's Basilica itself is free, though you'll queue for security. Climbing the dome for panoramic views over Rome costs extra (small fee for the stairs, slightly more if you use the lift for the first section). The Vatican Museums are a completely separate experience and require a paid ticket.
Do I need a Vatican Museums ticket to see the Sistine Chapel?
Yes — the Sistine Chapel is inside the Vatican Museums complex and can only be accessed through a Museums visit. You cannot enter the Sistine Chapel from St Peter's Basilica. Book your Museums ticket in advance to save significant queuing time.
How long do you need in Vatican City?
To do it properly — St Peter's Basilica, St Peter's Square, the Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel — you need a full day. A half day is possible but you'll feel rushed. Give the Museums at least three hours on their own.
What should I wear to the Vatican?
There's a strict dress code for both St Peter's Basilica and the Vatican Museums — shoulders and knees must be covered for both men and women. No shorts, no vest tops, no bare shoulders. A light scarf or shawl is handy to carry in summer so you can cover up on entry.
Can you take photos inside the Vatican?
Mostly yes — photography (without flash) is generally allowed in St Peter's Basilica and throughout most of the Vatican Museums. However, photography and video are strictly forbidden inside the Sistine Chapel — no exceptions. Guards will remind you if you forget.
What is the best time to visit the Vatican?
Early morning is best, especially for the Vatican Museums, which get very busy from mid-morning onwards. Book the earliest available Museums slot, and consider visiting St Peter's Basilica later in the day when the morning tour groups have moved on.
Can you see the Pope when visiting Vatican City?
Sometimes yes — the Pope traditionally gives a General Audience on Wednesday mornings when in residence in Rome, and the Angelus blessing on Sundays from a window overlooking St Peter's Square. Attendance is free but tickets are often required for the Wednesday audience. Check the Vatican's official website for current schedules, as arrangements change.

Planning your own Vatican visit?

Book your Museums tickets in advance, wear comfortable shoes, carry a light scarf for the dress code and give yourself a full day. Vatican City rewards every minute of it — and if a papal event is happening while you're in town, go. You'll remember it forever.
