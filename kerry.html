County Kerry —
Mountains, Magic & the Wild Atlantic

Big scenery, winding roads, unforgettable villages and the sort of views that make you pull over every five minutes.

If there's one county that knows how to show off Ireland's natural beauty, it's Kerry.

Towering mountains, sparkling lakes, golden beaches and rugged Atlantic islands: the Kingdom has an extraordinary range of landscapes, plus two of Ireland's most famous road trips, the Ring of Kerry and the Dingle Peninsula.

But there's much more beyond the postcard views: ancient monasteries, colourful harbour towns, historic houses and stories going back centuries. Kerry isn't somewhere to rush. It's the kind of place where the journey is often just as memorable as the destination.

02
Start here
Killarney & around
National park · Free

🦌 Killarney National Park

Lakes, ancient oak woods and mountains make Killarney National Park one of the most beautiful protected landscapes in Ireland. It was the country's first national park and is home to Ireland's only native herd of red deer.

Walk, cycle or take a jaunting car along the lakeshores and woodland paths, and keep an eye out for the deer.

Why visit? Nature, walking, photography and outdoor adventures.

House · Gardens

🏛️ Muckross House & Gardens

This grand 19th-century mansion sits inside the national park beside Muckross Lake. Its rooms and landscaped gardens give a glimpse of life on a great Irish estate, and Queen Victoria visited in 1861.

Nearby, Muckross Traditional Farms show a very different side of Kerry life.

Why visit? Historic houses, gardens and family heritage.

Waterfall · Free

💦 Torc Waterfall

A short walk through woodland brings you to this roughly 20-metre waterfall, at its most impressive after heavy rain.

Why visit? Woodland scenery and photography.

🥾 Expect steps and uneven ground on some paths.
Castle · Lakeside

🏰 Ross Castle

This 15th-century tower house stands on the edge of Lough Leane, the ancestral stronghold of the O'Donoghue chieftains. The lakeside setting is gorgeous, and boats to Innisfallen Island leave from here in season.

Why visit? Castles, history and lakeside scenery.

📅 The castle interior is open seasonally, by guided tour.
Mountain pass

⛰️ The Gap of Dunloe

A dramatic mountain pass near Killarney, with a winding road, small lakes and rugged scenery. Many people walk or cycle it, and some arrange a boat back across the lakes to Ross Castle.

Why visit? Mountain scenery, photography and outdoor adventures.

🚶 The road is narrow and busy with walkers, cyclists and jaunting cars, so it's best enjoyed on foot or by bike.
03
Road-trip territory
The peninsulas, islands & beyond
Scenic drive

🛣️ The Ring of Kerry

One of Ireland's most famous drives: a loop of roughly 180 km around the Iveragh Peninsula, through mountains, coastal villages and huge Atlantic views.

Killorglin, Cahersiveen, Waterville and Sneem are all worth a stop, and Derrynane and Kenmare make lovely detours.

Why visit? Scenic driving, villages and photography.

🚌 It's a long day's drive. Many drivers go clockwise to avoid meeting tour coaches on the narrowest stretches, and an overnight stop lets you slow down.
Harbour town · Gaeltacht

🎶 Dingle

Dingle is a colourful harbour town with a lively atmosphere, independent businesses and deep roots in Irish-language culture. It's the perfect base for exploring the peninsula.

Why visit? Harbour towns, food, music and culture.

Scenic drive · Archaeology

🌅 Slea Head Drive

A spectacular loop around the western tip of the Dingle Peninsula, with huge ocean views, the Blasket Islands offshore and ancient sites, including stone beehive huts, along the way.

Why visit? Coastal driving, archaeology and scenery.

🚗 The road is narrow in places. Take it slowly and only pull in where it's safe.
UNESCO · Seasonal boat

⛪ Skellig Michael

Rising sheer out of the Atlantic, Skellig Michael is one of Ireland's most extraordinary places. Early medieval monks built a monastery near its summit, reached by hundreds of ancient stone steps. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Landings are strictly limited, run only by licensed boats in summer, and depend entirely on the weather. Many trips are cancelled at short notice. Boat trips around the islands, without landing, are another option.

Why visit? Archaeology, history and exceptional Atlantic scenery.

⚠️ The 2026 season ended early, on 21 September, because of the weather. Landings resume in the 2027 season. Children under 12 aren't allowed on the island, the steps are steep and uneven, and there are no guarantees: never plan a trip around a landing.
Heritage Ireland ↗
Island · History

📡 Valentia Island

Valentia played a starring role in communications history: the first transatlantic telegraph cables came ashore here in the mid-1800s, linking Europe and North America.

Today you can explore its viewpoints, heritage sites and dramatic coastline. It's reached by bridge from Portmagee or by car ferry from Reenard.

Why visit? History, coastal scenery and unusual attractions.

House · Beach · Seasonal

🏛️ Derrynane House & Beach

Derrynane was the family home of Daniel O'Connell, 'the Liberator', one of the most important figures in Irish history. The house displays relics from his life, including the triumphal chariot given to him by the people of Dublin in 1844.

The parkland and nearby beach make it a lovely mix of history and scenery.

Why visit? Irish history, coastal walks and beaches.

📅 The house opens seasonally, roughly March to early November.
Heritage Ireland ↗
Town

🏘️ Kenmare

An attractive heritage town with colourful streets, independent shops and a relaxed feel. It's well placed for both the Ring of Kerry and the Beara Peninsula.

Why visit? Relaxed town breaks and scenic exploring.

Worth the detour
Hidden gems & unusual places

A mix of free viewpoints, beaches and seasonal attractions, labelled so you can plan around them.

Beach · Free

🏖️ Inch Beach

A sweeping sandy spit stretching out into Dingle Bay, with big skies and mountain views.

Early Christian · Free

⛪ Gallarus Oratory

A remarkable early stone church on the Dingle Peninsula, built from dry stone in the shape of an upturned boat, and still standing after many centuries.

Ruins · Exterior only

🏚️ Minard Castle

Atmospheric castle ruins above a storm beach on the Dingle Peninsula.

⚠️ The ruins are unstable, so admire them from outside only.
Viewpoint · Working pier

📸 Dunquin Pier

That famous zig-zag path down the cliff is one of the most photographed spots on the Dingle Peninsula, and the pier is where ferries leave for the Blasket Islands.

⚠️ It's a working pier, so don't drive past the ticket office, and leave the path clear for ferry passengers. Enjoy the view from the top, and stay back from the unguarded cliff edge.
Seaside town

🏰 Ballybunion

A seaside town in North Kerry with sandy beaches, dramatic cliffs and a ruined castle perched above the strand.

Mountain · Experienced hikers only

🏔️ Carrauntoohil

Ireland's highest mountain, at 1,038 metres, in the MacGillycuddy's Reeks.

⛰️ This is a serious mountain climb, not a walk. Only attempt it with experience, proper equipment, navigation skills and a good forecast, or go with a qualified guide.
Viewpoint · Free

👀 Ladies View

A famous viewpoint on the road between Killarney and Kenmare, named after Queen Victoria's ladies-in-waiting, who loved the view in 1861.

Viewpoint · Free

🛣️ Moll's Gap

A scenic mountain-road junction between Killarney and Kenmare with big views over the surrounding peaks.

Island · Seasonal boat

📜 Innisfallen Island

Historic monastic ruins on an island in Lough Leane, where the Annals of Inisfallen, a medieval chronicle of Irish history, are believed to have been written.

⛴️ You'll need a boat, usually from Ross Castle in season.
Harbour village

⚓ Portmagee

A colourful harbour village and the main departure point for Skellig boat trips.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

We've kept history and legend clearly labelled, because the best Irish stories are usually a bit of both.

History

⛪ The monks of Skellig Michael

Sometime around the 6th to 8th centuries, a small community of monks chose to live on a jagged rock eight miles out in the Atlantic. They built beehive-shaped stone cells and oratories near the summit, terraced gardens, and stairways cut into the rock.

How they survived the storms and isolation is one of the great stories of early Irish Christianity. The monastery was eventually abandoned, but its buildings still stand, astonishingly intact.

Legend

🐴 The legend of O'Donoghue

Ross Castle was the stronghold of the O'Donoghue chieftains, and legend says one of them vanished into the waters of Lough Leane.

According to local folklore, he rises from the lake on a white horse on May morning every seven years, and anyone who catches a glimpse of him will have good luck. It's folklore, of course, but it's a lovely one.

Legend

🦢 The Children of Lir

This famous legend isn't from Kerry, but it belongs to the whole of Ireland. Four children turned into swans by their jealous stepmother spent 900 years on Ireland's waters, from Lough Derravaragh in Westmeath to the Sea of Moyle and finally the wild Atlantic coast.

It's one of the most beautiful and heartbreaking stories in Irish mythology, and we told it in our Friday Night Frights series.

History

🇮🇪 Daniel O'Connell, the Liberator

Born near Cahersiveen and raised at Derrynane, Daniel O'Connell became a lawyer and politician who campaigned for the rights of Irish Catholics. His campaign led to Catholic Emancipation in 1829, earning him the title 'the Liberator'.

He returned to Derrynane almost every summer, and the house remains the best place to learn about his life.

Ready-made plans
Suggested days out

Kerry is big, so each of these focuses on one area.

🦌 Day 1: Killarney highlights

Ideal for: first-time visitors
  1. Morning: Ross Castle on the shore of Lough Leane.
  2. Late morning: Muckross House and Gardens.
  3. Afternoon: Torc Waterfall, then up to Ladies View.

🌅 Day 2: The Dingle Peninsula

Ideal for: coastal scenery and culture
  1. Morning: Dingle town.
  2. Midday: Slea Head Drive.
  3. Afternoon: heritage and coastal stops such as Gallarus Oratory, then back to Dingle.

🛣️ Day 3: The Ring of Kerry

Ideal for: a long scenic driving day
  1. Route: Killarney → Killorglin → Cahersiveen → Waterville → Sneem → Kenmare → Killarney.
  2. Tip: it's a long day of driving with lots of stops. An overnight stay on the Ring lets you explore properly.

⛪ Alternative: Kerry's islands

Ideal for: history and sea air (weather-dependent)
  1. Start: Portmagee.
  2. Midday: a seasonal Skellig boat trip, booked well ahead, if the weather allows.
  3. Afternoon: Valentia Island.
  4. Note: Skellig trips run only in summer and are often cancelled, so have a backup plan.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🗺️ Big county
Distances can be deceptive on narrow, scenic roads. Allow far more time than the map suggests.
🗓️ How long to stay
At least three to five days for a good selection of highlights.
🚗 Getting around
A car is best for rural Kerry, though Killarney and Dingle have public transport and plenty of tours.
⛰️ Mountain safety
Weather in the mountains changes fast. Carrauntoohil and the higher routes need proper experience and gear.
⛴️ Boats
Skellig and Blasket trips are seasonal and weather-dependent. Book ahead and keep a backup plan.
☔ Rainy-day options
Muckross House, Derrynane House and the cafés and pubs of Dingle and Killarney.

Opening times, seasons and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. For general visitor information, see Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Kerry FAQ
How many days do you need in Kerry?
At least three to five. Kerry is big, the roads are narrow and you'll want to stop constantly. Plan for Killarney, Dingle and the Ring of Kerry on separate days.
Can you visit Skellig Michael?
Yes, in summer only, with licensed boat operators, and only when the weather allows. Trips are often cancelled at short notice. The 2026 season ended early, on 21 September, so the next landings will be in 2027. Children under 12 aren't allowed on the island.
Which way should you drive the Ring of Kerry?
Tour coaches usually go anticlockwise, so many independent drivers go clockwise to avoid meeting them on the narrowest stretches. Whichever way you go, allow a full day or stay overnight along the route.
Is Killarney National Park free?
Yes, the park itself is free to explore. Some attractions inside it, like Muckross House, charge admission.
