Tel Aviv Travel Guide —
three hot, wine-filled nights by the Mediterranean

After the intensity of our Holy Land pilgrimage, we spent three nights in Tel Aviv — the first in a hotel and the final two in an Airbnb. It felt like a completely different ending to the trip: Mediterranean heat, beaches, easy public transport, a city that stayed lively well into the evening and, in our case, quite a lot of wine.

⚠️ Travel note: This page describes our experience in 2018. Conditions can change significantly, so always check current official travel advice before making arrangements.

Our stay
From pilgrimage mode to city mode

Tel Aviv was our decompression stop. We had spent days moving through biblical sites, desert landscapes and churches, and suddenly we were beside the Mediterranean with modern cafés, apartment blocks, beaches and a much more relaxed rhythm.

Our first night was in a hotel, then we moved into an Airbnb for the final two nights. That change made the city feel less like another tour stop and more like somewhere we were actually living for a couple of days.

The heat was unreal, the call to prayer was loud, everyone seemed ridiculously fit and good-looking, and we drank a lot of wine. That is basically our Tel Aviv memory in one sentence.

Planning ideas
Things to do in Tel Aviv

The Mediterranean coast

Tel Aviv's long seafront is one of the easiest ways to understand the city. Even if you are not planning a full beach day, the promenade is ideal for walking, people-watching and getting some air after a busy sightseeing itinerary.

Old Jaffa

Jaffa sits immediately south of central Tel Aviv and gives the trip a completely different texture: older stone streets, harbour views and layers of history beside the modern city. It is one of the obvious areas to include if you have a couple of days.

Markets, cafés and neighbourhood wandering

Tel Aviv works well without over-planning. Leave time simply to walk, stop for food or a drink and let the city reveal itself. That was the part we enjoyed most after a very structured pilgrimage itinerary.

Evenings

We remember the evenings as warm, sociable and easygoing. Tel Aviv has a reputation for nightlife, but you do not need clubs to enjoy it — sitting outside with a glass of wine was enough for us.

Common questions
Tel Aviv FAQ
How many nights did we spend in Tel Aviv?

Three nights in total: one night in a hotel and the final two in an Airbnb.

Was three nights enough?

For the role Tel Aviv played in our trip, yes. It gave us time to enjoy the coast and city atmosphere without rushing before we flew home.

Did we find public transport easy?

Yes. We found it useful and straightforward during our stay, although travellers should check the current system before visiting.

What do we remember most?

The heat, the Mediterranean light, the very noticeable call to prayer from where we were staying, how active and outdoorsy everyone seemed — and a fair amount of wine.

Would we add Tel Aviv to a Holy Land itinerary?

Yes. For us it was a very effective contrast to the pilgrimage part of the journey and gave the trip a more relaxed ending.
