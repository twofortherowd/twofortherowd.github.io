Longford is one of those counties that tends to get overlooked when people plan an Irish trip, and that's a shame.

From an Iron Age wooden road preserved in the bog to a cathedral rebuilt from the ashes, historic villages and lovely riverside landscapes, it's full of surprises, with literary connections and family attractions too.

You won't have to fight through crowds to enjoy any of it either!

Ancient roads, cathedrals & Norman mottes
Iron Age · Seasonal

🪵 Corlea Trackway Visitor Centre

Near Kenagh, an oak road built across the bog in 148 BC, preserved for over 2,000 years. A stretch of the original timbers is displayed inside the visitor centre.

Why visit? Archaeology, history and engineering.

📅 Open seasonally. Check Heritage Ireland before you go.
Cathedral

⛪ St Mel's Cathedral

Longford's cathedral was gutted by fire on Christmas morning 2009 and painstakingly restored, reopening at Christmas 2014. The result is stunning.

Why visit? Architecture, history and heritage.

Heritage centre

⚔️ Knights & Conquests Heritage Centre, Granard

Exhibitions on the Norman story of the area, with events throughout the year.

Why visit? Medieval history and families.

Norman earthwork

🏰 Granard Motte

A huge Norman motte and bailey with wide views across the county.

Why visit? History and landscapes.

Heritage village

🏘️ Ardagh

A neat 19th-century estate village. Oliver Goldsmith is said to have mistaken Ardagh House for an inn, an embarrassment he reworked into She Stoops to Conquer.

Why visit? Heritage and village wandering.

Literary centre

📚 Maria Edgeworth Centre, Edgeworthstown

Celebrating the novelist Maria Edgeworth and her remarkable family.

Why visit? Literature and history.

Waterways, woods & family breaks
Greenway

🚲 Royal Canal Greenway

A long, flat walking and cycling route along the canal through Longford's countryside, ending at the Shannon.

Why visit? Cycling, walking and nature.

Canal village

⚓ Clondra & Richmond Harbour

A pretty waterside village where the Royal Canal meets the Shannon.

Why visit? Canals, boating and peaceful scenery.

Lake

🌊 Lough Ree shoreline

Quiet stretches of shore on the Shannon's big lake, with views and angling.

Why visit? Nature and waterways.

Woodland

🌲 Derrycassin Wood

Woodland walks beside Lough Gowna in the north of the county.

Why visit? Walking and photography.

Family resort

A big woodland holiday village near Ballymahon with indoor and outdoor activities.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Archaeology

🪵 The road across the bog

Tree-ring dating shows the Corlea oaks were felled in 148 BC. The road was a huge undertaking, possibly ceremonial, and it seems to have sunk into the bog within about a decade of being built.

History

⚔️ Ballinamuck, 1798

In September 1798, a French force under General Humbert, joined by Irish rebels, was defeated at Ballinamuck. The French were treated as prisoners of war, but many Irish rebels were executed.

History

🏰 The Normans at Granard

The great motte at Granard was raised by the Normans in the late 12th century, a statement of power over the surrounding land.

Literature

📚 Maria Edgeworth

Maria Edgeworth, one of the most celebrated novelists of her day, lived and wrote at Edgeworthstown. Her novel Castle Rackrent (1800) is often called the first historical novel.

Common questions
County Longford FAQ
How old is the Corlea Trackway?
The timbers were felled in 148 BC, making it more than 2,000 years old.
What happened to St Mel's Cathedral?
It was badly damaged by fire on Christmas morning 2009 and reopened after a major restoration at Christmas 2014.
Is Longford good for a family break?
Yes. Center Parcs Longford Forest is the big draw, and the Royal Canal Greenway is great for family cycling.
