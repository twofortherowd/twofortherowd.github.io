County Antrim —
Giant Legends, Coastal Castles & Incredible Adventures

Where ancient giants supposedly built roads, castles cling to cliffs and every coastal bend reveals another spectacular view.

If you're looking for dramatic scenery, fascinating history and a few stories that sound almost too ridiculous to be true, County Antrim is going to be right up your street!

From the extraordinary stones of the Giant's Causeway to the Glens of Antrim, this corner of Ireland really knows how to put on a show: medieval castles, colourful harbour villages, magnificent coastal walks and some of the best-known Game of Thrones locations.

And that's before we get to Belfast, a city packed with history, culture, food and character. Whether you're following the Causeway Coastal Route or chasing legends, you'll want the camera charged!

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Antrim, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🛂 Crossing from the Republic?

There are no border checks between the Republic and Northern Ireland, but a few things change: prices are in pounds sterling (£), speed limits are in miles per hour, and it's worth checking your phone plan and car insurance cover before you go.

The Causeway Coast
UNESCO · National Trust

🪨 Giant's Causeway

Around 40,000 interlocking basalt columns, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Ireland's most famous natural wonders.

Geologists put it down to ancient volcanic activity. Irish folklore blames Fionn mac Cumhaill and a very awkward encounter with a Scottish giant (more on that below).

Why visit? Natural wonders, geology, photography and folklore.

ℹ️ Walking to the stones is separate from the paid visitor-centre experience and parking. Check the National Trust's current arrangements before you go.
⚠️ The stones are often wet and slippery, and waves can sweep across the lower rocks.
National Trust ↗
Book ahead · Heights

🌉 Carrick-a-Rede Rope Bridge

A rope bridge strung 30 metres above the sea to a tiny island once used by salmon fishermen. The views along the coast are spectacular, and it's definitely not one for anyone who hates heights!

Why visit? Adventure, coastal scenery and photography.

🎟️ Pre-booking is essential, even for National Trust members. Crossings can be affected by bad weather, and the route has steep slopes and steps.
National Trust ↗
Castle · Clifftop

🏰 Dunluce Castle

One of Ireland's most atmospheric ruins, clinging to a rocky headland near Bushmills. Its story involves the MacQuillans, the MacDonnells, sieges and a kitchen said to have fallen into the sea.

Why visit? Castles, history and dramatic coastal views.

Harbour · Film location

⚓ Ballintoy Harbour

A tiny harbour at the bottom of a twisting road, with rugged rocks and sea stacks. Game of Thrones fans will know it as the Iron Islands.

Why visit? Photography and filming locations.

Village · Whiskey

🥃 Bushmills

A historic village best known for the Old Bushmills Distillery, which can be toured depending on current arrangements.

Why visit? Village character and adults interested in distilling.

Island · Ferry

🐦 Rathlin Island

Reached by ferry from Ballycastle, Rathlin has cliffs, wildlife and maritime history. The RSPB West Light Seabird Centre is the place to see the seabird colonies in season.

Why visit? Wildlife, island adventures and coastal walks.

⛴️ Ferries depend on the weather. Puffins are seasonal (roughly late spring to mid-summer), not guaranteed.

Beyond the Causeway
The Glens, castles & cliff walks
Forest park · Waterfalls

💧 Glenariff Forest Park

The 'Queen of the Glens', with woodland trails, waterfalls and big views. The waterfall walk is at its best after rain.

Why visit? Walking, waterfalls and nature.

⚠️ Paths and boardwalks can be slippery after rain.
Villages · Glens

🏡 Cushendun & Cushendall

Two lovely coastal villages in the Glens. Cushendun has distinctive Cornish-style cottages and caves that appeared in Game of Thrones, while Cushendall sits in beautiful countryside with a strong cultural heritage.

Why visit? Scenic drives and relaxed coastal breaks.

Avenue · Film location

🌳 The Dark Hedges

An avenue of intertwined beech trees near Armoy, made famous by Game of Thrones. Atmospheric on a misty morning.

The trees are over 200 years old, and storms have brought several down in recent years, so the avenue has gaps now.

Why visit? Photography and film locations.

🚫 The road is closed to traffic. Use the designated parking and walk in, and don't drive along the avenue.
Castle · Belfast Lough

🏰 Carrickfergus Castle

One of the best-preserved medieval castles in Ireland, begun by John de Courcy in the late 12th century and standing guard over Belfast Lough.

Why visit? Medieval history and family visits.

Guided cliff walk

🧗 The Gobbins

A guided walk along bridges, tunnels and paths built into the basalt cliffs at Islandmagee. One of the most unusual outdoor experiences in Ireland.

Why visit? Adventure and spectacular coastal scenery.

⚠️ This is a demanding guided walk with fitness, footwear and weather requirements, and it's often cancelled in bad weather. Check it's running and book through the official website.
Seaside · Lighthouse

💡 Whitehead & Blackhead Lighthouse

A colourful seaside town with a relaxed feel. The Blackhead Path leads along the shore to the lighthouse on the headland.

Why visit? Seaside walks and a quieter coastal escape.

The city
Belfast highlights

Belfast spans counties Antrim and Down. These are the big hitters for a first visit.

Museum · Book ahead

🚢 Titanic Belfast

The story of RMS Titanic, built in Belfast, told through interactive galleries on the site where she was designed and launched.

Strictly speaking, the Titanic Quarter sits on the County Down side of the Lagan, but it's an essential part of any Belfast visit.

Why visit? Maritime history, engineering and families.

🎟️ Book timed entry in advance and allow several hours.
Titanic Belfast ↗
City centre

🏛️ Belfast City Hall & the Cathedral Quarter

Start at the grand City Hall, then wander up to the Cathedral Quarter's cobbled streets, street art, pubs and music.

Why visit? Architecture, food and nightlife.

Museum · Free

🖼️ Ulster Museum & Botanic Gardens

Free to visit, with everything from Egyptian mummies to Armada treasure and the story of the Troubles. The Botanic Gardens and their Victorian Palm House are next door.

Why visit? Rainy days and families.

Views · Walking

⛰️ Belfast Castle & Cave Hill

Belfast Castle stands on the slopes of Cave Hill, with views over the city and the lough. Walking routes climb to Napoleon's Nose, the rocky profile said to have inspired Gulliver's Travels.

Why visit? City views, walking and photography.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Legend

🪨 Finn McCool and the Giant's Causeway

Fionn mac Cumhaill built a stone causeway across the sea to fight the Scottish giant Benandonner. Then he saw how enormous his rival was.

His wife Oonagh dressed Fionn as a baby and tucked him into a cradle. When Benandonner saw the size of the 'baby', he fled back to Scotland, ripping up the causeway behind him. Fingal's Cave on Staffa is said to be the other end.

Legend & history

👻 The kitchen that fell into the sea

The most famous Dunluce story says that during a storm part of the kitchen collapsed into the sea, taking servants with it. It's a great tale, but historians aren't convinced it happened that way.

Tradition

🐑 St Patrick and Slemish

Tradition says Patrick was brought to Ireland as a slave and spent six years tending animals on Slemish before escaping. He later came back as a missionary.

Folklore

🧚 The Nine Glens of Antrim

Each of the Glens has its own landscape and stories, from fairy thorns to the ghost of Loughareema's drowned coach and horses.

History

🚢 Titanic and industrial Belfast

Harland and Wolff's shipyard made Belfast one of the great industrial cities of the world. Titanic, launched there in 1911, sank on her maiden voyage in April 1912.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚗 Getting around
A car is ideal for the Causeway Coast and Glens. Belfast has good public transport, with trains and buses to several coastal towns.
🗓️ Best time
May to September for long days and outdoor attractions.
⏳ How long
Three to five days for a good introduction.
💷 Currency
Pounds sterling. Speed limits are in miles per hour.
🎟️ Booking
Book Titanic Belfast, Carrick-a-Rede and The Gobbins ahead.
⚠️ Safety
Strong winds can close rope bridges, ferries and cliff paths, and coastal paths are exposed.

Opening times, tours and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Discover Northern Ireland, National Trust, Titanic Belfast and The Gobbins.

Common questions
County Antrim FAQ
Do you have to pay to see the Giant's Causeway?
Walking to the stones and the coast is separate from the paid visitor centre and parking. Check the National Trust's current arrangements before you go.
Do I need to book Carrick-a-Rede?
Yes. The National Trust says pre-booking is essential for the rope bridge crossing, even for members, and crossings can be affected by bad weather.
Is Belfast in County Antrim?
Mostly. Belfast spans counties Antrim and Down, with the River Lagan roughly marking the line. The city centre, Cave Hill and the Cathedral Quarter are on the Antrim side, while the Titanic Quarter is technically on the Down side.
Can you drive down the Dark Hedges?
No. The road has been closed to traffic since 2017. Park in the designated area and walk.
What currency do they use in Antrim?
Pounds sterling (£). Card payments are widely accepted, and speed limits are in miles per hour.
