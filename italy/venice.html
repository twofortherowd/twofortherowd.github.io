Venice twice, thirteen years apart

Nicola first visited Venice with friends in February 2012. Thirteen years later, she returned with Podge to celebrate his birthday in February 2025. The companions and occasions were different, but Venice remained every bit as extraordinary: canals instead of roads, palaces rising from the water and famous views appearing around almost every corner.

Two trips, two completely different occasions

Returning somewhere special after more than a decade lets you see both the city and your own travels differently.

Venice with friends — 2012

Nicola explored the classic sights and canals with friends in February 2012. It was a lively friends' trip, with plenty of wandering and the excitement of seeing a city that looks unlike anywhere else. Venice also became the starting point for a memorable day trip to Verona.

Podge's birthday — 2025

Nicola returned in February 2025, this time with Podge to celebrate his birthday. Thirteen years had passed, but the thrill of stepping out beside the lagoon and seeing Venice rise from the water had not worn off.

The second trip had a completely different rhythm. There was the pleasure of recognising places Nicola had seen before, but also the fun of watching Podge experience them for the first time. A birthday city break gave us the perfect excuse to slow down, enjoy the views and let the city itself be the main event.

Venice can look almost too familiar in photographs, but being there is different. You hear water instead of traffic, deliveries arrive by boat and even the most ordinary journey involves bridges, narrow lanes and another beautiful canal.

The Venice experiences everyone should do once

Some cities reward avoiding the obvious. In Venice, the obvious sights are famous because they really are magnificent—although getting lost between them is half the pleasure.

🦁

St Mark's Square

Venice's grand public space, surrounded by arcades and some of the city's most important buildings.

⛪

St Mark's Basilica

A dazzling mixture of domes, mosaics and centuries of Venetian power and history.

🏛️

Doge's Palace

The ornate former seat of Venice's government, with grand rooms, courtyards and prison passages.

🌉

Rialto Bridge

The most famous crossing of the Grand Canal and one of the classic Venice views.

🚤

Grand Canal

Travel it by vaporetto, water taxi or gondola to see Venice's palaces from their intended front doors.

💭

Wander without a plan

Leave the busiest route and follow smaller canals, quiet squares and bridges just to see where they lead.

Seeing Venice from the water

In most cities, a boat trip is an optional extra. In Venice, the water is the transport system, the main street and one of the greatest attractions all at once.

🚤 The vaporetto

The public water bus is the practical way to move around, but it also delivers one of the best-value sightseeing journeys in the city. A trip along the Grand Canal reveals façades designed to impress visitors arriving by water.

🛶 The gondolas

They are expensive and unapologetically touristy—but they are also uniquely Venetian. Even if you decide not to take one, watching gondoliers guide their boats through impossibly tight turns is part of the atmosphere.

🌉 Bridges everywhere

Every route involves steps, bridges and detours. The Bridge of Sighs and Rialto are the famous ones, but dozens of tiny neighbourhood crossings may end up in your favourite photographs.

🌅 Winter light

February gave the lagoon a softer, moodier character. Sunsets reflected across the water and the city looked especially atmospheric as daylight disappeared.

Venice does not always make getting from A to B easy—but it makes the journey far more interesting.

Eating, drinking and celebrating in Venice

Venice can be expensive, particularly beside St Mark's Square, so a little wandering pays off at mealtimes too. Step back from the busiest routes and look for smaller bacari—traditional Venetian bars serving drinks and cicchetti, the city's answer to little snacks or tapas.

For Podge's birthday trip, the setting did much of the work. Even an ordinary drink feels like an occasion when you have spent the day crossing canals and the evening light is falling over the lagoon. Our advice is to choose one meal or drink to make special, then balance it with simpler stops away from the most famous squares.

🍷
Try cicchetti

Small bites let you sample several Venetian flavours without committing to another enormous restaurant meal.

☕
Check prices before sitting down

A drink at a famous square may include the location in the price. Sometimes the splurge is worth it; just make it a conscious one.

🌾
Ask clearly about gluten-free preparation

Italy understands coeliac disease well, but always confirm cross-contamination procedures rather than assuming every gluten-free option is safe.

🎂
Give a birthday trip breathing room

Venice is much nicer when the celebration is not buried beneath a frantic list of timed attractions.

Venice is at its best between the landmarks

The basilica, palace and Rialto Bridge deserve their fame, but Venice's real magic often arrives unexpectedly: laundry above a narrow canal, footsteps echoing through a quiet lane or a tiny bridge with nobody else on it.

Both trips included the usual major sights, but simply wandering the streets—and occasionally having no idea where you were—was part of the experience.

Venice travel FAQ

Is Venice worth visiting in February?

Yes. It is cool and can be damp, but winter light and fewer visitors can make the city extremely atmospheric. Check the year's Carnival dates because they change annually.

How many days do you need in Venice?

Two to three full days suit a first visit, giving you time for the major sights and unhurried wandering.

Can you walk everywhere?

The historic centre is made for walking, although the maze of canals means routes are rarely straight. Vaporetti are useful for longer journeys and for seeing the Grand Canal from the water.

Is a gondola ride essential?

It is an iconic Venice experience, but it can be expensive. A vaporetto journey along the Grand Canal still gives wonderful water-level views for much less.

Should you book St Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace ahead?

Advance booking is sensible, particularly during weekends, Carnival and the warmer months.
