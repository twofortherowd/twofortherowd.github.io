Two visits, fourteen years apart

Rome was part of Nicola's travels long before Two for the Row'd began. Returning in 2019 meant seeing the Eternal City again with older—and probably slightly more appreciative—eyes.

Rome in 2005

Nicola's first encounter with the Italian capital included all the major Roman sights and an extraordinary visit to Vatican City during the final weeks of Pope John Paul II's life.

Rome in 2019

Fourteen years later, Nicola returned for another round of Rome's monuments, piazzas and tours—and confirmed that this is a city worth seeing more than once.

The classic Rome sights

Yes, they are the places everyone visits—and there is a reason for that. Rome's headline attractions genuinely live up to their fame.

🏟️

Colosseum

Rome's unmistakable icon and the essential starting point for understanding the scale of the ancient city.

🏛️

Roman Forum

Temples, arches and ruins at what was once the political and social heart of the Roman Empire.

⛲

Trevi Fountain

Crowded, extravagant and completely unmissable. Throwing in a coin supposedly guarantees a return to Rome.

⭕

The Pantheon

An astonishingly preserved Roman building with an extraordinary dome and almost two thousand years of history.

🪜

Spanish Steps

One of Rome's great meeting places, linking Piazza di Spagna with the church of Trinità dei Monti.

🎨

Piazza Navona

A beautiful baroque square built over an ancient stadium and filled with fountains, cafés and street life.

The Vatican belongs to another country—and another story

It is physically surrounded by Rome, but Vatican City is the world's smallest independent state. Nicola's 2005 visit became far more than an ordinary sightseeing stop: she witnessed one of Pope John Paul II's final public appearances shortly before his death.

We have kept that remarkable memory on its own Vatican City page, along with St Peter's Basilica, St Peter's Square and the wider Vatican experience.

Rome travel FAQ

Is Rome worth visiting?

Absolutely. Its combination of ancient ruins, huge churches, theatrical fountains, lively piazzas and brilliant food is unlike anywhere else.

How many days do you need in Rome?

Allow at least three to four full days for the major sights. A longer trip makes it easier to slow down and enjoy the neighbourhoods between the monuments.

Can you walk between Rome's main attractions?

Many central sights are walkable, but Rome is larger than it first appears. Comfortable shoes and occasional use of public transport make a considerable difference.

Should you book the Colosseum and Vatican Museums ahead?

Yes. They are among Europe's busiest attractions, so timed tickets or organised tours are well worth arranging in advance.

Is Vatican City part of Rome?

Vatican City is surrounded by Rome but is an independent country. It is easy to combine with a Rome trip, and we cover it on a separate destination page.
