Tunisia —
beaches, ancient history and an unforgettable Sahara adventure

We visited Tunisia in 2009 and it remains one of the most magical trips we have ever done. Two completely different sides to one incredible journey — first, time on the Mediterranean coast with beach days and wandering through ancient souks in the cool of the evening. Then, a journey south to another world entirely: the Roman amphitheatre at El Jem, traditional troglodyte dwellings, dramatic desert landscapes, and an overnight excursion into the Sahara with camels and stars. Tunisia is beautiful, genuinely welcoming and brilliant value for money. We recommend it without hesitation.

✈️ Before you go

Tunisia practical information

  • Entry requirements. Irish citizens need a valid passport. Most EU/EEA nationals do not need a visa for tourism stays up to 90 days. Requirements can change, so check the latest information with your government and with official Tunisian travel documentation before booking.
  • Flights. Flight routes between Ireland and Tunisia can be seasonal and change over time. Check current flight options for your travel dates with major airlines or travel comparison sites.
  • Best time to visit. Spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are ideal — warm and sunny without peak summer heat or winter rain. The Sahara in particular is more pleasant in these shoulder seasons.
  • Currency. Tunisian Dinar (TND). ATMs are widespread. Credit cards accepted in larger establishments. Always check current exchange rates before travelling.
  • Language. Arabic is the official language. French is widely spoken in tourism and hospitality. English is less common away from tourist areas.
  • Packing. Lightweight, breathable clothing for heat. A wide-brimmed hat, strong sunscreen and insect repellent are essential, particularly for a Sahara trip. Covered clothing for visits to medinas and mosques shows respect.
  • Value for money. Tunisia is exceptionally good value — food, accommodation, activities and souvenirs are all reasonably priced compared to Mediterranean Europe.
  • Safety. As with any destination, check your government's current travel advice before booking.

Common questions

Tunisia FAQ

Is Tunisia worth visiting?
Absolutely — Tunisia is one of the most accessible and rewarding North African destinations. Beautiful Mediterranean beaches, fascinating ancient history, vibrant souks, incredible food and the extraordinary Sahara Desert all within easy reach. It is brilliant value and the people are genuinely warm and welcoming.
What is a Sahara Desert trip from Tunisia like?
An overnight Sahara Desert trip from Tunisia is one of the most magical travel experiences you can have. Riding a camel into the dunes at sunset, watching the colours shift across the sky, sleeping under a blanket of stars and then waking for a sunrise camel ride as the desert comes back to life — it is genuinely unlike anything else.
What are the souks in Tunisia like?
The souks are a labyrinthine maze of covered streets filled with spices, ceramics, leather, textiles, jewellery and the sounds and smells of daily Tunisian life. They are best explored in the cool of the evening and are an absolute must.
Is Tunisia good value for money?
Yes — Tunisia is excellent value compared to most Mediterranean and North African destinations. Food, accommodation, activities and souvenirs are all very reasonably priced, which makes it a brilliant choice for a holiday that delivers a huge amount without breaking the bank.
Is Tunisia safe to visit?
Tunisia is generally a safe and welcoming destination for tourists. The Tunisian people are known for their warmth and hospitality. As with any destination, it is worth checking your government's current travel advice before booking, but Tunisia has a long and proud tradition of welcoming visitors from around the world.
What is the best time to visit Tunisia?
Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are ideal for most of Tunisia — warm and sunny without the peak summer heat. If you are planning a Sahara Desert trip, spring and autumn are particularly pleasant. Summer beach holidays work well on the coast but the desert can be extremely hot.
