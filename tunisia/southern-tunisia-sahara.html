From El Jem to the Sahara —
our Southern Tunisia adventure

The second part of our 2009 Tunisia trip took us on a completely different journey — leaving the coast behind for an organised overnight excursion into Southern Tunisia. We visited the Roman amphitheatre at El Jem, passed through Douz, stopped at traditional troglodyte dwellings, and spent an unforgettable night in the Sahara with camel rides at sunset and sunrise. This is the part of our Tunisia holiday that stayed with us most vividly.

🏜️ Our Southern Tunisia Story

El Jem, Douz, troglodyte homes and the desert

Journey south into a different world

"The contrast stayed with us most — leaving the coast behind for Roman history, dry southern landscapes and then the Sahara itself."

El Jem — the Roman amphitheatre

El Jem was our first major stop heading south, and the scale of its Roman amphitheatre took us by surprise. Standing in that space, looking up at structures that have stood for 2,000 years, marked a real shift from the Mediterranean coast we'd left behind. It was the perfect way to begin the southern part of the trip.

The road south and changing landscapes

As the journey continued, the scenery shifted. The landscape became progressively drier and more dramatic — rocky southern terrain, 4x4 roads, and a real sense of moving into a completely different Tunisia. The photographs we still have from this part of the trip capture that transition.

Troglodyte homes and tea

We stopped at traditional troglodyte dwellings, and we still remember sitting down for tea in someone's home. It's a small moment that has stayed with us — the coolness of the underground space after the heat of the afternoon, the hospitality. These are the memories that don't fade.

Douz

Douz stands out in our memories as another point on the southern journey. It's often described as a gateway to the Sahara, and that sense of crossing a threshold into the desert proper is something we remember clearly.

Camels at sunset

The camel ride as the sun went down over the Sahara dunes is one of the defining moments of our entire Tunisia trip. Riding deeper into the dunes, watching the light change, feeling the space open up around us — it's one of those travel experiences that simply doesn't compare to anything else. The colours as the sun approached the horizon are still vivid in our memory.

Overnight in the desert

We slept under Sahara skies, surrounded by stars in a way you simply don't experience in most places. The silence and the scale of the desert at night was genuinely profound.

Sunrise the next morning

The following morning brought a sunrise camel ride as the desert came back to life. If the sunset was beautiful, the sunrise was something else entirely — and it remains one of our favourite memories from the entire trip.

Common questions

Southern Tunisia & Sahara FAQ

What was your Southern Tunisia excursion like?
Extraordinary. We visited El Jem and Douz, stopped at troglodyte homes, travelled through increasingly dramatic desert landscape, and experienced an overnight in the Sahara with camel rides at sunset and sunrise. It was completely different from the coastal part of the trip.
Did you ride camels?
Yes — both at sunset when we rode into the Sahara dunes and at sunrise the following morning as the desert came back to life. Both rides are among the defining memories of our trip.
What was the overnight desert experience like?
Magical. Sleeping under a Sahara sky full of stars, surrounded by the silence and scale of the desert. The sunrise the following morning was as memorable as the sunset before.
Did you visit El Jem?
Yes — the Roman amphitheatre is extraordinary and one of the clearest memories from the southern part of our trip. Standing inside such a vast surviving Roman structure is genuinely striking.
Did you visit traditional troglodyte homes?
Yes — we visited and remember being welcomed into someone's home for tea. It's one of our clearest memories from the journey and a genuine connection with local life.
Is this excursion suitable for everyone?
Generally yes, but it involves camel riding (which requires reasonable fitness), long drives in 4x4 vehicles, and sleeping in desert conditions. Check with tour operators about accessibility and any specific requirements you may have.
