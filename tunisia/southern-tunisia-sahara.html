From El Jem to the Sahara —
our Southern Tunisia adventure

The most unforgettable part of our 2009 Tunisia holiday began when we left the coast on an organised overnight excursion into the south. We stood inside the vast Roman amphitheatre at El Jem, travelled through increasingly stark desert landscapes, visited traditional troglodyte dwellings and sat for tea in someone's home before heading farther into the desert for camels, sunset, an overnight stay and sunrise the next morning.

🏛️ The journey south

Our first-hand 2009 memories

El Jem — a Roman giant in Tunisia

One stop we can identify with absolute certainty is El Jem — we have the photos to prove that one!

Our excursion included a visit to the Roman amphitheatre at El Jem. Even all these years later, the scale of it is one of the clearest memories of the journey south: huge stone arches, open sky and the feeling of standing inside a monument that seemed almost impossibly large.

El Jem is one of the places from this old trip that we can confidently put back on the map. We don't have the original 2009 itinerary anymore, so elsewhere on this page we deliberately distinguish between what we remember for certain and what is only likely.

Through Southern Tunisia

As we travelled farther south, the scenery changed dramatically. The Mediterranean holiday landscape gave way to dry, rocky country and long views across a much harsher landscape. Our old photographs capture some of that journey, including time travelling by 4x4.

We can't reconstruct every road or town seventeen years later, and we're not going to pretend we can. What we can reconstruct is the sequence of experiences that stayed with us.

Troglodyte dwellings — and tea in someone's home

One of the most personal memories from the excursion is visiting traditional troglodyte dwellings. We remember stopping at someone's home and sitting down for tea — one of those small moments that has stayed in our heads long after the exact route disappeared from memory.

Matmata is the likely location for this stop, because it is famous for its traditional underground dwellings and is commonly included on southern Tunisia circuits. But without our original booking documents, we won't turn “very likely” into “definitely”.

We remember the home and the tea. We don't remember the family's name, exactly what was served with it or the precise itinerary — so we won't invent any of that.
🐪 Into the desert

Camels, sunset and the Sahara

Then came the part that really lodged itself in our memories: the desert. We joined the camel experience as the light began to soften, with the landscape stretching out around us and the sun dropping towards the horizon.

The sunset camel ride was one of the defining moments of the whole Tunisia trip. Our photographs still capture the silhouettes and warm desert light far better than our seventeen-year-old memory can supply every logistical detail.

Overnight in the desert

Our southern excursion was an overnight one. We stayed in the desert area and experienced the change from sunset into night before waking for the following morning's desert experience.

We don't remember enough to name the exact camp or accommodation, so we deliberately haven't. The memory that matters is the experience itself: being far from the coastal resort, surrounded by a completely different Tunisia.

Sunrise — and one last camel experience

The next morning brought sunrise and another camel experience before the journey continued. Sunset and sunrise bookended the desert part of the excursion, and together they remain among the strongest images we have of Tunisia in 2009.

We also know for certain that we were in Douz. That puts another firm place back onto our 2009 route and helps reconnect the desert memories with the journey through southern Tunisia.

❤️ Looking back

Would we do it again?

Absolutely. The contrast is what made it: Roman history at El Jem, the troglodyte-home visit, the increasingly dramatic southern landscape and then the desert itself. It turned what could have been simply a beach holiday into the part of Tunisia we still remember most vividly.

And that's why we're happy to leave a few blanks in the story. We'd rather tell you what genuinely stayed with us than manufacture a perfect itinerary seventeen years later.
