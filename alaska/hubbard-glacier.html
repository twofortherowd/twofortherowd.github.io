👀 What the Glacier Day Actually Feels Like

One of those days where everyone ends up on deck

Hubbard Glacier wasn't a normal port day at all. There was no getting off the ship, no coach waiting at the pier and no list of attractions to tick off. Instead, the ship itself became the viewing platform as we sailed into the glacier area.

The closer we got, the more impressive the landscape became — mountains surrounding the ship, floating ice on the water and that extraordinary blue colour in the glacier itself. What surprised us most was the scale. You can take dozens of photographs, but it's very difficult to show just how enormous everything feels when you're actually standing there looking at it.

Because we were onboard, we could wander outside for photographs, head back indoors to warm up, grab a coffee and then go back out again. For us — particularly with the heads we had that morning! — that made it a very civilised way to experience one of Alaska's biggest natural sights.

📸 Our Hubbard Glacier Viewing Tips
🧥
Layer Up

Even if the rest of your Alaska cruise feels mild, standing outside watching a glacier can get cold. Layers mean you can stay on deck longer.

📷
Take the Photo — Then Look

Get your photographs, but don't spend the entire approach behind a screen. The sheer scale is the bit your camera won't capture properly.

☕
Use the Ship

One advantage of scenic cruising is being able to warm up indoors, grab a drink and head back outside whenever you want.

🔭
Keep Watching

The view changes as the ship moves and turns, so don't assume the first viewpoint you find will be the only one worth seeing.

🧊 Why Hubbard Glacier Is So Memorable

It's the scale more than anything

For us, Hubbard Glacier wasn't about doing an activity. It was about simply being there and seeing a landscape that felt completely different from anything we'd experienced elsewhere on the trip.

There's something wonderfully strange about having a major cruise day where the itinerary is essentially: go outside and look at this enormous glacier. No ticket queue, no transfer and no rushing back to the ship — just Alaska doing the entertaining.

It was one of the quietest days of the cruise in terms of what we actually did — but one of the biggest in terms of what we saw.
🚢 How Hubbard Glacier Fits the Cruise

A scenic day rather than a shore day

Hubbard Glacier sat between Icy Strait Point and Juneau on our itinerary. There was no rushing to a meeting point or choosing a shore excursion: we could watch from the ship, warm up inside and head back out whenever we wanted.

❓ Good to Know

Hubbard Glacier FAQ

Do you get off the ship at Hubbard Glacier?
We did not. On our itinerary Hubbard Glacier was a scenic cruising experience viewed from the ship.
How long did our ship spend at Hubbard Glacier?
Our itinerary listed scenic cruising from 9:30 am to 2:30 pm.
Do you need a shore excursion for Hubbard Glacier?
Not for the experience we had. The glacier was the scenic highlight of the ship's route that day.
What should you wear on deck?
We would dress in warm layers and have a waterproof outer layer so you can stay outside comfortably while watching the glacier.
Was Hubbard Glacier worth it?
Yes. The huge scale, blue ice and mountain setting made it one of the major scenic moments of the cruise.
