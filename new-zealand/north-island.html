The beginning
Auckland & Coromandel Peninsula

Landing in Auckland — the start of an extraordinary adventure

We had one night in Auckland — just enough to get our bearings in New Zealand's largest city before the tour began. Auckland sits on a narrow isthmus between two harbours and the skyline, dominated by the Sky Tower, is genuinely impressive.

But it was Coromandel that delivered the first wow moment of the tour. The drive up the peninsula is stunning but it's Cathedral Cove that stops you in your tracks — a magnificent natural rock arch opening onto a pristine beach that looks like it belongs on a screensaver. There's a bit of a hike down to reach it but every step is worth it. One of the most beautiful spots in New Zealand.

Geothermal & culture
Rotorua

The heart of Māori culture

Rotorua is extraordinary — geothermal geysers, bubbling mud pools, and most importantly, the Māori cultural evening that included a traditional hāngī meal and a haka performance that genuinely moved us to tears.

"The haka was extraordinary. You know it's coming, you've seen it a thousand times on TV — and it still absolutely floored us. The power of it, the emotion, the connection to something ancient and proud. We were genuinely moved."

This experience deserves its own dedicated page where we've written about it in full detail.

Other North Island stops
Taupō & Wellington

Taupō — Adventure on the lake

Taupō sits on New Zealand's largest lake and is the adventure capital of the North Island. The Taupō Rapids Jet boat experience screams through narrow river canyons at full speed, complete with 360 spins on the water — pure adrenaline and absolutely mad craic. It's one of those experiences where you spend the whole time laughing and screaming simultaneously.

Wellington — Capital with character

Wellington is New Zealand's capital city and sits at the southern tip of the North Island. The capital has something genuinely moving about it — the architecture, the port setting, the creative energy. It's a brief stop on the tour but an important one as the jumping-off point to the South Island via ferry.

Questions answered
North Island FAQ
Is Hobbiton worth visiting if I'm not a Lord of the Rings fan?
Yes — even if you've never watched a single film, Hobbiton is worth doing. The attention to detail is extraordinary — every hobbit hole, every garden, every detail of Bag End is maintained exactly as it appeared on screen. The Waikato hills rolling away in every direction provide a backdrop that feels genuinely Middle-earth. The guided tour brings it all to life in a way that just wandering around independently never could.
What is the glowworm cave experience really like?
Floating through a vast underground cave in total silence, looking up at a ceiling covered in thousands of tiny glowworms creating their own galaxy above you — it is genuinely one of the most extraordinary natural experiences we have ever had. The silence, the darkness and the sheer otherworldliness of it makes Waitomo completely unlike anything else. If you want the full adrenaline version, black water rafting takes you through the cave on an inflated tube.
Should I do a Māori cultural experience in Rotorua?
Without hesitation, yes. Learning about Māori culture, history and traditions from the people themselves gave the whole evening a depth and authenticity that made it one of the most memorable nights of our entire time in New Zealand. The hāngī meal and haka performance are experiences you'll remember for the rest of your life.
