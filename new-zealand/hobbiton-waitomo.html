Hobbiton & Waitomo —
glowworms & Middle-earth

These are two of the most extraordinary experiences on New Zealand's North Island. Hobbiton is the world of Middle-earth brought to life on a farm in the Waikato, and Waitomo is a glowworm cave where thousands of tiny lights create their own galaxy above you. Both are genuinely unforgettable.

01
Hobbiton Movie Set Tour

A real place that feels like Middle-earth

Hobbiton is a film set created for The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit films, and it is still standing — maintained exactly as it appeared in the movies — on a farm in Matamata in the Waikato region of New Zealand's North Island.

The attention to detail is extraordinary. Bag End — Bilbo's hobbit hole — is there exactly as you remember it from the films. The Green Dragon pub, the Party Tree, hobbit holes scattered across the rolling hills. Every single detail has been thought through and maintained. The Waikato countryside surrounding it provides a backdrop that genuinely feels Middle-earth.

"Even if you've never watched a single Lord of the Rings film, Hobbiton is worth doing. The attention to detail is extraordinary and the setting itself is beautiful. The guided tour brings it all to life in a way that just wandering around independently never could."

The Hobbiton experience

You can't just wander around independently — it's a guided tour experience only. This turns out to be a brilliant thing. The guides know every detail, they bring the stories to life, and they've clearly spent significant time thinking about how to present this in the most interesting way possible. Tours take roughly 2.5 hours and include access to the Party Tree, the Green Dragon pub and various hobbit holes.

Photography

Photography is allowed everywhere and encouraged. The film set is genuinely one of the most photographable places on earth. You will take hundreds of photos.

02
Waitomo Glowworm Caves

Glowworms in a vast underground cathedral

Waitomo Glowworm Cave is genuinely one of the most extraordinary natural experiences available in New Zealand. You board a small boat and are guided through a vast underground cave in almost total darkness. Above you, thousands upon thousands of tiny glowworms create their own galaxy, their lights reflected in the water beneath you.

The silence, the darkness and the sheer otherworldliness of floating through a cave lit only by thousands of tiny lights creates an experience that is impossible to put into words. It's the kind of thing you have to experience in person.

Waitomo options

There are two main cave systems you can visit: the main Waitomo Glowworm Cave (the standard experience of floating through on a boat) and Ruakuri Scenic Reserve (also a boat experience but with a slightly different cave system). There's also black water rafting for the more adrenaline-seeking — you tube through the cave with a headlamp, getting genuinely wet and genuinely muddy.

Common questions
Is Hobbiton worth visiting if I'm not a Lord of the Rings fan?
Absolutely yes. Even if you've never watched a single film, Hobbiton is genuinely worth doing. The attention to detail is extraordinary — every hobbit hole, every garden, every detail of Bag End is maintained exactly as it appeared on screen. The Waikato hills rolling away in every direction provide a backdrop that feels genuinely otherworldly. The guided tour brings it all to life in a way that just wandering around independently never could.
What is the glowworm cave experience really like?
Floating through a vast underground cave in total silence, looking up at a ceiling covered in thousands of tiny glowworms creating their own galaxy above you — it is genuinely one of the most extraordinary natural experiences available on earth. The silence, the darkness and the sheer otherworldliness of it makes Waitomo completely unlike anything else. If you want the full adrenaline version, black water rafting takes you through the cave on an inflated tube.
How long do the tours take?
The Hobbiton tour takes roughly 2.5 hours. The Waitomo glowworm cave experience takes about 45 minutes to an hour. Most tours combine both in a single day and it's possible to do both without feeling rushed.
