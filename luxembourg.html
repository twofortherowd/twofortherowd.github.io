Luxembourg —
the tiny Grand Duchy that genuinely surprised us

Our complete city guide: what to see, do and know before you go.

We flew into Luxembourg from Dublin expecting a pleasant but unremarkable city break. What we got was one of the most visually stunning and historically rich small capitals in Europe — a place that packs an extraordinary amount into a very compact space. Dramatic fortifications carved into cliff faces, a gorgeous lower town in the river valley below, a beautiful grand ducal palace and a bar scene that kept us very happily occupied for several evenings. Luxembourg is criminally underrated as a city break destination — this is everything we'd tell a friend who was thinking of going.

❤️ Our Luxembourg Experience

A few nights in one of Europe's most underrated capitals

"Luxembourg completely caught us off guard. We expected a neat, quiet European capital. What we found was a city of extraordinary drama — fortifications plunging into river valleys, medieval towers rising above modern boulevards and a warmth and liveliness that nobody had warned us about."

We first visited Luxembourg back in 2010, flying direct from Dublin to Luxembourg Airport — one of the easiest city break journeys you can do from Ireland, barely two hours in the air and you're practically in the old town before you've finished your in-flight coffee. We spent a few nights there and between a city tour, plenty of wandering and a very thorough exploration of the local bar scene, we felt like we got a proper feel for the place.

Luxembourg City sits dramatically on a plateau with deep river valleys on three sides — the Alzette and Pétrusse rivers carve their way around the rock below, giving the city its extraordinary vertical character. Standing on the Chemin de la Corniche — dubbed the most beautiful balcony in Europe — and looking out across the Grund and the fortification towers rising from the cliffs is genuinely one of the great urban views in Europe.

The city does something that very few European capitals manage — it is simultaneously ancient and thoroughly modern, multilingual and cosmopolitan, and yet it retains a sense of human scale and warmth that bigger capitals often lose. It is a place that invites you to slow down and explore on foot.

🏰 Best Things to Do in Luxembourg City
🏰
1. Explore the Bock Casemates

A network of underground tunnels and galleries carved into the sandstone cliffs — part of the city's extraordinary UNESCO-listed fortifications. The views from the top over the Alzette valley and the Grund below are breathtaking.

🌊
2. Wander through the Grund

The valley quarter below the old town — accessible by lift or by winding paths and staircases. Full of restaurants, bars and café terraces along the river, with dramatic views up to the fortifications towering above. One of the loveliest neighbourhoods in the Benelux.

📸
3. Walk the Chemin de la Corniche

Nicknamed "the most beautiful balcony in Europe" — a walkway along the top of the old ramparts with jaw-dropping views over the Grund, the Alzette valley and the fortification towers rising from the cliffs. Do it at sunset if you can.

👑
4. See the Grand Ducal Palace

The official residence of the Grand Duke sits right in the middle of the old town. An ornate Renaissance building that looks almost impossibly grand for such a small country — a reminder that Luxembourg punches well above its weight in every respect.

🏛️
5. Have a drink in Place d'Armes

The lively main square of Luxembourg City — lined with café terraces, surrounded by beautiful architecture and full of locals and visitors enjoying the atmosphere. The perfect place to sit with a coffee or a beer and watch the city go by.

🌉
6. Cross the Adolphe Bridge

The iconic bridge crossing the Pétrusse valley — a stunning piece of early 20th century engineering with beautiful arches that frame the valley below. The views from either end of the bridge are some of the best in the city.

🍺
7. Explore the bar scene

Luxembourg has a surprisingly lively and cosmopolitan bar scene — a multilingual, international crowd, good local beers and wines, and bars ranging from traditional to very modern. We gave it a thorough examination and can confirm it stands up very well.

🗺️ Our Suggested 48-Hour Luxembourg Itinerary

Two nights is our sweet spot for Luxembourg City — enough time to see the big sights without rushing, and enough evenings to enjoy the bars properly. Here's how we'd play it if we were going back tomorrow.

Day 1 — The Old Town & the Grund

  1. Morning: Start in the old town — wander around the Grand Ducal Palace and Place d'Armes with a good coffee.
  2. Late morning: Head to the Chemin de la Corniche for that famous view over the Grund and the fortifications.
  3. Afternoon: Take the lift or walk down into the Grund. Lunch by the river, then wander the winding streets of the lower town.
  4. Evening: Back up top for dinner in the old town, then find a bar in Place d'Armes or the Grund for a proper Luxembourg night out.

Day 2 — Fortifications & the Pétrusse Valley

  1. Morning: Explore the Bock Casemates — the underground tunnel network is genuinely fascinating and the views from the top are unmatched.
  2. Late morning: Walk across the Adolphe Bridge for that iconic Luxembourg view of the Pétrusse valley.
  3. Afternoon: Wander the Pétrusse valley below, or take a city walking tour to fill in the historical detail. Coffee stop wherever takes your fancy.
  4. Evening: One last dinner and drinks in the old town — try somewhere new so you leave with a reason to come back.
🚋 Getting Around Luxembourg

Luxembourg made public transport free — and it's brilliant

Here's a fact that most people don't know: since 1 March 2020, Luxembourg has been the first country in the world to offer free nationwide public transport. Buses, trams and second-class trains are all completely free — for residents and visitors alike. You just get on. No ticket, no fare, no faff. (First-class rail and journeys crossing the national border are the only exceptions.)

Practically that means Luxembourg City is exceptionally easy to move around, and it's also easy to get out to smaller towns like Vianden or Echternach for a day trip without spending a cent on transport.

On foot: The old town itself is completely walkable — you can cover the main sights in a day. Just know that the city is genuinely hilly, with the plateau of the old town sitting high above the Grund and Pétrusse valleys. Comfortable shoes are non-negotiable.

Between upper city and lower: The Pfaffenthal Panoramic Lift connects the old town plateau directly to the Pfaffenthal valley below — free to use and worth doing just for the view on the way down. There are also winding paths and staircases if you fancy the walk.

From the airport: Luxembourg Findel airport is only 6km from the city centre and connected by (free) bus and tram. You can be in the old town within about 30 minutes of landing.

❓ Luxembourg FAQ
Is Luxembourg worth visiting?
Absolutely — Luxembourg is one of those destinations that genuinely surprises people. It is small but it packs in extraordinary fortifications, a beautiful old town, the gorgeous Grund quarter in the valley below, excellent food and a very lively bar scene. Most people visit as a day trip but it really rewards a couple of nights.
How many days do you need in Luxembourg?
Two to three nights is ideal — enough time to explore the old town properly, visit the Bock Casemates, wander down to the Grund, take a city tour and enjoy the restaurant and bar scene without rushing. A day trip is possible but you'll miss a lot of what makes the city special.
Is Luxembourg City easy to get to from Ireland?
Yes — there are direct flights from Dublin to Luxembourg Airport with both Luxair and Ryanair, taking around two hours. The airport is very close to the city centre so you can be in the old town within 30 minutes of landing. It is a very easy and underrated city break destination from Ireland.
Is public transport really free in Luxembourg?
Yes — since 1 March 2020, Luxembourg has been the first country in the world to offer free nationwide public transport. Buses, trams and second-class trains are free for both residents and visitors. First-class rail and journeys crossing the national border are the only exceptions. It's brilliant, and it makes exploring the country beyond the city very easy.
What is the Grund in Luxembourg?
The Grund is the valley quarter of Luxembourg City, sitting far below the plateau of the old town and connected to it by lifts, stairs and winding paths. It is one of the most charming and photogenic neighbourhoods in the city — full of restaurants, bars and terraces along the Alzette river, with dramatic views up to the old town fortifications above.
What is Luxembourg City known for?
Luxembourg City is best known for its extraordinary medieval fortifications — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — which include the famous Bock Casemates, a network of underground tunnels carved into the rock. The city is also one of the capitals of the European Union and has a cosmopolitan, multilingual character unlike anywhere else in Europe.

Planning your own Luxembourg trip?

Luxembourg is one of Europe's best kept travel secrets — small enough to explore properly in a few days, beautiful enough to keep you staring at your photos for years afterwards. Fly direct from Dublin, walk the Corniche, descend to the Grund and raise a glass in one of the city's excellent bars. You'll wonder why you didn't go sooner.
