County Donegal —
Wild Atlantic Beauty, Rugged Cliffs & Untamed Adventures

Wild coastlines, spectacular mountains, golden beaches and some of the most breathtaking scenery in Ireland.

If ever a county could convince you that Ireland is one of the most beautiful places on earth, Donegal would give it a good go.

Up in the far north-west, it's a land of dramatic cliffs, sweeping beaches, rugged mountains and coast roads that make you stop for photos every five minutes. Just when you think you've seen the best of it, another view appears around the corner.

It has a strong identity, a living Irish language and plenty of stories. The biggest mistake you can make in Donegal is trying to rush it.

Start here
South-west Donegal: cliffs, towns & beaches
Sea cliffs · Viewpoints

🌊 Slieve League (Sliabh Liag)

Among the highest sea cliffs in Europe, rising dramatically above the Atlantic, with views along the coast and over the mountains. You can enjoy them from designated viewpoints or tackle more demanding walking routes.

Why visit? Landscapes, photography and outdoor adventures.

⚠️ Cliff edges, steep ground and fast-changing weather. Take great care, and check current parking, visitor-centre and shuttle arrangements before you travel.
Town · Castle

🏰 Donegal Castle & Donegal Town

The restored castle of the powerful O'Donnell clan stands in the middle of Donegal Town, a lively place with a central Diamond, independent shops and plenty of places to eat.

Why visit? History, culture and a good base for the south-west.

Heritage · Seasonal

🏡 Glencolmcille Folk Village

In the Gaeltacht village of Gleann Cholm Cille, a cluster of cottages recreates rural life across the 1700s, 1800s and 1900s, including the hardship of emigration and change.

Why visit? Irish history, folklore and traditional culture.

📅 Open seasonally, so check before you go.
Beach · Steps

🏖️ Silver Strand, Malin Beg

A horseshoe of pale sand tucked beneath steep green cliffs, one of Donegal's most photogenic beaches. You reach it by a long flight of steps from the car park.

Why visit? Photography and scenic beach visits.

⚠️ The steps are steep and long. Check tides and sea conditions, and don't assume it's safe for swimming.
Harbour town

⚓ Killybegs

One of Ireland's most important fishing ports, with harbour views, seafood and easy access to Slieve League.

Why visit? Maritime heritage and seafood.

Surf town

🏄 Bundoran

A lively seaside town at Donegal's southern tip, popular for surfing, beaches, amusements and family holidays.

Why visit? Families, surfing and seaside fun.

03
Head north
Glenveagh, Fanad, Inishowen & Malin Head
National park · Castle

🏞️ Glenveagh National Park

Mountains, lakes, woodland and open bog, with Glenveagh Castle and its gardens on the shore of Lough Veagh.

Why visit? Nature, gardens, history and walking.

ℹ️ The park is open all year, but the castle has its own opening days and occasional closures. Check the official updates.
Glenveagh National Park ↗
Northernmost point · We've been

🧭 Malin Head

The northernmost point on the island of Ireland, on the Inishowen Peninsula. Wild, windy and dramatic, with Banba's Crown at the tip. It was a filming location for Star Wars: The Last Jedi.

Why visit? Coastal scenery, photography and film fans.

🧥 Bring layers, even in summer. It's exposed up there.
Lighthouse · Book ahead

💡 Fanad Head Lighthouse

A working lighthouse on a spectacular headland between Lough Swilly and Mulroy Bay, and one of the most photographed in Ireland. Guided tours cover its history and the lives of the keepers.

Why visit? Photography, history and coastal drives.

🎟️ Book tours in advance. The tower has lots of stairs and a minimum height for children.
Fanad Lighthouse ↗
Forest · Beaches

🌲 Ards Forest Park

Woodland, beaches, salt marsh and trails of different lengths near Creeslough.

Why visit? Families, walking and outdoor relaxation.

Mountain · Serious hike

🏔️ Mount Errigal

Donegal's distinctive quartzite cone, glowing pink at sunset. Stunning from the road, and a real challenge to climb.

Why visit? Landscape photography and challenging hikes.

⛰️ This is a mountain walk, not a stroll. You need good visibility, proper footwear, navigation skills and an eye on the weather.
Peninsula · Road trip

🚗 The Inishowen Peninsula

Coastline, beaches and history, with Malin Head, Five Finger Strand, Fort Dunree and Culdaff. The Inishowen 100 driving route shows off the lot.

Why visit? Road trips, coastal scenery and history.

Heritage · Seasonal

🏚️ Doagh Famine Village

Near Ballyliffin, guided storytelling and displays explore rural Donegal life, from the Famine to more recent times.

Why visit? History, families and cultural heritage.

📅 Check the seasonal tour schedule before visiting.
Headland · Village

🌅 Horn Head & Dunfanaghy

Magnificent cliff scenery at Horn Head, with the welcoming village of Dunfanaghy, its cafés and beaches nearby.

Why visit? Scenery and relaxed village exploring.

Hidden gems & islands
Stone fort · We've been

🏛️ Grianán of Aileach

An ancient stone ringfort on a hilltop near Burt, with views over Lough Swilly and Lough Foyle. We filmed here, see our video above.

Island · We've been

⛴️ Arranmore Island

Reached by ferry from Burtonport, with quiet roads, walks, cosy pubs and big Atlantic views. We loved it, see our video above.

Beach · Remote

🏝️ Murder Hole Beach (Boyeeghter Bay)

A remote, beautiful cove near Melmore Head.

⚠️ Check lawful access first, and stay out of the water. The currents are dangerous.
Beach · Caves

🕳️ Maghera Beach & Caves

Spectacular scenery near Ardara.

⚠️ The caves are only reachable at low tide and can be cut off. Check tide times carefully.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Legend

👁️ Balor and the Poisoned Glen

Folklore links the Poisoned Glen with Balor of the Evil Eye, the fearsome Fomorian king whose gaze could destroy whatever it fell on. In some versions he is slain nearby, and the poison of his eye seeps into the valley.

Another explanation is simply a mistranslation: the Irish for 'heavenly' and 'poisonous' are very close.

History

🛡️ The O'Donnells

The O'Donnells ruled Tír Chonaill, the territory that became much of Donegal, for centuries. Donegal Castle is the easiest place to start understanding their story.

History

⛵ The Flight of the Earls, 1607

From Rathmullan in September 1607, Hugh O'Neill and Rory O'Donnell sailed into exile with their followers. Their departure marked the end of the old Gaelic order and opened the way for the Plantation of Ulster.

Tradition

✝️ St Colmcille

St Colmcille (Columba) was born in Donegal around 521, at Gartan, before founding his famous monastery on Iona. Gleann Cholm Cille bears his name, and its pilgrimage route, the turas, is still walked today.

History

🌊 Maritime Donegal

Donegal's coast is full of stories of shipwrecks, storms and seafaring communities. Fanad Head and Lough Swilly, where the RMS Laurentic sank in 1917 with a cargo of gold, are good places to start.

Ready-made plans
Suggested days out

Grouped by region, because trying to cross Donegal in a day is a mistake.

🌊 South-west Donegal

Ideal for: big cliffs
  1. Route: Donegal Town → Killybegs → Slieve League → Glencolmcille.
  2. Tip: allow plenty of time. The coast roads are slow and the viewpoints are distracting.

🏞️ North-west Donegal

Ideal for: nature and gardens
  1. Route: Glenveagh National Park → Dunfanaghy → Horn Head.
  2. Tip: give Glenveagh several hours if you want the castle and gardens.

💡 Fanad Peninsula

Ideal for: lighthouse lovers
  1. Route: Rathmullan → Portsalon → Fanad Head Lighthouse.

🧭 Inishowen adventure

Ideal for: a full day
  1. Route: Grianán of Aileach → Fort Dunree → Ballyliffin → Doagh Famine Village → Malin Head.
  2. Tip: this is a lot for one day. Split it over two if you like a relaxed pace.

🏖️ Donegal's beaches

Ideal for: photographers
  1. Route: Narin and Portnoo → Maghera Beach → Assaranca Waterfall → Ardara.

🗓️ Three-day introduction

Ideal for: a first visit
  1. Day 1: Donegal Town, Killybegs and Slieve League.
  2. Day 2: Glenveagh, Errigal viewpoints and Dunfanaghy.
  3. Day 3: Fanad Lighthouse and the peninsula.
  4. Tip: this is a lot of driving. Consider staying in two different parts of the county.

Eat, drink, repeat
Food, pubs & music

Places with a strong local reputation. We haven't eaten at these ourselves yet, so treat this as a starting point rather than our personal picks, and check opening hours before you set off.

🍽️ Food

  • The Olde Castle Bar, Donegal Town – traditional pub food beside the castle.
  • The Rusty Mackerel, Teelin – a well-known pub and kitchen near Slieve League.
  • Nancy's Bar, Ardara – a long-established family pub known for its food and hospitality.

🍺 Pubs & music

  • Leo's Tavern, Meenaleck (near Crolly) – run by the family of Clannad and Enya.
  • The Reel Inn, Donegal Town – known for traditional music sessions.

ℹ️ Good to know

  • Gaeltacht areas – Irish is a living language in parts of Donegal. A go raibh maith agat goes a long way.
  • Remote spots – services can be sparse, so pack snacks for long coastal drives.

Please check opening hours before you travel, especially out of season.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚗 Getting around
A car is strongly recommended. Buses serve the towns, but remote places are hard to reach without one.
🗓️ Best time
May to September for long days and more services, though the weather is always unpredictable.
⏳ How long
Three days for an introduction, five to seven to see the different regions.
🛣️ Driving
Allow extra time for narrow roads and scenic stops.
⛴️ Islands
Ferries to Arranmore and Tory depend on schedules and sea conditions.
⚠️ Safety
Avoid exposed cliffs in strong wind, rain or poor visibility. Beaches can have dangerous currents.

Opening times, ferries and access change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Go Visit Donegal, Discover Ireland, Glenveagh National Park, Fanad Lighthouse and Heritage Ireland.

Common questions
County Donegal FAQ
How many days do you need in Donegal?
Three days is a good introduction, but five to seven is far better. It's a big county with very different regions, and the roads are slow.
Can you visit Glenveagh Castle all year?
The national park is open all year, but the castle has its own opening days and occasional closures. Check the official National Parks updates before you go.
Is Malin Head the most northerly point in Ireland?
Yes, Malin Head on the Inishowen Peninsula is the northernmost point on the island of Ireland.
Do I need to book Fanad Lighthouse?
Booking tours in advance is a good idea. The tower involves lots of stairs, and there's a minimum height requirement for children.
