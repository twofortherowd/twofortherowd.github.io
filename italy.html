Italy at a glance

Useful things to know before you go

The practical basics for planning an Italian city break or a longer journey between regions.

💶
CurrencyEuro (€). Cards are widely accepted, although some cash is useful for smaller purchases.
👥
PopulationApproximately 59 million people across Italy.
🏛️
CapitalRome—the country's largest city and one of Europe's great historic capitals.
🗣️
LanguageItalian. English is commonly understood in the main visitor destinations.
🕐
Time zoneCentral European Time—one hour ahead of Ireland.
🔌
Plug typesTypes C, F and L. Visitors from Ireland need a European plug adaptor.
🚄
Getting aroundHigh-speed trains make Rome, Venice, Turin and other major cities easy to combine.
🌤️
When to visitSpring and autumn suit sightseeing; summer is hotter and busier, while winter is ideal for quieter city breaks.
📞
Emergency numberDial 112 for emergency assistance anywhere in Italy.

Our Italian city guides

Four very different cities, from Turin's elegant arcades and Venice's canals to romantic Verona and the astonishing layers of Rome.

Rome

2005 & 2019 • Nicola

Two visits filled with the essential Roman sights: the Colosseum, Roman Forum, Pantheon, Trevi Fountain, Spanish Steps and the unforgettable atmosphere of the Eternal City.

The 2005 trip also included Nicola's extraordinary Vatican experience during Pope John Paul II's final weeks.

Verona

February 2012 • Nicola & friends

A lovely day trip from Venice spent wandering Verona's streets, visiting Juliet's House and soaking up the romance and history of the city.

December 2023 • Nicola & Podge

A winter weekend of baroque piazzas, arcaded streets, festive lights and some of the best gluten-free pizza Nicola has ever eaten.
