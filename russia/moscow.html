Moscow —
Red Square, the Kremlin and one of the world's great cities

Moscow is vast, dramatic and utterly compelling. Standing in Red Square with St Basil's Cathedral on one side and the Kremlin walls on the other is one of those travel moments where you genuinely have to stop and take in the fact that you are actually there. The Kremlin grounds, the Bolshoi Theatre, the extraordinary Metro stations, Novodevichy Convent, the day trip to Sergiev Posad, the energy of one of the world's great megacities — Moscow grabbed us by the collar and refused to let go.

🚄 Arrival by Sapsan

Four hours through the Russian heartland

We arrived in Moscow in the evening after a spectacular four-hour journey on the Sapsan high-speed train from St Petersburg. After our time in St Petersburg, the Sapsan carried us on to the second major city of our Russian journey. Arriving in Moscow felt like the next chapter of the trip — a completely different city, with a scale and energy all of its own.

We checked into the Park Inn by Radisson before beginning our Moscow sightseeing. The contrast with St Petersburg was immediate: less imperial elegance, more sheer scale, drama and big-city energy.

⭐ Red Square & St Basil's Cathedral

One of the most iconic sights in the world

"Standing in Red Square with St Basil's Cathedral on one side and the Kremlin walls on the other is one of those travel moments where you genuinely have to stop and take in the fact that you are actually there."

Red Square is as iconic in person as in any photograph — and more so. The scale of the space, the impossibility of St Basil's Cathedral with its coloured onion domes, the Kremlin walls running along one side, the historical weight of the place — it's genuinely extraordinary. You stand in Red Square and you feel like you're standing in one of the great centers of human history.

St Basil's itself is even more remarkable up close. The different-coloured domes, the intricate architecture, the sheer audacity of its design — it's unlike anything else in the world. Seeing it up close was every bit as memorable as we had hoped.

The Kremlin — the seat of Russian power

The Kremlin is the heart of Moscow and of Russia itself — the seat of power since the 15th century. Walking the Kremlin grounds, seeing the massive walls, the towers, the historic buildings, visiting one of its cathedrals — you get a visceral sense of Russian history and political power.

The scale is immense. The walls stretch on and on. The sense of history and authority is overwhelming. This isn't just a tourist site — this is where Russia is governed from. It's genuinely awe-inspiring.

Theatre Square & the Bolshoi

Theatre Square is one of Moscow's most beautiful public spaces, dominated by the extraordinary Bolshoi Theatre — one of the world's most famous opera houses. Whether or not you manage to see a performance inside, the building itself is worth standing and admiring.

The square and its surrounding architecture added another completely different side to Moscow after the monumental history of Red Square and the Kremlin.

Sparrow Hills & Moscow University

Our Moscow sightseeing also took us to Sparrow Hills, where the view opens out across the enormous city and gives a real sense of Moscow's scale.

Moscow State University's monumental Stalinist-era building dominates this part of the skyline and is another striking contrast with the older Moscow we had been exploring.

⛪ Sergiev Posad — Trinity Lavra of St Sergius

Moscow's most important day trip

An optional full-day excursion to Sergiev Posad — a historic and deeply spiritual monastery town about 70km north of Moscow. The Trinity Lavra of St Sergius is the most important Russian monastery and the spiritual heart of the Russian Orthodox Church. Its churches, domes and monastery complex offered a very different experience from central Moscow.

The monastery was extraordinarily beautiful, and the visit gave us another glimpse of Russia beyond the two great cities on our itinerary.

It also meant getting outside Moscow for part of the day and seeing another side of the country.

Our original tour itinerary also offered an optional Old Russian Circus experience in the evening.

Novodevichy Convent — Moscow's hidden gem

One of Moscow's most quietly magnificent places — the Novodevichy Convent is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the burial place of many famous Russians. The walled convent, with its extraordinary churches, peaceful grounds and fascinating history stretching back to the 16th century, is a wonderful contrast to the grandeur of Red Square and the Kremlin.

Novodevichy feels removed from the chaos of central Moscow — a sanctuary of calm and history. The architecture of the churches is beautiful, and the sense of place and spirituality is profound. It's one of the great underseen places in Moscow.

🍽️ Russian Dinner

An evening of food, warmth and great company

One of our Moscow evenings included a Russian dinner at Restaurant Russian Kitsch — a lively end to a day of sightseeing and another chance to enjoy the warm atmosphere we remember from the trip.

It is one of the memories that sits alongside the famous landmarks when we think back on Moscow: not just the buildings and history, but the atmosphere of being there.

📅 Our Moscow Itinerary

Day 1 — Arrival in Moscow

Arrival by Sapsan from St Petersburg. Hotel check-in at Hotel Park Inn by Radisson. Evening exploration of the city — first impressions of Moscow's scale and energy.

Day 2 — Red Square, Kremlin & Theatre Square

Red Square with St Basil's Cathedral — one of the most iconic sights in the world. Kremlin grounds visit and one of the Kremlin's cathedrals. Theatre Square and the Bolshoi Theatre. Sparrow Hills and Moscow University for the panoramic views over the city.

Four-hour excursion to the magnificent Novodevichy Convent — one of Moscow's most beautiful sites and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Russian dinner at Restaurant Russian Kitsch in the evening — an evening of food, warmth and celebration before departure the following morning.

Day 5 — Departure

Early morning departure from Moscow, returning to Dublin.

💡 What Stood Out for Us

What we remember most about Moscow

  • Red Square lived up to the hype. Seeing somewhere so familiar from photographs and television in real life was a genuine pinch-me travel moment.
  • Moscow felt completely different from St Petersburg. St Petersburg had swept us away with its imperial beauty; Moscow felt bigger, bolder and more intense.
  • The Kremlin added the historical weight. It is impossible to walk around this part of Moscow without being conscious of how much history has unfolded there.
  • Sergiev Posad showed us another side of Russia. Getting beyond the capital and seeing the monastery complex added something very different to the itinerary.
  • Give Moscow time. Our organised itinerary packed a huge amount into the Moscow section, and the city is vast.

The Kremlin grounds, the Bolshoi Theatre, the extraordinary Metro stations and the energy of one of the world's great megacities make it a genuinely thrilling place to visit.

How many days should you spend in Moscow?
Our Moscow section ran across several days, including arrival, the major city sights, a Sergiev Posad excursion, Novodevichy and departure. Moscow is enormous, so allowing several days makes sense if you want to see more than Red Square and the Kremlin.
