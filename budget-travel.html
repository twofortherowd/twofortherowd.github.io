Budget Travel Big trips. Real jobs. Honest budgets.

We both work full-time, and we've still made it to more than 60 countries and territories. We don't have a secret fund or a sponsor. We just squeeze every bit of value out of our money and our days off. This is how we do it.

Budget doesn't mean miserable

We're not the type to sleep on airport floors or live on supermarket sandwiches for a week. Budget travel, for us, is about getting the most from what we spend. We cut costs where they don't matter, like hotels we don't need, overpriced parking and expensive big-name cities. Then we spend on the bits that make a trip.

And there's a catch: Nicola is a teacher, so we can only travel during school holidays, exactly when prices are at their highest. If we can find value in mid-term and the summer holidays, so can you. Here's how we travel on a teacher's calendar →

Save where it doesn't matter, so you can spend where it does.

💶 Our money-saving playbook
1

Skip the hotel altogether: do it in a day

≈ €150 each

That's what our 14-hour day in Liverpool cost, flights, parking, two tours, afternoon tea and dinner included. Our extreme day trips are simple: the first flight out of Ireland, a full day exploring, and the last flight home. No hotel bill, no luggage fees, and only one day of annual leave.

See our extreme day trips →
2

Park off-airport

Whenever we fly from Dublin, we park at the off-airport car park right beside the airport (Park2Travel, formerly Quick Park) instead of the airport's own car parks. Always compare the options for your dates before you book, as it's an easy saving that adds up over a year of trips.

3

Pick the city nobody else picks

Bydgoszcz instead of the famous Christmas markets. Turin instead of Rome or Venice. The less obvious city is usually cheaper and quieter, and it's often the one we love most.

Read about Bydgoszcz → Read about Turin →
4

Book the one big thing first

Choose the one experience you'd be gutted to miss, book it, and build the rest of the trip around it. It stops you overspending on things you only half-want and makes sure you don't miss the thing you came for.

5

Travel light

Cabin bag only, or even just a small backpack on a day trip. No checked-bag fees, no bag drop queue and no waiting at the carousel.

6

Let a tour do the heavy lifting on big trips

For bigger trips, a group tour bundles your transport, guide and much of your accommodation into one price, so there are fewer surprise costs. We booked our Morocco trip through TourRadar, and first bookings get a small automatic saving through TourRadar+.

7

Never pay full price for an attraction

Before booking a tour or attraction, check for a code. We keep our Klook promo codes page updated every month and round up the big sales, like Black Friday, as they happen.

8

Try before you fly

Not sure a city is for you? Take a virtual drive around it first with our partners at Drive From Home, and save your money and leave days for the places you'll really love.

❓ Budget travel FAQ
Can you travel a lot from Ireland on a budget with a full-time job?
Yes. We both work full-time and have visited more than 60 countries and territories. The trick is getting the most from every euro and every day off: extreme day trips with no hotel, underrated cities instead of the big names, group tours for the bigger trips, and the discount codes and sales we actually use.
How much does an extreme day trip from Ireland cost?
Our 14-hour Liverpool day trip from Dublin cost about €150 each in September 2026, including return flights, airport parking, the Magical Mystery Tour, a hop-on hop-off bus, afternoon tea at The Beatles Story and dinner. See the full breakdown.
Is budget travel just about spending as little as possible?
Not for us. We save where it doesn't matter (hotels on day trips, airport parking, the expensive big-name cities) so we can spend on the things that make a trip, like a good tour or an afternoon tea.
