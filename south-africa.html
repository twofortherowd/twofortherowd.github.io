📖 Understanding South Africa

A little history makes the journey mean so much more

South Africa's story begins long before European settlement, with indigenous Khoisan communities and powerful African societies whose histories shaped the region for centuries.

Colonial rule: The Dutch East India Company established a permanent refreshment settlement at the Cape in 1652. British control followed in the 19th century, alongside frontier conflict, dispossession and the expansion of settler rule. The South African War (1899–1902) was followed by the creation of the Union of South Africa in 1910.

Apartheid: Racial segregation existed long before 1948, but after the National Party came to power that year it was turned into a formal system of apartheid. South Africans were classified by race, communities were forcibly segregated and the Black majority was denied fundamental political and civil rights. Resistance came from the ANC and many other organisations, activists, communities and ordinary people; Nelson Mandela spent 27 years in prison.

Democracy: Negotiations in the early 1990s dismantled apartheid. South Africa held its first fully democratic election in April 1994 and Nelson Mandela became president. Today it is a constitutional democracy, while the social and economic legacy of apartheid remains visible.

Why this matters when you visit: Places such as Soweto, Vilakazi Street and the Apartheid Museum make far more sense with this context. They are not side attractions to the wildlife and scenery; they are central to understanding the country.

✨ The Standout Moments

What made this trip extraordinary

🏔️
Table Mountain

The cable car to the top gives you one of the great views in the world — Cape Town spread below you, the Atlantic on one side, the Cape Peninsula on the other and Robben Island visible on a clear day.

🐧
Penguins at Boulders Beach

Walking among African penguins on a beach at the southern tip of the continent — one of those completely unexpected wildlife encounters that makes you stop and laugh with sheer delight.

Kruger at Dawn

An open vehicle, the African bush coming alive around you, and the Big Five appearing out of the early morning light. There is simply nothing else like it anywhere in the world.

🌅

Blyde River Canyon, God's Window and Bourke's Luck Potholes — one breathtaking natural wonder after another on one of Africa's great scenic drives.

The most powerful day of the entire trip. The Apartheid Museum tells South Africa's story with extraordinary honesty. Mandela's house makes history tangible. It is deeply moving and absolutely essential.

A boat cruise through an estuary teeming with hippos, crocodiles, sea turtles and sharks — a wild and completely unique ecosystem that showcases the extraordinary biodiversity of KwaZulu-Natal.

✈️ Planning Your Trip to South Africa

Before you go — the practical stuff

☀️
When to go

There is no single best season for the whole country. Cape Town has a Mediterranean-style climate, while safari regions have different rainfall patterns. Drier months can make wildlife easier to spot; warmer months bring greener landscapes and strong birdlife.

💳
Money

The currency is the South African rand (ZAR). Cards are widely useful in cities and tourism areas, but having some local cash is still sensible. Avoid relying on a fixed exchange rate when planning.

🗣️
Language

South Africa has 12 official languages, including South African Sign Language. English is widely used in tourism, but the country's multilingual character is part of what makes travelling here so interesting.

🔌
Electricity

South Africa commonly uses Type M sockets, alongside other socket types. Check that your travel adaptor specifically covers South Africa before you leave rather than assuming a generic adaptor will fit.

🚗
Getting around

Distances are enormous and traffic drives on the left. Domestic flights, organised transfers and multi-day tours can simplify an itinerary that combines Cape Town, safari country and neighbouring Eswatini.

🩺
Health

Malaria precautions can be relevant in parts of the north-east, including areas around Kruger. Check current travel-health guidance before departure, especially if your route includes safari regions.

🧭
Safety

Use sensible urban precautions: be aware of your surroundings, avoid displaying valuables and follow reliable local advice about transport and areas to avoid after dark. Check current official travel advice before travelling.

🕒
Time

South African Standard Time is UTC+2 year-round, with no daylight-saving time.

Common questions

South Africa FAQ

Is a South Africa group tour worth it?
Absolutely — South Africa is one of those destinations where a guided group tour genuinely transforms the experience. The distances involved, the game drives, the logistics of moving between Cape Town, KwaZulu-Natal, Eswatini and Johannesburg are all handled for you, leaving you free to focus on the extraordinary experiences at every stop.
What is the best time to visit South Africa?
There isn't one perfect season for the whole country. Cape Town has wetter winters and warm, dry summers, while the north-eastern safari regions have different rainfall patterns. For wildlife, the drier months can make animals easier to spot; for Cape Town and the coast, warmer months are popular. Choose the season around the regions you most want to visit.
How many days do you need in South Africa?
Our 12-day route worked very well for combining Cape Town, KwaZulu-Natal, Eswatini, Kruger, the Panorama Route and Johannesburg/Soweto. If you have longer, South Africa easily rewards extra time for the Winelands, Garden Route or a slower safari itinerary.
Is Kruger National Park worth visiting?
Yes — Kruger National Park is one of the greatest wildlife experiences on earth. Larger than the state of Connecticut, it offers some of the best game viewing in Africa. An open-vehicle game drive at dawn is one of those travel experiences that genuinely has no equal.
