Dordogne & Lascaux — stepping back into prehistoric France

The Dordogne chapter of our France story is dominated by one extraordinary name: Lascaux. This is where some of the world's most famous prehistoric cave art was discovered — and where visitors today can experience painstaking reproductions created to protect an original cave that has been closed to the public for decades.

🦬 Our Lascaux Story

A replica — and that's exactly the point

Lascaux is specifically part of our existing France travels, and it's the experience that gives this Dordogne page its focus. The important thing to understand before going is that visitors do not enter the fragile original cave. It has been closed to the public since 1963 to protect the prehistoric paintings.

What visitors can experience instead are extraordinarily careful reproductions. Lascaux II recreates part of the cave, while Lascaux IV, the International Centre for Cave Art, reproduces the original cave in its entirety and adds modern interpretation spaces.

🎨 Why reproduce a cave?

The original paintings were threatened by the effects of mass tourism. Closing the cave protects the art; the replicas allow people to understand its scale, contours and imagery without sacrificing the thing everyone has come to see.

🕯️ More than paintings

Lascaux is about the whole decorated environment — animal figures, engravings, the shape of the cave and what the art tells us about people living in prehistoric Europe.

🪨 Lascaux Explained

Original cave, Lascaux II and Lascaux IV

🔒

The Original

Discovered in 1940, the original cave is not open to ordinary visitors. It was closed in 1963 after tourism began damaging the delicate environment and paintings.

🕯️

Lascaux II

Opened in 1983 close to the original cave, Lascaux II is a partial facsimile reproducing the first major sections and much of the famous artwork.

🏛️

Lascaux IV

Opened in 2016, the International Centre for Cave Art reproduces the entire original cave and uses exhibition spaces and technology to put the prehistoric art into context.

Worth knowing: if you see a ticket advertised for “Lascaux”, don't assume it grants access to the original cave. Check exactly which visitor site and experience you are booking.

⏳ The Lascaux Timeline

From accidental discovery to careful preservation

1940

The discovery

Four teenagers discovered the cave near Montignac in September 1940, revealing one of the twentieth century's great archaeological finds.

1963

The closure

Heavy visitor numbers altered the cave environment and threatened the paintings, leading to the original cave being closed to the public.

2016

Lascaux IV

The International Centre for Cave Art opened with a complete reproduction of the original cave and new spaces devoted to understanding cave art.

Common questions

Dordogne & Lascaux FAQ

Can you visit the real Lascaux cave?
No. The original cave has been closed to the public since 1963 for conservation. Visitors experience faithful reproductions at Lascaux II and Lascaux IV.
What's the difference between Lascaux II and IV?
Lascaux II is the older partial facsimile, while Lascaux IV reproduces the entire original cave and forms part of the International Centre for Cave Art.
Did we actually visit Lascaux?
Yes. Lascaux is specifically included in our existing France travel story and is the personal experience around which this Dordogne page is built.
When was Lascaux discovered?
Four teenagers discovered the cave near Montignac in September 1940. It became one of the most famous prehistoric art sites in the world.
