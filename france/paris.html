🗼 Our Paris Story

Forget the stereotype — our experience of Paris has been a good one

Our main France page describes Paris as a genuinely lovely city, with brilliant food and more to see than you could comfortably fit into a single visit. We've also never bought into the lazy idea that Parisians are automatically rude. Speaking French certainly helps us, but our own experience simply hasn't matched the stereotype.

One of the advantages of returning to France over many years is that Paris doesn't have to become a frantic checklist. The city has enough depth to reward repeat visits: major landmarks when you want them, museums that could occupy days by themselves, and neighbourhoods where the pleasure is simply being in Paris rather than racing to the next attraction.

🇫🇷 Speaking French helps

Nicola being a French teacher is part of our wider France story. Being able to communicate comfortably in French changes the experience — particularly when asking questions, dealing with practicalities or simply having a proper conversation.

🧭 Don't try to “complete” Paris

For a first visit, choose the things that matter most to you. Paris is too large and too layered to turn into a sensible one-day checklist, and the museums alone can absorb a huge amount of time.

✨ Paris Highlights

The famous sights are famous for a reason

These are useful starting points for planning your own Paris trip. We're deliberately presenting them as visitor highlights, not pretending that every landmark below represents a specific personal memory from our source page.

🗼

Eiffel Tower

Paris's defining landmark. If going up the tower matters to you, check current ticket arrangements before travelling rather than relying on walk-up availability.

🖼️

The Louvre

One of the world's great museums and far too large to absorb properly in a rushed visit. Decide what you most want to see before you arrive.

⛪

Notre-Dame

A landmark at the heart of historic Paris. Visitor access and arrangements can change, so use current official information when planning.

🎨

Musée d'Orsay

A major art museum in the former Gare d'Orsay, particularly famous for its nineteenth- and early twentieth-century collections.

🌿

Montmartre

A hilltop district associated with artists, Sacré-Cœur and some of the city's most recognisable streets and views.

🚤

The Seine

The river gives Paris its geography. Walking its banks or seeing the city from the water can help connect landmarks that otherwise feel scattered across a map.

💡 Paris Without the Panic

A few sensible planning principles

📍

Group your sightseeing

Paris is large. Grouping attractions by area is usually more pleasant than zig-zagging across the city all day.

🎫

Check tickets ahead

For the major attractions you care most about, check current official ticketing and reservation arrangements before your trip.

🚇

Use public transport

Paris has extensive public transport. Check the current network, fares and any service changes for your travel dates.

Our approach: leave room for Paris itself. A perfectly efficient itinerary isn't necessarily the best way to enjoy a city you've travelled all that way to experience.

Common questions

Paris FAQ

Do we actually like Paris?
Yes. Our France page describes it as a genuinely lovely city with brilliant food and far more to see than one visit can comfortably cover. Our own experience hasn't matched the tired “rude Parisian” stereotype.
Did we go to Disneyland Paris?
Yes — in 2016. We loved the atmosphere and rides, and its accessibility from Ireland is a major advantage compared with travelling to the American Disney parks.
How many days do you need in Paris?
There isn't one correct number. Several days gives you much more breathing room, but the best length depends on whether your priority is major landmarks, museums, food, neighbourhoods or day trips. Don't try to see everything at once.
Should I book Paris attractions ahead?
For popular attractions that matter to you, it's sensible to check current ticket and reservation arrangements before travelling. Avoid building your trip around old prices, opening hours or booking rules.
Is speaking French necessary?
No, but we find speaking French genuinely useful. Even a few polite phrases can make everyday interactions easier, while Nicola's fluency gives us a particular advantage when we're travelling in France.
