Côte d'Azur — when France meets the Mediterranean

The Côte d'Azur is the France of blue Mediterranean water, palm-lined promenades, old towns, elegant façades and coastal light. Nice and Cannes give us two very different anchors: one a substantial Mediterranean city with a strong identity of its own, the other forever associated with cinema and Riviera glamour — but with much more behind the red carpet.

🌊 Nice

More than a promenade and a beach

Nice is the Côte d'Azur's major city and a destination in its own right. The Promenade des Anglais follows the Mediterranean waterfront, but the city also has the lanes of Vieux-Nice, Cours Saleya, museums, distinctive Niçois food and a landscape that moves quickly from coast towards hills and mountains.

The official tourism office describes Nice Côte d'Azur as a place of contrasts between sea, mid-country villages and high mountain landscapes — which is exactly why the city works as more than a simple beach stop.

🌴

Promenade des Anglais

The famous waterfront promenade is one of Nice's defining images, running alongside its long stretch of Mediterranean shoreline.

🏘️

Vieux-Nice

The old town gives you the dense streets, colourful façades and southern atmosphere that contrast with the broad seafront.

🌺

Cours Saleya

A central old-town market area associated with flowers and local produce — a useful reminder that Nice has its own culture and food identity rather than being generic Riviera.

🎬 Cannes

Yes, there's a red carpet — but keep walking

Cannes inevitably brings the film festival to mind, and the Palais des Festivals and La Croisette are part of the experience. But the city's own tourism information also points visitors towards Le Suquet, the old port and the Lérins Islands — enough to make Cannes worthwhile even when there isn't a film star in sight.

🎥

Palais des Festivals

The building and famous steps are inseparable from Cannes' international cinema identity.

🌴

La Croisette

The seafront boulevard delivers the classic Cannes image of Mediterranean water, palms, grand hotels and Riviera polish.

🏘️

Le Suquet

The old quarter brings a very different texture to Cannes, with narrow streets above the old port and views across the bay.

🚆 Exploring the Riviera

You don't necessarily need to drive the coast

One of the useful things about this stretch of the Côte d'Azur is that the principal coastal cities are linked by regional trains. That makes it realistic to base yourself in one place and make day trips rather than changing hotel every night.

France's official tourism information specifically recommends regional trains for travelling between major Côte d'Azur cities including Nice, Cannes, Antibes and Menton. For us, that makes this one of the easier French regions to explore without building the entire trip around a hire car.

🚉 City hopping

Nice and Cannes can comfortably form part of the same Riviera trip, while Antibes and other coastal stops can be added according to your time and interests.

⛰️ Look inland too

The Côte d'Azur doesn't stop at the beach. Perched villages and the mountainous hinterland create a completely different landscape only a short distance from the Mediterranean.

Common questions

Côte d'Azur FAQ

Is Nice worth more than a day trip?
Yes. Nice is a substantial city with its own old town, food culture, museums, waterfront and easy access to the wider Riviera and hinterland.
Is Cannes only about the film festival?
No. The Palais des Festivals and Croisette are famous, but Le Suquet, the old port and Lérins Islands give Cannes plenty beyond its cinema identity.
Can you travel from Nice to Cannes without a car?
Yes. Regional trains connect the major coastal cities, making Nice, Cannes and other Riviera stops practical to combine without hiring a car.
Are Côte d'Azur beaches sandy?
It varies by location. Nice is particularly associated with pebble beaches, while other parts of the Riviera include sandy beaches and coves.
Can I explore Nice and Cannes virtually?
