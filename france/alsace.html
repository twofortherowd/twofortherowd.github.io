🏘️ Our Colmar Story

Yes, it really does look like that

We based ourselves in Colmar, and it gave us exactly the Alsace atmosphere we'd hoped for: colourful façades, half-timbered buildings, cobbled streets and the canals of Little Venice. The official tourist office describes Little Venice as the stretch of the Lauch running through the south-east of the city, historically associated with winegrowers, market gardeners and boatmen.

Colmar is also exceptionally well positioned for the wine country around it. The official tourism office calls the Alsace Wine Route a 170 km route through vineyards, medieval and Renaissance villages and wine cellars — and Colmar sits right in the middle of that world.

🌊 Little Venice

One of Colmar's signature areas, with the Lauch, bridges and colourful historic houses creating the view everybody associates with the city.

🍇 Wine capital

Colmar's central position makes it a natural jumping-off point for the Alsace Wine Route and the villages scattered through the surrounding vineyards.

🍷 Wine Tasting — Twice

Two tastings, two completely different settings

Wine wasn't just scenery on this trip. During our full-day Alsace excursion we stopped for a tasting at Stoecklé. Then the following day, our Colmar walking tour with Nicolas finished with another tasting in the cellar at Domaine Karcher, where we tried three wines.

That second experience worked particularly well because it connected Colmar itself to the vineyards surrounding it. The city's wine identity isn't merely something you have to leave town to experience — wine production and cellars are part of Colmar's own story too.

🚶 Colmar on Foot

Our walking tour with Nicolas

On 26 July we explored Colmar on a walking-and-wine tour with Nicolas. After the previous day's village-hopping, slowing down and concentrating on the city itself gave us a different perspective on Alsace.

🍷 Book the Colmar Walking Tour & Wine Tasting →

The tour we did: This is the actual Colmar walking-and-wine tour. The booking page confirms the wine tasting at Robert Karcher & Fils, and reviews specifically mention Nicolas as the guide.

Colmar's historic centre is made for wandering. Little Venice gets the photographs, but there is far more to look at: the Quai de la Poissonnerie, Renaissance architecture such as the Pfister House and streets where the region's French and Germanic influences sit side by side.

Our tip: if you're doing both Colmar and the Wine Route, give them separate time. The villages are gorgeous, but don't use Colmar merely as somewhere to sleep between excursions.
