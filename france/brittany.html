Brittany was one of our earliest French loves. 🌊 Our Brittany Story

A month gave us time to see beyond the obvious stops

Our main France page records a full month in Brittany in 2010, with Plancoët becoming our base while we explored the region. That longer stay is a big part of why Brittany stands out in our memories: it wasn't simply a quick city break or one famous sight ticked off a list.

Two places in particular stayed with us. Dinan brought the medieval Brittany people picture — cobbled streets, half-timbered buildings and a historic centre full of character. Dinard, across the water from Saint-Malo, felt completely different: an elegant seaside town of villas, beaches and Atlantic views.

And then there is the Celtic connection. Brittany has its own language and cultural traditions, and that distinctive Breton identity was part of what made the region feel familiar to us as Irish visitors without ever feeling like anywhere other than France.

🇮🇪 The Irish connection

For us, the interest is not that Brittany is somehow “Ireland in France”; it's that both places have strong Celtic cultural histories. The Breton language and regional identity give Brittany a character of its own.

🚗 A region worth slowing down for

Our longer stay meant Brittany became more than one headline attraction. Small towns, coast and historic centres are a large part of the appeal, so it suits travellers who want to explore beyond a single city.

📍 Three Places That Stayed With Us
🏡

Plancoët

Plancoët became our base during that month in Brittany. It isn't the sort of place that dominates international France itineraries, which is part of why it belongs in our story: it represents the smaller-town Brittany we got to know during a longer stay.

🏘️

Dinan

Dinan is the historic showstopper of the three — a medieval centre of cobbled streets and half-timbered buildings that makes it an easy place to explore on foot and one of the Breton stops we remember most clearly.

🏖️

Dinard

Dinard changes the mood completely. Its villas, beaches and seaside setting give it an elegant resort feel, with Saint-Malo sitting just across the water. It showed us another side of Brittany altogether.

Also nearby: Saint-Malo is an obvious companion to Dinard for travellers exploring this part of the coast. We're mentioning it here as useful regional context rather than inventing a separate personal story around it.

Common questions

Brittany FAQ

Is Brittany worth visiting?
For us, absolutely. It's one of the parts of France that stayed with us most strongly after a longer stay. The combination of coast, historic towns and a distinctive Breton identity gives it a very different character from Paris or southern France.
Are Dinan and Dinard similar?
Not really, and that's part of their appeal. Dinan is known for its medieval historic centre and half-timbered buildings; Dinard is an elegant seaside resort with villas and beaches across the water from Saint-Malo.
Why does Brittany have a Celtic connection?
Brittany has a distinct Celtic cultural heritage and the Breton language is a Celtic language. As Irish visitors, that regional identity was one of the things we found particularly interesting.
Where did we base ourselves?
Our existing France story records Plancoët as our base during Nicola's month in Brittany in 2010. We also remember Dinan and Dinard particularly strongly from our time in the region.
Do you need a car for Brittany?
A car can make regional exploration easier, particularly if you want to connect smaller towns and coastal areas, but the best option depends on your itinerary. Check current rail, bus and ferry connections for the places you plan to visit.
