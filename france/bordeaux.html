Bordeaux — much more than a very famous glass of wine

Bordeaux may be one of the world's great wine names, but the city itself deserves attention before you even reach the vineyards. Think grand eighteenth-century façades, sweeping squares, the Garonne waterfront, historic streets and a modern wine museum unlike the usual château tasting room.

🏛️ Bordeaux itself

Don't rush straight out to the vineyards

Bordeaux's historic centre is one of the reasons the city works even for travellers who aren't particularly interested in wine. Bordeaux, Port of the Moon has been on UNESCO's World Heritage List since 2007, recognised for an urban and architectural landscape shaped over more than two millennia.

The grand classical and neoclassical cityscape developed particularly strongly from the eighteenth century onwards, when Bordeaux's role as a major port transformed its architecture and urban form.

🏛️ Place de la Bourse

One of the city's defining architectural ensembles, facing the Garonne and forming the classic Bordeaux view with the Miroir d'eau.

🌊 The quays

The Garonne is fundamental to Bordeaux's story. Walking the redeveloped riverfront helps make sense of the city as a historic trading port rather than simply a wine destination.

💦 Place de la Bourse & the Water Mirror

The photograph everybody wants — but the square matters too

The Place de la Bourse is one of Bordeaux's great eighteenth-century set pieces. In front of it, the modern Miroir d'eau creates the reflection that has become one of the city's best-known images.

It's an excellent example of old and new Bordeaux working together: monumental historic architecture facing a contemporary public space on the riverfront.

🍷 Cité du Vin

Wine without having to pretend you know what “terroir” means

La Cité du Vin approaches wine as culture rather than simply as something to drink. Its permanent exhibition explores vineyards, winemaking, terroirs and wine civilisations from around the world through an interactive visitor experience.

The standard permanent-exhibition ticket currently includes access to the Belvedere on the eighth floor and a tasting with panoramic views over Bordeaux. The venue advises allowing roughly two to three hours for the permanent exhibition.

🍇 Beyond Bordeaux — Saint-Émilion

This is where the city turns into wine country

If you want the vineyard side of Bordeaux, Saint-Émilion is one of the obvious additions. The medieval town and its surrounding wine landscape make it much more than a tasting stop, and Bordeaux tourism actively combines the city and Saint-Émilion in short itineraries.

🍇

The vineyards

Wine estates surround the historic settlement, giving you the château-and-vineyard Bordeaux experience that the city centre obviously cannot.

⛪

The medieval town

Saint-Émilion has its own historic appeal, including underground monuments and the extraordinary monolithic church.

🚐

Day-trip friendly

If nobody wants to drive after a tasting, an organised excursion from Bordeaux is a very sensible way of doing wine country.

🗺️ How we'd approach Bordeaux

Give the city and the vineyards separate time

1️⃣

Walk Bordeaux

Start with the UNESCO centre, riverfront and major squares so Bordeaux doesn't become merely the hotel you sleep in before a wine tour.

2️⃣

Do the wine culture

Cité du Vin gives useful context before or after visiting an actual vineyard and works even if you're not a wine expert.

3️⃣

Then leave town

Use a separate day for Saint-Émilion or another vineyard area rather than squeezing city sightseeing and château visits into one frantic schedule.

Common questions

Bordeaux FAQ

Is Bordeaux worth visiting if you don't drink wine?
Yes. The UNESCO-listed architecture, historic centre, Garonne waterfront, museums and neighbourhoods give Bordeaux plenty beyond wine.
Why is Bordeaux UNESCO listed?
Bordeaux, Port of the Moon, is recognised for its exceptional urban and architectural ensemble and the city's role in international exchange over more than two thousand years.
How long should you allow for Cité du Vin?
The venue recommends roughly two to three hours for the permanent exhibition, with time for the Belvedere and included tasting.
Can you visit Saint-Émilion from Bordeaux?
Yes. It's one of the classic wine-country additions to a Bordeaux stay and can be visited independently or on an organised excursion.
