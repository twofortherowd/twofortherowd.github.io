Underground Wonders, Island Castles & Lakeland Adventures

A magical county where beautiful lakes, hidden caves and centuries of history are waiting to be discovered.

If your idea of a brilliant getaway involves lakes, forests, castles and a bit of adventure, County Fermanagh has you covered.

It's famous for Upper and Lower Lough Erne, a world of waterways, islands and peaceful countryside, but there's plenty happening beneath the surface too, from the Marble Arch Caves to the striking boardwalk up Cuilcagh Mountain.

Add grand estates, atmospheric ruins and the welcoming island town of Enniskillen, and you've got the makings of a fantastic short break.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Fermanagh, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🛂 Crossing from the Republic?

There are no border checks, but prices are in pounds sterling (£), speed limits are in miles per hour, and it's worth checking your phone plan and car insurance cover before you go.

Start here
Caves, mountains & Enniskillen
Geopark · Guided tours

🕳️ Marble Arch Caves

Guided tours take you through limestone caves carved by underground rivers, with a boat trip on an underground river at the start. Part of the cross-border Cuilcagh Lakelands UNESCO Global Geopark.

Why visit? Geology, nature and families.

📅 Tours are guided and depend on water levels and weather. Check before you travel.
Walk · Strenuous

🪜 The Cuilcagh Boardwalk

Nicknamed the Stairway to Heaven, this trail crosses blanket bog before a boardwalk and staircase climb towards the summit plateau, with huge views.

Why visit? Fit walkers and dramatic scenery.

⚠️ It's a strenuous walk with lots of steps. Stay on the path, check the weather and access arrangements, and allow plenty of time.
Castle · Museums

🏰 Enniskillen Castle

Beside the River Erne, the castle houses the Fermanagh County Museum and the Inniskillings Museum, telling the story of the area and its famous regiments.

Why visit? History and culture.

Island · Monastic site

⛪ Devenish Island

Early Christian ruins on an island in Lower Lough Erne, including one of the finest round towers in Ireland. Getting there by boat is half the fun.

Why visit? Archaeology, history and island adventures.

⛴️ Boat access is seasonal. Check ferry and tour arrangements.
National Trust

🏛️ Castle Coole

A magnificent neoclassical mansion just outside Enniskillen, with parkland walks.

Why visit? Architecture, history and gardens.

National Trust

🌳 Florence Court

An elegant 18th-century house with woodland walks, walled gardens and the original Irish yew tree.

Why visit? Architecture, gardens and heritage.

Around the county
Lough Erne, estates & craft
Craft · Tours

🏺 Belleek Pottery

Ireland's oldest working pottery, famous for its delicate parian china. Factory tours run depending on current arrangements.

Why visit? Craftsmanship and industrial heritage.

Lakes · Boating

🛶 Lough Erne

The lakes are the heart of Fermanagh. Take a boat trip, go kayaking or fishing, or just enjoy the waterfront.

Why visit? Water activities, relaxation and wildlife.

🌊 Use reputable operators and wear a buoyancy aid.
Country park · WWII

✈️ Castle Archdale Country Park

A former estate on Lower Lough Erne that became a flying boat base in the Second World War. Woodland, family recreation and a fascinating history.

Why visit? Families, history and walking.

National Trust · Nature

🦦 Crom Estate

Woodland, wetlands, islands and ruins on Upper Lough Erne. A haven for wildlife.

Why visit? Nature, history and wildlife.

Castle ruins

🏰 Tully Castle

Plantation-era castle ruins by Lower Lough Erne, attacked in the 1641 rebellion, with a recreated 17th-century garden.

Why visit? History and photography.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Mystery

🗿 The Janus figure

Nobody knows exactly who carved the two-faced Boa Island figure or why. It may be a pagan god, a Christian-era carving with older echoes, or something else entirely, and that mystery is half its charm.

History

⛪ Devenish and the waterways

In early medieval Ireland the lakes were highways. Devenish, founded by St Molaise in the sixth century, became an important centre of learning.

History

🏰 The Plantation of Ulster

From 1609 settlers were granted land across Fermanagh, and castles like Tully and Monea were built to defend it. Many were attacked in the 1641 rebellion.

Folklore

🌊 Lakeland stories

The islands and waters of Fermanagh are full of local tales, from drowned towns beneath the lakes to saints who sailed between them.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚗 Getting around
A car is useful, but a boat trip is a highlight.
🗓️ Best time
Late spring to early autumn for outdoor activities.
⏳ How long
Three days.
💷 Currency
Pounds sterling. Speed limits are in miles per hour.
⚠️ Safety
Caving, mountain walking and lake activities need the right supervision and equipment.
🗺️ County lines
Marble Arch and Cuilcagh are in Fermanagh, but the geopark extends into Cavan.

Opening times, tours and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Discover Northern Ireland, Marble Arch Caves and National Trust.

Common questions
County Fermanagh FAQ
Is the Cuilcagh Boardwalk hard?
It's strenuous, with a long gravel approach and a steep staircase near the top. Allow several hours, wear good footwear and check the weather.
Can you explore the Boho Caves on your own?
We wouldn't recommend it. Wild caving needs specialist knowledge, equipment and an experienced guide.
Are the Marble Arch Caves open all year?
Tours depend on the season, water levels and the weather, so check the official website before you travel.
What currency is used in Fermanagh?
Pounds sterling (£). It's right beside Cavan and Leitrim, and there are no border checks.
