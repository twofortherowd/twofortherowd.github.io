Nicola's Wicklow base
Baltinglass and the west
Fifteen working years

🏘️ Baltinglass

A handsome west-Wicklow town on the River Slaney, backed by Baltinglass Hill. Nicola worked here for 15 years, making this the most personal starting point for our county guide.

Founded in 1148

⛪ Baltinglass Abbey

Dermot MacMurrough, King of Leinster, founded this Cistercian abbey as a daughter house of Mellifont. Its nave, arches and carved stonework still show the scale and ambition of the medieval monastery.

Palladian masterpiece

🏛️ Russborough House

Built for Joseph Leeson, later Earl of Milltown, and designed by Richard Castle, Russborough is one of Ireland's great Palladian houses. Its later history is closely connected with Sir Alfred and Lady Beit and their art collection.

Lakeside west Wicklow

🌊 Blessington Lakes

The Poulaphouca Reservoir transformed this landscape in the 1940s. Today its long shoreline and mountain backdrop make it one of west Wicklow's defining views.

Saints, Vikings, prisoners and industry
A short history of Wicklow
Sixth-century roots

🗼 Glendalough

The monastic settlement associated with St Kevin grew into an important religious centre. Its round tower, churches and graveyard survive in a glacial valley whose landscape is as memorable as its archaeology.

Crime and punishment

⛓️ Wicklow Gaol

The gaol tells the stories of prisoners held through rebellion, famine and the age of transportation, when men, women and children could be sent from Ireland to penal colonies overseas.

Mining valley

⛏️ Avoca

Avoca's valley was shaped by centuries of copper mining, while its handweaving tradition became famous far beyond Wicklow. The village carries both an industrial and a craft story.

Viking coast

🏰 Wicklow Town

Wicklow's coastal story includes Norse settlement, the ruins of the Black Castle and centuries of maritime life — a reminder that the county's history does not stop at the mountains.

05
The Garden of Ireland
Glendalough and the Wicklow Mountains

Glendalough is the historic anchor, but the wider mountains deserve time too. Mountain roads, lakes, forest walks and high passes make this a county where the journey between stops is often the best part.

Monastic valley

🥾 Glendalough walks

Choose anything from an easy walk around the monastic sites and Lower Lake to longer routes overlooking the Upper Lake. Go early when possible.

Mountain road

🚗 Sally Gap

A dramatic crossroads in the mountains, with open moorland and access towards Lough Tay, Glenmacnass and west Wicklow.

Ireland's highest waterfall

🌊 Powerscourt Waterfall

Set below the Wicklow Mountains, the waterfall is a separate visit from Powerscourt House and Gardens and is particularly impressive after rain.

Landscape first

🌧️ Mountain weather

Conditions change quickly. Bring proper layers, allow extra driving time and never let a packed itinerary rush you on narrow roads.

06
Designed landscapes
Great houses and gardens
Terraces and mountain views

🌿 Powerscourt

The formal terraces, ornamental lakes and view towards the Sugar Loaf make Powerscourt one of Ireland's best-known designed landscapes.

Living family history

🏡 Kilruddery House

The Brabazon family has been associated with Kilruddery since the early 17th century. The gardens and later house preserve layers of aristocratic and architectural history.

Plants beside the Vartry

🌸 Mount Usher Gardens

A relaxed Robinsonian garden in Ashford, shaped around the river and celebrated for mature trees, informal planting and seasonal colour.

West-Wicklow grandeur

🖼️ Russborough

Architecture, collections, parkland and an eventful history make Russborough much more than a photograph from the front steps.

From Bray Head south
Wicklow's coast
Classic seaside town

🌊 Bray

A Victorian promenade, Bray Head and an easy rail connection from Dublin make Bray a natural northern gateway to coastal Wicklow.

Harbour and food

⚓ Greystones

A lively coastal town with beaches, harbour views and enough cafés and restaurants to turn a walk into a full day out.

County town

🏰 Wicklow Town

Pair Wicklow Gaol with the harbour, coastline and Black Castle ruins for a day built around both history and the sea.

Village and valley

🧶 Avoca

Avoca sits inland from the coast but fits naturally into an east-Wicklow route, particularly if you want history, scenery and craft together.

What we'd tell a friend
Our practical Wicklow tips
🚗 A car gives you freedom.
Public transport reaches several towns, but the mountains and west Wicklow are far easier to join together by car.
⏰ Reach Glendalough early.
It is one of Ireland's busiest scenic sites, especially on fine weekends and during summer.
🌧️ Expect four seasons.
Bring layers and waterproofs even when the coast or Dublin looks bright.
🏛️ Check separate tickets.
Powerscourt House and Gardens and Powerscourt Waterfall are separate attractions in different locations.
🧭 Don't ignore the west.
Baltinglass, Russborough and Blessington show a side of Wicklow many coastal itineraries miss.
📅 Confirm current access.
Gardens, houses and heritage attractions can have seasonal opening days.

Common questions
County Wicklow FAQ
What is County Wicklow best known for?
Wicklow is known as the Garden of Ireland for its mountains, valleys, gardens and estates. Glendalough and Powerscourt are among its headline attractions.
Is Baltinglass worth visiting?
Yes. Baltinglass has a beautiful River Slaney setting and a substantial Cistercian abbey founded in 1148. Nicola worked in the town for 15 years.
What is the history of Glendalough?
Glendalough grew around a monastic settlement associated with St Kevin from the sixth century. Its round tower and churches survive in a dramatic glacial valley.
Is Russborough House in Wicklow?
Yes — it is near Blessington in west County Wicklow, and we've visited and filmed it ourselves.
How long should I spend in Wicklow?
One focused day works, but two or three days lets you combine west Wicklow, Glendalough, a major garden or estate and part of the coast.
Have you visited these places yourselves?
Yes. We've visited all the featured places, and Nicola also worked in Baltinglass for 15 years.
