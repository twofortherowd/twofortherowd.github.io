There's far more to County Waterford than its famous crystal.

Ireland's oldest city, a coastline carved by the Atlantic, charming fishing villages, imposing castles and some of the country's most spectacular gardens are all waiting to be discovered. This is a county where you could spend the morning with the Vikings, the afternoon driving past dramatic sea cliffs, and the evening wandering a seaside town with something tasty in your hand.

And that's exactly the sort of county we love featuring on Two for the Row'd. Whether you're planning a quick day trip, a weekend away or a slower journey through Ireland's south-east, there's plenty here to keep you busy.

A thousand years of stories
Castles, towns & heritage
History

🏰 Waterford City & the Viking Triangle

Waterford is Ireland's oldest city, founded by the Vikings in 914, and its historic heart is the Viking Triangle: a compact, walkable quarter packed with more than a thousand years of stories.

Its best-known landmark is Reginald's Tower, the sturdy stone tower on the quays, with roots in Viking Waterford and widely regarded as Ireland's oldest civic building. Nearby, the Medieval Museum displays some extraordinary objects from the medieval city.

For something different, the King of the Vikings virtual-reality experience is led by a costumed guide, or you can simply join a guided walking tour.

Why visit? History, architecture, museums and the perfect base for exploring the county.

🎟️ King of the Vikings runs in small groups, so booking ahead is recommended.

History · Gardens

🏯 Lismore

Lismore is one of Waterford's most handsome heritage towns, with the great bulk of Lismore Castle rising above the River Blackwater.

The castle itself is a private residence, so the interior isn't open to visitors. What you can visit, seasonally, are Lismore Castle Gardens and the contemporary gallery, Lismore Castle Arts. Parts of the gardens date back to the early 1600s.

Add St Carthage's Cathedral, the historic streets and the riverside setting, and Lismore earns its place on any West Waterford itinerary.

Why visit? History, heritage, gardens and beautiful architecture.

📅 The gardens open seasonally, roughly spring to autumn. Check the season dates before you travel.

History · Coast

⛪ Ardmore

Ardmore pairs a sandy beach with one of Ireland's most interesting early Christian sites. Its round tower rises above the village, beside the remains of a cathedral and monuments linked with St Declan.

The Ardmore Cliff Walk adds spectacular coastal scenery, historic landmarks and a dose of maritime history.

Why visit? Early Christian heritage, a beach, coastal walking and beautiful scenery.

🥾 Cliff paths need suitable footwear and extra care in wet or windy weather.
Town · Harbour

⚓ Dungarvan

Dungarvan mixes a lively town feel with a lovely waterfront. The harbour, historic streets, cafés and restaurants make it a destination in its own right, and Dungarvan Castle adds another layer of history.

It's also where the Waterford Greenway ends, so it's ideal if you want to combine a cycle with a relaxed afternoon by the water.

Why visit? Harbour scenery, local food, history and Greenway access.

📅 Check Dungarvan Castle's current opening before planning a visit around it.

Outdoors · Geopark

🌊 The Copper Coast

The Copper Coast UNESCO Global Geopark is one of Waterford's great natural treasures: rugged cliffs, hidden coves, remarkable geology and the remains of a once-busy mining industry.

Villages like Bunmahon and coastal stops around Annestown and Boatstrand make it perfect for a slow drive. This is a coast for people who'd rather take a winding road and stumble on a viewpoint than queue for a big attraction.

Why visit? Dramatic coastal scenery, geology, history and quieter corners of Ireland.

💡 Allow time to stop and explore rather than just driving through.

Outdoors · Cycling

🚲 Waterford Greenway

The Waterford Greenway runs for 46 km along an old railway line from Waterford city to Dungarvan, crossing eleven bridges, three viaducts and the atmospheric Ballyvoyle Tunnel.

You don't need to do the whole thing to enjoy it. Pick a shorter stretch and make an afternoon of it: Kilmacthomas sits around the midpoint, and Mount Congreve Gardens is close to the city end.

Why visit? Cycling, walking, scenery and family-friendly days out.

Gardens

🌸 Mount Congreve Gardens

Just outside Waterford city at Kilmeaden, Mount Congreve is home to one of the largest private plant collections anywhere, with trees and shrubs from every continent.

Winding paths let you explore at your own pace. Spring brings colourful blooms, while autumn turns the gardens into a riot of reds, oranges and golds. You can even walk or cycle straight in from the Greenway.

Why visit? Beautiful gardens, photography and a peaceful escape.

Mount Congreve ↗
Coast · Village

⛵ Dunmore East

Colourful houses, a picturesque harbour and coastal scenery: Dunmore East has all the ingredients for a lovely seaside afternoon.

Wander the village, take in the harbour views and try some of the nearby coastal walks. It's ideal if you like pretty villages, sea air and a slower pace.

Why visit? Harbour views, coastal walks and a relaxed seaside atmosphere.

Mountains

💧 Mahon Falls & the Comeraghs

The Comeragh Mountains are a dramatic contrast to Waterford's coastal towns. Their best-known sight is Mahon Falls, where water tumbles down a steep mountainside into a glacial landscape. It's a great spot for photos and for seeing the county's wilder side.

Experienced walkers may also want to see Coumshingaun, a striking glacial lake ringed by steep cliffs.

Why visit? Mountains, waterfalls, dramatic scenery and outdoor adventure.

⛰️ Mountain weather changes fast. Coumshingaun and the higher routes are not casual strolls: they need proper gear, fitness and navigation skills.

Hidden gem · Outdoor + visitor centre

⛏️ Bunmahon

A coastal village with a fascinating copper-mining past. The Copper Coast Geopark Visitor Centre here has a geology and mining exhibition, trail information, a gift shop and a café.

Hidden gem · Outdoor

🏝️ Stradbally Cove

A scenic little cove that's well worth a stop on any Copper Coast outing, especially if you like quieter coastal corners.

Hidden gem · Outdoor, free

🎨 Waterford Walls

Colourful large-scale street art has transformed buildings all over Waterford city. Keep your eyes open as you walk around, and you'll keep spotting new pieces.

Ticketed · Guided

🕯️ The Irish Wake Museum

Ireland's first wake museum explores the traditions, superstitions and rituals surrounding death and mourning in Ireland. It's housed in a 15th-century almshouse in the Viking Triangle and visited on a guided tour.

ℹ️ The museum advises it isn't suitable for children under 8, and the historic building isn't suitable for all mobility needs.

Ticketed · Seasonal

🚂 Waterford & Suir Valley Railway

A heritage railway that runs from Kilmeaden along the River Suir, giving you a completely different view of the countryside west of the city.

📅 Trains run seasonally. Check the current timetable before you go.
Hidden gem · Outdoor

✝️ The heritage of St Declan

Ardmore's links to St Declan, one of Ireland's earliest Christian saints, run through the whole village, from the round tower and cathedral ruins to the legends told on the beach. More on that in the legends section below.

History, myth & mystery
A little history, myth and mystery

We've kept history and legend clearly labelled, because the best Irish stories are usually a bit of both.

History

⚔️ The Vikings of Waterford

Waterford began as a Viking settlement in 914, which makes it Ireland's oldest city. Its streets, medieval buildings and surviving monuments tell a story of centuries of trade, conflict and settlement. Reginald's Tower, standing guard over the quays, is the most famous survivor.

Tradition & legend

✝️ St Declan and Ardmore

Tradition credits St Declan with founding a religious settlement at Ardmore, possibly even before St Patrick arrived in Ireland. Historians treat that early date with caution, but his story is woven deep into the county's identity.

Legend also has it that a large boulder on Ardmore's beach, known as St Declan's Stone, floated across the sea after him. Local tradition says it has healing powers, so you'll sometimes hear of people squeezing underneath it on the saint's feast day.

History

⛏️ The Copper Coast's mining past

Today's beautiful coastline hides a much tougher story. In the 1800s, copper mining shaped the lives of communities along this coast, and you can still spot traces of the industry among the cliffs and fields. That history is what gives the Copper Coast its name.

Ready-made plans
Three suggested day trips

Three geographically sensible days out. Mix and match if you're staying longer.

🏰 A: Viking Waterford

Ideal for: history lovers and first-time visitors
  1. Morning: explore the Viking Triangle and Reginald's Tower.
  2. Lunch: in Waterford city.
  3. Afternoon: visit the Medieval Museum, then wander the historic centre.
  4. Optional extra: go street-art hunting with Waterford Walls.

🌊 B: The Copper Coast drive

Ideal for: coastal scenery and photography
  1. Start in Tramore.
  2. Coast: stop around Annestown, Boatstrand and Bunmahon, where the Geopark visitor centre is.
  3. Continue towards Stradbally and Dungarvan, leaving plenty of time for viewpoints.
  4. Finish with a stroll along Dungarvan's waterfront.

🏯 C: West Waterford heritage

Ideal for: historic towns and picturesque villages
  1. Begin in Lismore, and check whether Lismore Castle Gardens are open for the season.
  2. Then choose: Dungarvan if you fancy a harbour town, or Ardmore if you'd prefer a beach and a cliff walk.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
👍 Best for
Coastal road trips, history fans, walkers, cyclists, couples and weekend escapes.
🚗 Getting around
A car gives you the most freedom, especially on the Copper Coast and in rural West Waterford. Waterford city itself is easy to explore on foot.
🗓️ How long to stay
One day for Waterford city, two to three days for a mix of coast and inland highlights, or longer to see the whole county.
☀️ Best time to visit
Late spring to early autumn gives you longer days for the coast. Autumn is especially beautiful at Mount Congreve.
💶 Budget tip
Mix free coastal walks, villages and viewpoints with just a few ticketed attractions.
🥖 Food note
Waterford is known for the traditional blaa, a soft local bread roll. Standard blaas contain gluten, so coeliac visitors shouldn't assume a gluten-free version is available.

Opening times, seasons and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. For general visitor information, see Visit Waterford and Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Waterford FAQ
Is Waterford really Ireland's oldest city?
Yes. Waterford was founded by the Vikings in 914, which makes it Ireland's oldest city. Reginald's Tower and the Viking Triangle are the best places to start exploring that history.
How long is the Waterford Greenway?
The Waterford Greenway is 46 km long, running from Waterford city to Dungarvan along a former railway line. You don't need to do it all: a shorter stretch, such as one around Kilmacthomas, makes a lovely afternoon.
Can you go inside Lismore Castle?
No. Lismore Castle is a private residence, so the interior isn't open to visitors. Lismore Castle Gardens and Lismore Castle Arts open seasonally, so check the dates before you travel.
What's the best way to see the Copper Coast?
By car, at a slow pace. Drive the coast road between Tramore and Dungarvan and stop around Annestown, Boatstrand, Bunmahon and Stradbally. The Geopark visitor centre in Bunmahon is a great place to learn about the area's geology and mining history.
Have Two for the Row'd visited everywhere on this page?
Not yet, and we'd rather be upfront about it. This Waterford guide is researched rather than first-hand. When we visit and film, our own videos and tips will be added here.
