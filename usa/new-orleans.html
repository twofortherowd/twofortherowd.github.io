❤️ Our New Orleans Experience

Three music cities, three completely different personalities

By the time we reached New Orleans, we'd travelled through two other cities synonymous with American music: Nashville and Memphis. Yet New Orleans didn't feel like a repeat of either.

Nashville → Memphis → New Orleans became one of the strongest stretches of our entire USA itinerary.

🚢 Our Evening on the Steamboat NATCHEZ

Dinner on the Mississippi

One of our New Orleans evenings was spent aboard the Steamboat NATCHEZ for a dinner cruise on the Mississippi. It was a completely different way to experience the city after dark and gave us another classic Deep South memory from this road trip.

The current NATCHEZ jazz dinner cruise runs for around three hours and includes a Creole dinner buffet, with drinks available to buy on board. If a riverboat evening is on your New Orleans list, check current sailing times and availability before travelling.

Our experience We did the evening dinner cruise rather than simply seeing the boat from the riverfront — so the NATCHEZ is very much part of our own New Orleans story.

🌳 Oak Alley & Plantation Country
OAK ALLEYVACHERIE, LOUISIANA

Our visit to Oak Alley Plantation

While we were in Louisiana, we also visited Oak Alley Plantation in Vacherie, on the Mississippi River between New Orleans and Baton Rouge.

The estate is instantly recognisable for its extraordinary avenue of live oak trees leading towards the Greek Revival mansion. Seeing that famous approach in person was one of the most striking sights of this part of our Deep South journey.

More than the mansion

Oak Alley also has a much darker history that is essential to understanding the site. Sugarcane was grown here using the labour of enslaved men, women and children, and the estate's interpretation today includes exhibits examining the lives of the people who were enslaved there.

That context matters. Oak Alley is visually beautiful, but its history is inseparable from slavery and the plantation economy that created the wealth behind estates like this one.

Our experience Oak Alley added another completely different layer to our time in Louisiana — from the music and streets of New Orleans to the history of the plantations along the Mississippi.

🏘️ The French Quarter & New Orleans Atmosphere

The historic heart of the city

The French Quarter, or Vieux Carré, is New Orleans' oldest neighbourhood and the part of the city most visitors picture first: historic architecture, music, restaurants and streets built for wandering. Jackson Square makes a natural starting point, with many of the Quarter's best-known sights within a short walk.

Music is woven through New Orleans far beyond a single street. Frenchmen Street, beside the French Quarter in the Marigny, is particularly known for live jazz, blues and other music venues.

Worth knowing You don't need to treat New Orleans as a checklist. A big part of its appeal is the atmosphere — walking, listening and allowing time to take in the streets around you.
🚋 Getting Around New Orleans

Walk the centre, then use the streetcars

👟 French Quarter on footThe historic core is compact enough to explore largely on foot, particularly around Jackson Square and the surrounding streets.
🚋 StreetcarsNew Orleans' streetcars are both useful transport and part of the experience. The Riverfront line is handy around the riverfront and French Quarter area.
💵 Current fareRTA currently lists a standard single ride at $1.25 and a one-day Jazzy Pass at $3.
📱 Le Pass appThe official RTA Le Pass app can be used to plan journeys, buy fares and track buses and streetcars.
