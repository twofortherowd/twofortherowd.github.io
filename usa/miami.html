🗽 Ellis Island — Practical Info

A powerful piece of New York history

Ellis Island was one of the historic experiences we made time for. More than 12 million immigrants were processed there during the peak years from 1892 to 1924, and today the main immigration building houses the Ellis Island National Museum of Immigration.

If you're planning a visit, the National Park Service advises booking the authorised ferry in advance. The same ferry service provides access to both Ellis Island and Liberty Island.

🚇 Getting Around New York

Getting around Manhattan and beyond

  • The subway is king. Fastest, cheapest and most iconic way to get around. Runs 24/7 across all five boroughs. Buy a MetroCard or use contactless tap-to-pay (OMNY) at the turnstiles.
  • Walk whenever you can. Manhattan is famously walkable — many of the classic sights are closer to each other than they look on the map.
  • Yellow cabs, Uber & Lyft. All widely available. Ride-share is often easier in outer boroughs; yellow cabs are quicker to hail on the street in Midtown.
  • Buses are useful for crosstown routes (e.g. across Central Park) where the subway doesn't help.
  • Don't drive in Manhattan. Traffic and parking are punishing — even locals often don't own cars.
  • City passes like the New York CityPASS or Sightseeing Pass can be great value if you're hitting several paid attractions in a short trip. Do the maths first.

❓ New York FAQ

Good to know

How many times have we visited New York?
We've visited New York twice: first in 2017 and again in 2024.
Where did we stay?
In 2017 we stayed across the Hudson in New Jersey. In 2024 we stayed right in central New York City, giving us two quite different ways of experiencing the area.
What were our New York highlights?
Our experiences included Ellis Island, the Empire State Building, a New York Jets game, a Broadway show (MJ the Michael Jackson musical), a city bus tour and a Sex and the City tour.
How many days do you need in New York?
Three to four nights is a solid minimum for a first visit — enough to cover the big classics like Ellis Island, the Empire State Building and Times Square, plus a Broadway show and a bit of neighbourhood wandering. Longer stays reward you with museums, day trips and the city's neighbourhoods beyond Manhattan.
What is the best time to visit New York?
Spring (April–June) and autumn (September–early November) are the sweet spot — comfortable temperatures, easier crowds and beautiful light. Summer is hot and humid, and winter is famously cold but comes with holiday lights, ice rinks and a distinct New York atmosphere all its own.
How do you get around New York?
The subway is by far the fastest, cheapest and most iconic way to get around Manhattan and the outer boroughs — it runs 24/7. Walking is often the best option for shorter distances. Yellow cabs, Uber and Lyft are plentiful. Driving in Manhattan is not recommended for visitors.
Where did New York fit into our Deep South road trip?
New York was the starting point of our New York-to-Miami road trip, before we continued south to Philadelphia.
