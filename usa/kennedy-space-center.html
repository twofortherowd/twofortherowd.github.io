❤️ Our Kennedy Space Center Experience

We aren't space buffs — and we absolutely loved it

This was probably one of the biggest surprises of our Florida road trip. Neither of us is particularly into space, so Kennedy Space Center wasn't somewhere we expected to spend hours completely engrossed. We ended up staying for around five hours.

What really worked for us was how well organised the whole visitor complex felt. It wasn't just a collection of things behind glass. We wandered through the exhibits looking at astronaut suits, spacecraft and the Space Shuttle displays, but there were also interactive and simulation activities that made the visit much more engaging.

Even without knowing a huge amount about NASA or space exploration beforehand, the scale of everything is fascinating when you're standing beside the real thing.

You definitely don't have to be a space fanatic to enjoy Kennedy Space Center. We aren't — and somehow five hours disappeared!

🚀 What You'll Find Inside

Much more than a museum

One reason Kennedy Space Center held our attention for so long was the variety. You can move from enormous spacecraft to personal astronaut stories and then into interactive experiences rather than spending the whole day reading display boards.

🚀
Rocket Garden

Walk among rockets representing different eras of America's space programme.

🛰️
Space Shuttle Atlantis

The real Atlantis orbiter is one of the showstoppers — the scale is something else when you're beside it.

🌕
Apollo/Saturn V

Another enormous piece of space history and a chance to explore the Apollo era in much more detail.

🎮
Interactive Experiences

Simulations and hands-on activities were a big part of why the visit worked so well for us.

Our planning tip: Allow far more than a quick stop. We spent about five hours here and could easily understand why keen space fans make a full day of it.
🤔 Is It Worth It If You're Not Into Space?

For us: absolutely yes

If you're looking at Kennedy Space Center and thinking, “But I'm not really interested in space…”, that was basically us too.

What surprised us was that you don't need to arrive knowing the difference between every mission or rocket. The exhibits are visual, the spacecraft are enormous, the human stories are interesting and the interactive elements keep things moving. It felt accessible rather than overly technical.

In fact, the fact that neither of us is a space enthusiast and we still happily spent around five hours there is probably the strongest recommendation we can give it.
