❤️ Why We Loved Williamsburg

A complete change of pace

Coming after the scale and monuments of Washington DC, Williamsburg felt completely different. It was smaller, calmer and full of character — exactly the sort of place that makes a road trip interesting.

The historic streets, period buildings and slower pace gave the town a genuinely quaint atmosphere. We loved just being there and taking it all in rather than rushing from one headline attraction to another.

Williamsburg was one of those places that charmed us immediately — historic, beautifully kept and so easy to enjoy at a slower pace.

🏛️ Colonial Williamsburg

History you can actually walk through

Colonial Williamsburg is a 301-acre living-history area centred on Virginia's 18th-century colonial capital. It contains 89 original 18th-century structures, with hundreds of other buildings reconstructed using historical and archaeological evidence.

Rather than feeling like a conventional museum, the Historic Area is something you move through. Historic tradespeople, interpreters, gardens, shops and major sites such as the Governor's Palace and Capitol help recreate the atmosphere of the colonial city.

🗺️ The Historic Triangle

Williamsburg, Jamestown & Yorktown

Williamsburg sits within Virginia's Historic Triangle alongside Jamestown and Yorktown. The three historic areas are connected by the 23-mile Colonial Parkway, making Williamsburg a strong base if you want to explore more of this part of Virginia.

Worth knowing Colonial Williamsburg itself is substantial: the Historic Area is roughly a mile long and half a mile wide, and complimentary buses operate from the Visitor Center to stops around the area.
👟 Exploring Colonial Williamsburg

This is a place to slow down

Williamsburg worked for us because it wasn't about racing from attraction to attraction. The historic streets, gardens and buildings are part of the experience, so give yourself time simply to wander.

🚌 Use the busesComplimentary buses run from the Visitor Center to stops around the Historic Area, useful when you've had enough walking.
🎭 Check the day's programmeHistoric trades, interpreters and programmes vary, so it's worth checking what's happening on the day of your visit.
🏛️ Think beyond one attractionThe appeal is the whole historic setting — buildings, streets, gardens and living-history experiences together.

❓ Good to Know

Williamsburg FAQ

What is Colonial Williamsburg?
Colonial Williamsburg is a 301-acre living-history area recreating Virginia's 18th-century colonial capital. It includes 89 original 18th-century structures alongside carefully reconstructed buildings.
What can you see in Colonial Williamsburg?
The Historic Area includes sites such as the Governor's Palace and Capitol, working historic trades, gardens, museums, shops and daily programmes.
Is Williamsburg part of Virginia's Historic Triangle?
Yes. Williamsburg, Jamestown and Yorktown form Virginia's Historic Triangle and are linked by the Colonial Parkway.
Where did Williamsburg fit into our road trip?
Williamsburg came after Washington DC on our route, before we continued through Virginia towards Roanoke, Gatlinburg and the Great Smoky Mountains.
