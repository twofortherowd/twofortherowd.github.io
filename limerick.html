Limerick sometimes gets overlooked in favour of Ireland's more famous tourist counties, and that's a pity, because there's a surprising amount to discover here.

From King John's Castle on the Shannon to the postcard-pretty streets of Adare, Limerick mixes fascinating history with lovely countryside and plenty of local character. Throw in ancient archaeological sites, one of Ireland's most unusual aviation museums, picturesque villages and some great walking routes, and you've the makings of a proper adventure.

Whether you've a day in the city or time to head out into the county, Limerick deserves more attention than it gets.

Limerick city
History · Ticketed

🏰 King John's Castle

Limerick's most iconic landmark stands on King's Island, guarding the River Shannon. This medieval fortress tells a story of centuries of sieges, conflict and shifting power.

Its exhibitions and surviving castle walls make an engaging introduction to Limerick's past, and the riverside view of the castle is one of the best-known sights in the city.

Why visit? Medieval history, architecture and family days out.

King John's Castle ↗
History · Place of worship

⛪ St Mary's Cathedral

Founded in the 12th century, St Mary's is one of Limerick's most important historic buildings. Its ancient stonework and monuments reflect centuries of history.

The peaceful interior is a lovely contrast to the bustle of the streets outside.

Why visit? Religious heritage, architecture and history.

🙏 St Mary's is a working cathedral, so be mindful of services.
Museum · Rainy-day pick

🖼️ The Hunt Museum

In a historic building beside the Shannon, the Hunt Museum holds a wide-ranging collection of art, antiques and archaeological objects from many periods and cultures.

It's a rewarding stop for art and history lovers, and a great plan B when the weather isn't playing ball.

Why visit? Art, archaeology and rainy days.

Food · Market

🧺 The Milk Market

One of the city's favourite gathering places. The covered market hosts traders selling food, local produce and much more on market days, and it's the best place to feel Limerick's buzz.

Why visit? Food lovers, local produce and city life.

📅 Market days, traders and opening hours vary, so check before you go.
Sport · Guided tours

🏉 Thomond Park

Home of Munster Rugby and one of Ireland's most famous sporting venues. Even if you can't get to a match, there's a guided museum and stadium tour.

Why visit? Rugby fans and sporting heritage.

🎟️ Tours run on weekdays (Saturdays for pre-booked groups only). Book ahead, and check before visiting on or near a match day.

Beyond the city
Around the county
Village · Heritage

🏡 Adare

With its thatched cottages, historic buildings and attractive main street, Adare is one of Ireland's best-known heritage villages.

It's perfect for a relaxed stroll, some photos and a café stop, and the village has some interesting medieval and church landmarks too.

Why visit? Pretty villages, architecture, heritage and photography.

Prehistory · Outdoors

🪨 Lough Gur

Lough Gur is one of Ireland's most important prehistoric landscapes, with archaeology around the lake going back thousands of years.

Nearby, the Grange Stone Circle is the largest stone circle in Ireland and one of the area's best-known monuments. Come for the history and stay for the peaceful lakeside walks.

Why visit? Prehistory, archaeology and outdoor walks.

Museum · Aviation

✈️ Foynes Flying Boat & Maritime Museum

In the flying-boat era, the little Shannon-side town of Foynes became a key stopping point for transatlantic air travel. The museum brings that remarkable chapter to life.

It's also where Irish coffee was famously invented. More on that in the legends section below.

Why visit? Aviation, maritime heritage and unusual Irish history.

Foynes museum ↗
Woodland · Free

🌳 Curraghchase Forest Park

Extensive woodland, walking trails and historic estate features make Curraghchase a lovely spot for a peaceful walk or a family afternoon. It's associated with the poet Aubrey de Vere, whose family estate it was.

Why visit? Woodland walks, nature and picnics.

Hiking · Mountain biking

🚵 The Ballyhoura Mountains

The Ballyhoura region offers some of County Limerick's best outdoor adventures, with forests, hills and big networks of walking and mountain-bike trails.

Why visit? Hiking, cycling, forests and active escapes.

🥾 Trails range from gentle to tough. Pick a route that matches your fitness and experience.

Hidden gem · Monastery

🔔 Glenstal Abbey

A Benedictine monastery near Murroe in beautiful wooded grounds. Day visitors can walk the avenues, visit the gift shop and attend services in the Abbey Church. The icon chapel can be seen on a guided visit.

ℹ️ The school buildings and playing fields aren't open to visitors.
Castle · Summer only

🏰 Desmond Castle, Newcastle West

An important medieval site linked to the powerful Earls of Desmond. Its banqueting hall, with a restored oak minstrels' gallery, once hosted lavish feasts.

📅 Open in summer only, by guided tour. The 2026 season has finished, so plan for 2027.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

We've kept history and legend clearly labelled, because the best Irish stories are usually a bit of both.

History

⚔️ The Sieges of Limerick and the Treaty Stone

Limerick was at the centre of the Williamite War, withstanding sieges in 1690 and 1691. The war ended with the Treaty of Limerick in 1691, one of the defining moments in the city's history.

Tradition says the treaty was signed on the Treaty Stone, which now stands on a plinth across the river from King John's Castle. It's a small stone with a very big story.

Folklore

🧚 The fairies of Knockfierna

Knockfierna is steeped in fairy lore. Local tradition says it's the home of Donn Fírinne, a fairy king of the otherworld, and stories of the 'good people' on the hill were passed down for generations.

We'd file these firmly under folklore, but it's a wonderful example of how landscape and storytelling go hand in hand in rural Ireland.

History

☕ How Irish coffee began

During the flying-boat era, Foynes was a busy stop on transatlantic routes. As the story goes, in the 1940s chef Joe Sheridan served cold, weary passengers coffee laced with Irish whiskey and topped with cream. When asked if it was Brazilian coffee, he reportedly replied, "No, that's Irish coffee."

It's a lovely reminder that a small Irish town played a part in international travel history.

Ready-made plans
Three suggested days out

Three realistic days out, each focused on one part of the county.

🏰 Limerick city history day

Ideal for: history fans and first-time visitors
  1. Morning: King John's Castle, then the Treaty Stone across the river.
  2. Late morning: St Mary's Cathedral.
  3. Afternoon: the Hunt Museum, then a riverside walk such as the Three Bridges.
  4. Bonus: fit in the Milk Market if you're there on a market day.

🏡 Adare and West Limerick

Ideal for: pretty villages and woodland
  1. Morning: Adare village.
  2. Midday: a walk in Curraghchase Forest Park.
  3. Afternoon: on to Newcastle West. Desmond Castle opens in summer only.
  4. Tip: allow time to enjoy each stop rather than rushing.

🪨 Ancient Limerick

Ideal for: prehistory and scenery
  1. Morning: Lough Gur and its lakeside walks.
  2. Midday: the Grange Stone Circle nearby.
  3. Afternoon: continue south to the Ballyhoura countryside for a forest walk.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
👍 Best for
Castles, local history, rugby, archaeological sites, pretty villages and countryside.
🚗 Getting around
Limerick city is walkable. A car is useful for Adare, Lough Gur, Foynes and the rural attractions.
🗓️ How long to stay
One day for the city highlights; two to three days to mix city and county.
☀️ Best time to visit
Spring to autumn for outdoor exploring, but the city's attractions work all year round.
💶 Free & low-cost ideas
Riverside walks, People's Park, the Treaty Stone, village wanders and woodland walks.
☔ Rainy-day options
The Hunt Museum, King John's Castle's exhibitions and the Foynes museum.

Opening times, seasons and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. For general visitor information, see Limerick.ie and Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Limerick FAQ
What is Limerick best known for?
King John's Castle, the Treaty of Limerick, Munster Rugby at Thomond Park, and pretty Adare. Add Lough Gur's prehistoric landscape and the Foynes Flying Boat Museum (the birthplace of Irish coffee), and there's a lot more to Limerick than people expect.
Is Limerick city worth a day?
Yes. King John's Castle, St Mary's Cathedral, the Hunt Museum, the Milk Market and the riverside walks fill a day nicely, and the city centre is compact enough to explore on foot.
How do I get to Adare from Limerick city?
Adare is a short drive south-west of the city, and there are also bus connections. It's an easy half-day trip, or combine it with Curraghchase and West Limerick.
Can you tour Thomond Park?
Yes. Guided museum and stadium tours run on weekdays, with Saturday tours for pre-booked groups. Book ahead, and check before you go around match days.
