County Armagh —
Ancient Warriors, Apple Orchards & Legendary Landscapes

A small county with mighty stories, ancient royal connections and more history than you could shake a sword at.

County Armagh might not be the biggest county in Ireland, but don't let its size fool you.

This is where ancient warriors supposedly roamed and saints left their mark. Known as the Orchard County, it's famous for its Bramley apples, and the orchards burst into blossom every spring.

There's far more than apples, though: an ancient royal site, two cathedrals dedicated to St Patrick, a planetarium, beautiful forest parks and the myths of Slieve Gullion. Whether you're into mythology, history, countryside or a family day out, Armagh has plenty of surprises.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Armagh, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🛂 Crossing from the Republic?

There are no border checks, but prices are in pounds sterling (£) and speed limits are in miles per hour. Check your phone plan and car insurance cover before you go.

Armagh city: myths, cathedrals & stars
Ancient site · Myth

👑 Navan Fort & the Navan Centre

Navan Fort, Emain Macha in Irish, is one of Ireland's most important ancient ceremonial sites. In mythology it was the seat of the kings of Ulster and home of the Red Branch Knights.

The Navan Centre brings the archaeology and the legends to life with exhibitions, storytelling and guided experiences.

Why visit? Irish mythology, archaeology and families.

🎟️ Book the guided experience to get the most out of the site.
Cathedral

⛪ St Patrick's Roman Catholic Cathedral

Twin spires on a hilltop, dominating the city skyline, with a grand flight of steps up to the doors.

Why visit? Architecture, religious heritage and photography.

Cathedral · Ancient site

⛪ St Patrick's Church of Ireland Cathedral

On the hill where tradition says St Patrick founded his church in the fifth century. Brian Boru, High King of Ireland, is said to be buried in the grounds.

Why visit? History, architecture and early Christianity.

Science · Family

🔭 Armagh Observatory & Planetarium

Fancy a trip into space without leaving Ireland? Dome shows, interactive exhibits and an Astropark with a scale model of the solar system in the grounds.

Why visit? Families, science and rainy days.

Armagh Observatory & Planetarium ↗
Library · Heritage

📚 Armagh Robinson Library

Founded in 1771 by Archbishop Richard Robinson, with rare books and manuscripts in a beautiful, scholarly setting. Its copy of Gulliver's Travels carries Jonathan Swift's own handwritten notes.

Why visit? Books, culture and heritage.

Museum

🏺 Armagh County Museum

On the Mall in the city centre, covering local history, archaeology, art and folk traditions.

Why visit? Local history and culture.

Around the county
Mountains, forests, lakes & orchards
Mountain · Forest park

⛰️ Slieve Gullion

Near the border with County Louth, Slieve Gullion rises at the heart of the Ring of Gullion. The forest park has walking trails, a scenic drive and family attractions, and the summit has a passage tomb and a small lake steeped in legend.

Why visit? Walking, families, scenery and mythology.

⛰️ The summit walk is steep. Wear proper footwear and watch for changing weather.
Forest park · Family

🌳 Gosford Forest Park

Woodland walks, cycling trails, wildlife and play areas near Markethill, with Gosford Castle in the grounds.

Why visit? Families, cycling, walking and nature.

National Trust · House

🏡 The Argory

A National Trust country house beside the River Blackwater, with gardens and riverside walks and an interior that has barely changed since the early 20th century.

Why visit? Heritage, gardens and families.

Nature reserve · Lough Neagh

🦆 Oxford Island

A nature reserve on the shore of Lough Neagh near Lurgan, with walking trails, bird hides and a discovery centre.

Why visit? Birdwatching, peaceful walks and families.

⚠️ Lough Neagh has had blue-green algae blooms in recent summers. Follow local advice and keep dogs out of the water if warned.
Park · Lurgan

🦢 Lurgan Park & Brownlow House

One of Northern Ireland's largest public parks, with a lake and open green spaces. Brownlow House, the striking 19th-century mansion beside it, is open for tours and exhibitions.

Why visit? Walking, families, picnics and architecture.

Family · Farm

🐐 Tannaghmore Gardens & Animal Farm

Farm animals, gardens and play space near Craigavon. A good one for young families.

Why visit? Young families and outdoor fun.

Food heritage · Seasonal

🍎 Apple country

Armagh is the Orchard County, and its Bramley apples have protected (PGI) status. The orchards around Loughgall are glorious in spring blossom.

Why visit? Food heritage, countryside and spring scenery.

ℹ️ Orchards are private working farms. Stick to public roads, trails, tours and organised events. Blossom timing depends on the weather.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Legend

🛡️ Cú Chulainn and the Red Branch Knights

Emain Macha is the heart of the Ulster Cycle, the great stories of warriors, battles and royal rivalries. Its greatest hero, Cú Chulainn, earned his name as a boy by killing the fearsome hound of Culann with a hurley and sliotar.

Legend

🐎 The curse of Macha

Macha, a supernatural woman, was forced by the king to race against his horses while heavily pregnant. She won, gave birth at the finish line and cursed the men of Ulster to suffer the pains of childbirth in their hour of greatest need.

The site's name, Emain Macha, is linked to her story.

Legend

🧙 Fionn and the lake on Slieve Gullion

The Cailleach Beara tricked Fionn mac Cumhaill into diving into the little lake on the summit to fetch her ring. He came out an old man with white hair. Local lore says bathing in the lake will turn your hair grey.

History

✝️ St Patrick and Armagh

Armagh became Ireland's ecclesiastical capital, and it is still the seat of both the Catholic and Church of Ireland Archbishops of Armagh, Primates of All Ireland.

History

🍎 The Orchard County

Apples have been grown in Armagh for centuries. The orchards represent generations of farming skill, and they're still a proud part of the county's identity.

Food & pubs

Places with a strong local reputation. We haven't eaten at these ourselves yet, so treat this as a starting point rather than our personal picks, and check opening hours before you set off.

🍽️ Food

  • The 4 Vicars, Armagh – café dining on historic Vicars' Hill.
  • Digby's, Killylea – award-winning family-run gastropub.
  • Basil Sheils, Keady – riverside gastropub with a strong food reputation.

🍺 Pubs in Armagh city

  • The Hole in the Wall – a historic pub with a famously long bar, said to have once been a jail.
  • Red Neds – a long-established city pub with weekend music.
  • Keegan's – family-run traditional pub with home-cooked bar food.

🍺 Around the county

  • McConville's, Portadown – Victorian pub with preserved snugs.
  • O'Hanlon's (The Bridge Bar), Mullaghbane – centuries-old riverside pub in the Ring of Gullion.

Prices are in pounds sterling (£). Please check opening hours and food service before you travel.

Common questions
County Armagh FAQ
Why are there two St Patrick's Cathedrals in Armagh?
Armagh is the seat of both the Catholic and the Church of Ireland Archbishops of Armagh. Each church has its own St Patrick's Cathedral, on neighbouring hills.
When is the best time to see the apple blossom?
Usually late April into May, but it depends on the weather, so dates can't be guaranteed.
Is Banbridge in County Armagh?
No. Banbridge, the F.E. McWilliam Gallery and the Game of Thrones Studio Tour are in County Down, although they're often promoted alongside Armagh.
What currency is used in Armagh?
Pounds sterling (£). There are no border checks if you're coming from the Republic, and speed limits are in miles per hour.
