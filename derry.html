County Derry —
Historic Walls, Wild Coastlines & Stories Worth Telling

A legendary walled city, spectacular Atlantic beaches, fascinating history and a county with plenty of character.

There's something special about County Derry. Maybe it's the history, maybe it's the coastline, or maybe it's the humour and warmth of the people. Whatever it is, it leaves an impression.

Derry city is one of Ireland's most fascinating places to visit, famous for its walls, colourful streets, culture and, of course, a certain TV series about a group of teenagers growing up in the 1990s.

Beyond the city, there are enormous sandy beaches, a temple on a cliff, seaside towns and the quiet countryside that shaped Seamus Heaney's poetry. Whether you're into history, culture, coast or somewhere a little different, County Derry has plenty to explore.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Derry, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🛂 Coming from Donegal or the south?

Derry city is minutes from the Donegal border, and there are no border checks. Prices are in pounds sterling (£) and speed limits are in miles per hour. Check your phone plan and car insurance cover before you go.

Start here
Derry city
Walls · Free

🏰 The City Walls

Built between 1613 and 1619, Derry's walls are the most complete city walls in Ireland and one of the finest examples in Europe. You can walk the whole circuit, about a mile, past bastions, cannons and views over the streets below.

Why visit? History, photography and walking.

🧭 A guided walking tour is the best way to understand the city's complex history.
Landmark

🌉 The Peace Bridge

The curving foot and cycle bridge across the Foyle, opened in 2011, links the city centre with Ebrington Square and has become a symbol of connection and reconciliation.

Why visit? Photography, riverside walks and modern architecture.

Civic building

🏛️ The Guildhall

A grand neo-Gothic building just outside the walls, with beautiful stained glass and exhibitions on the city's story.

Why visit? History, architecture and heritage.

Museum · Modern history

✊ Museum of Free Derry

In the Bogside, this museum tells the story of the civil rights movement, the Troubles and Bloody Sunday in January 1972, when 13 people were shot dead by British soldiers and another later died of his injuries.

Why visit? Visitors who want to understand modern Derry.

🙏 A powerful, sometimes difficult visit. Take your time.
Museum · TV

📺 The Derry Girls Experience

At the Tower Museum: original memorabilia from the show, including Erin's diary and those Spice Girls costumes, plus a replica of the Quinn family kitchen. Don't miss the Derry Girls mural on Orchard Street afterwards.

Why visit? TV fans, pop culture and a good laugh.

Head for the sea
The coast: temples, strands & cliffs
National Trust · Clifftop

🏛️ Mussenden Temple & Downhill Demesne

Perched on a cliff above Downhill Strand, Mussenden Temple was built in 1785 as a summer library by Frederick Hervey, the eccentric Earl Bishop. The ruins of his Downhill House and the clifftop grounds make a wonderful walk.

Why visit? Photography, architecture and coastal scenery.

National Trust ↗
Beach · Film location

🏖️ Downhill Strand

A huge sandy beach beneath the cliffs, perfect for long walks. Game of Thrones fans will know it as Dragonstone.

Why visit? Beach walks and dramatic scenery.

🌊 Check tides, and take care with vehicle access on the sand.
Beach · National Trust

🌾 Portstewart Strand

Two miles of sand backed by dunes, cared for by the National Trust and an important habitat as well as a favourite seaside spot.

Why visit? Beach walks, scenery and nature.

Beach

🌊 Benone Strand

One of the longest beaches in Northern Ireland, with Binevenagh rising behind it. Beautiful on a clear day.

Why visit? Families, beach walks and photography.

Seaside village

🚂 Castlerock

An attractive seaside village with its own beach and a railway station, handy if you're travelling by train.

Why visit? Seaside walks and car-free day trips.

Mountain · Viewpoint

⛰️ Binevenagh

A dramatic escarpment above the coast, with sweeping views over Lough Foyle to Donegal.

Why visit? Scenery, photography and walking.

⚠️ Stick to established routes and keep well back from the cliff edges.

Heaney country, valleys & towns
Literature

📖 Seamus Heaney HomePlace

In Bellaghy, celebrating the life and poetry of the Nobel laureate who grew up nearby. The exhibition explores his childhood and the places that shaped his work.

Why visit? Literature, culture and heritage.

Seamus Heaney HomePlace ↗
Country park

🌲 Roe Valley Country Park

Riverside paths, woodland, waterfalls and old industrial heritage near Limavady.

Why visit? Woodland walks, families and wildlife.

Town

🏘️ Coleraine

A historic town on the River Bann, a good base for the coast and the Causeway Coast next door in Antrim.

Why visit? Town exploring and a central base.

06
History, myth & mystery
History & stories

History and legend, clearly labelled.

History

🏰 The Siege of Derry, 1689

In December 1688, thirteen apprentice boys shut the city gates against the troops of King James II. The siege that followed lasted 105 days and caused terrible suffering before relief ships broke through the boom on the Foyle.

It's still remembered in different ways by different communities, and the walls themselves are its biggest reminder.

Tradition

✝️ St Colmcille and the oak grove

The city's Irish name, Doire, means 'oak grove'. Tradition says St Colmcille founded a monastery here in the sixth century before leaving for Iona, and he's still the city's patron saint.

History & legend

📚 The Earl Bishop and Mussenden Temple

Frederick Hervey, Bishop of Derry and Earl of Bristol, was famously eccentric. He named the temple after his cousin, Frideswide Mussenden, which fuelled plenty of romantic gossip, and is said to have raced his clergy along the beach on horseback for vacant parishes.

Literature

🌾 Seamus Heaney's countryside

Heaney grew up on a farm at Mossbawn, near Bellaghy. Bogs, wells, digging and family life in this landscape run all through his poetry.

History

🌊 The River Foyle

The Foyle made Derry a port, a trading town and, for millions of emigrants, the last sight of Ireland.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚶 Getting around
The city centre is walkable. Trains and buses link several towns, and a car helps for the countryside and coast.
🗓️ Best time
May to September for outdoor attractions. October brings the Halloween festival.
⏳ How long
Two days for the city, three or four for the wider county.
💷 Currency
Pounds sterling. Speed limits are in miles per hour.
🙏 History
Approach sites linked to the Troubles with respect and an open mind.
🗺️ County lines
The Giant's Causeway is in Antrim, and Malin Head is in Donegal.

Opening times and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Visit Derry, Discover Northern Ireland, National Trust and Seamus Heaney HomePlace.

Common questions
County Derry FAQ
Is it Derry or Londonderry?
Both names are used. You'll hear 'Derry' most locally, and you'll see 'Derry~Londonderry' on a lot of official tourism material.
Is the Giant's Causeway in County Derry?
No, it's in County Antrim, though it's an easy drive from Coleraine and Portstewart. Malin Head is also nearby, but it's in County Donegal.
When is Derry's Halloween festival?
Derry hosts one of the biggest Halloween celebrations in Europe each October, with parades, fireworks and events all over the city. Check the official programme for dates.
What currency is used in County Derry?
Pounds sterling (£). There are no border checks with Donegal, but speed limits change to miles per hour.
