Why this county matters to us
Our Wexford

Wexford has been part of Nicola's story for a long time. She spent many childhood summers around Duncannon and Curracloe — two beaches that still bring back those unmistakable memories of Irish summer holidays by the sea.

Years later, exploring Wexford with Podge has brought a different appreciation of the county. These days, we're just as likely to seek out the calm of Blackwater Beach, wander around a historic house or garden, or find one of those wonderfully unexpected places that ends up becoming a Two for the Row'd favourite.

And that's really Wexford for us. It can be the famous sweep of Curracloe, the harbour at Kilmore Quay or a summer afternoon in Courtown — but it's also Johnstown Castle, Wells House, the Kennedy story, an extraordinary Masonic Lodge hidden away in Gorey, and quiet stretches of coastline where you can sometimes feel like you've got Wexford entirely to yourself.

We've known parts of this county since childhood. We're still discovering the rest.

The Wexford Nicola knew first
The coast — old favourites and quieter finds
Childhood favourite

🏖️ Duncannon

One of the beaches wrapped up in Nicola's childhood memories of Wexford. It still has that classic south-east holiday feel — sea, sand and a village sitting right above the beach.

Watch our Duncannon Short ↓
The famous one

🎬 Curracloe & Ballinesker

Curracloe's huge sweep of sand is Wexford's best-known beach. The opening D-Day sequences of Saving Private Ryan were filmed here and on adjoining Ballinesker — but for Nicola, its connection goes back much further than Hollywood.

Our calmer choice now

🌾 Blackwater Beach

These days, Nicola and Podge have a particular appreciation for the calm of Blackwater. It's the sort of Wexford beach that suits a slower wander rather than a packed itinerary.

Harbour Wexford

⚓ Kilmore Quay

A peaceful fishing village with a very different feel from the big beach resorts. Come for the harbour, the sea air and that unmistakable south-Wexford atmosphere.

Watch our Kilmore Quay Short ↓
North Wexford nostalgia

🎡 Courtown

Another part of Nicola's younger Wexford: mini golf, amusements and going mad on the waltzers. It is classic family-holiday nostalgia rather than a polished hidden gem — and that's exactly why it belongs here.

Quiet coastal stop

🌊 Dollar Bay

A gorgeous little Hook Peninsula beach and one of those places that proves Wexford's coast is about far more than the famous names.

More coast worth knowing

☀️ Rosslare & Ballymoney

Rosslare is one of the county's long-established seaside bases, while Ballymoney gives north Wexford another beautiful stretch of coast. Both are easy additions to a wider Wexford road trip.

A Wexford icon

💡 Hook Lighthouse

We've been to Hook ourselves, and it remains one of the county's landmark stops: dramatic sea views, centuries of maritime history and a lighthouse that has stood guard here for more than 800 years.

Castles, houses & wonderfully unexpected history
A proper day-out stop

🏰 Johnstown Castle

One of the county's grand heritage stops, with the castle, grounds and agricultural museum giving you plenty more than a quick photo opportunity. We paired it with the Kennedy Homestead for a history-heavy Wexford day.

Irish-American history

🇮🇪 Kennedy Homestead

A different side of Wexford history, telling the story of the Kennedy family's Irish roots. It works particularly well as part of a New Ross and south-Wexford heritage route.

Heritage Week discovery

🏡 Wells House & Gardens

One of our Heritage Week stops and exactly the sort of place we love finding: a historic house, grounds to explore and enough character to make it more than a simple tick-box attraction.

🧭 Gorey Masonic Lodge

This is the kind of discovery that makes us want to keep digging around Ireland. We visited during Heritage Week and got a rare look inside a place many people pass without ever knowing what is behind the door.

What we'd tell a friend
Our practical Wexford tips
🚗 Give yourself a car if you can.
Wexford's best mix of beaches, villages and heritage stops is much easier when you can detour whenever something catches your eye.
🌦️ Don't build the whole day around sunshine.
Pair a beach with a house, castle or museum so the day still works if Irish weather changes its mind.
🏖️ Don't stop at Curracloe.
It's famous for good reason, but Duncannon, Blackwater, Kilmore Quay and the Hook Peninsula give you completely different versions of coastal Wexford.
🧭 Watch for Heritage Week.
Events can open doors that normally stay closed — our Gorey Masonic Lodge visit is a perfect example.
⏰ Allow longer than the map suggests.
Wexford is built for "we'll just stop here for ten minutes" moments that somehow become an hour.
💎 Mix famous and obscure.
Our ideal Wexford day is one well-known stop, one beach and at least one place we'd never heard of before.

Common questions
County Wexford FAQ
What are the best beaches in Wexford?
Curracloe is the famous one, but our own Wexford story also includes Duncannon, Blackwater and Dollar Bay. Rosslare and Ballymoney are worth knowing too. The best choice depends on whether you want a big classic strand, a family resort or somewhere quieter.
Was Saving Private Ryan filmed in Wexford?
Yes. The opening D-Day scenes were filmed at Curracloe and adjoining Ballinesker Beach. It's one of Wexford's best-known film connections.
What should I visit in Wexford besides beaches?
We'd look at Hook Lighthouse, Johnstown Castle, the Kennedy Homestead and Wells House. For something more unusual, keep an eye on heritage openings and our Hidden Ireland collection.
Is Wexford good for a weekend?
Absolutely. A weekend gives you enough time to combine coast and heritage without racing around the county. Pick one area each day rather than trying to cross Wexford repeatedly.
Have you visited Seal Rescue Ireland?
Not yet! It is on our list, but we haven't personally visited, so we're keeping it in our future-Wexford section until we do.
