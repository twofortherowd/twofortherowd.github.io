❤️ Our Gibraltar Experience

A place that makes no sense — and is brilliant because of it

"There is genuinely nowhere else on earth like Gibraltar. It is simultaneously completely British and unmistakably Mediterranean. You walk down a street with red phone boxes and a Marks & Spencer and then you look up and there is an enormous limestone rock with monkeys on it and Africa visible across the water. It is completely mad and completely wonderful."

I had been to Gibraltar before — once arriving by bus from Portugal, crossing the border on foot with the Rock filling the entire horizon ahead of me. But the 2024 visit with Podge and friends from Fuengirola was the most complete experience of the place. We got the bus from Fuengirola — about two hours along the Costa del Sol — crossed into Gibraltar on foot at the border and arranged a guided tour of the Upper Rock for the day.

The tour covered everything — the macaques on the Upper Rock, St Michael's Cave lit up in extraordinary blues, the Great Siege Tunnels carved through the limestone, Europa Point at the very southern tip of Europe with the coast of Morocco visible across the Strait, and the views from the top of the Rock over Spain, the Mediterranean and the Atlantic simultaneously. It is one of the most geographically dramatic viewpoints in the world.

And then there is Main Street — a pedestrianised British shopping street in the sunshine of southern Spain, complete with Gibraltar police in traditional uniforms, red phone boxes, British pubs and Marks & Spencer. The cultural collision never gets less surreal, no matter how many times you see it.

⭐ Best Things to Do in Gibraltar
🐒
1. Meet the Barbary Macaques

Europe's only wild primates, roaming the Upper Rock in troops and completely unbothered by humans. Watch respectfully from a distance — bags zipped, food out of sight. See the Monkey Rule below before you go.

🦇
2. Visit St Michael's Cave

A spectacular natural cavern inside the Rock, dramatically lit in blues and purples, with stalactites and stalagmites forming over millennia. Used as a concert venue — the acoustics are extraordinary.

⚔️
3. Walk the Great Siege Tunnels

Military tunnels carved directly into the limestone during the Great Siege (1779–1783). An extraordinary feat of engineering and a vivid sense of what the Rock's defenders endured.

🌍
4. Stand at Europa Point

The southernmost tip of Gibraltar, with Morocco visible 14km across the Strait on a clear day. The Ibrahim-al-Ibrahim Mosque, the lighthouse and the quiet awe of standing at the tip of Europe.

🇬🇧
5. Wander Main Street

Red phone boxes, Marks & Spencer, British pubs and Gibraltar police in traditional uniforms — all in the Mediterranean sunshine. Duty-free shopping is genuinely excellent too.

🏖️
6. Catalan Bay

A colourful little beach village on the eastern side of the Rock — fishing boats, small beach, a handful of restaurants. A completely different side of Gibraltar to Main Street.

✈️
7. The Airport Runway Area

Even with the Kingsway Tunnel now handling vehicle traffic, the runway itself is still one of the most extraordinary sights in Europe. See the section below for the full story.

🐒 Nicola & Podge's Monkey Rule

The macaques are one of Gibraltar's best sights — but they're wild animals, not props. Here's what we'd tell anyone going for the first time:

  • Don't feed them. It's illegal in Gibraltar and it makes them aggressive.
  • Keep bags zipped and pockets closed. They will investigate everything and they are much quicker than you.
  • Don't deliberately encourage one onto you. A monkey on your shoulder isn't a photo op — it's a wild animal that can bite and scratch.
  • Give them space. Watch them from a respectful distance, take your photos, and move on. Everyone wins.
  • Sunglasses off before you get too close. Ask anyone who's lost a pair to a macaque.
🪨 Our Upper Rock Tour

Everything you see on a full Gibraltar Rock tour

A guided Upper Rock tour is the easiest way to see all the main sights in a single day. Here's what a typical one covers — and what we saw on ours.

Stop 1
🐒 The Barbary Macaques — Upper Rock

The macaques live in troops across the Upper Rock and every tour includes a stop where you'll almost certainly see them. Legend has it that if the macaques ever leave Gibraltar, British sovereignty over the territory will end — so they are looked after extremely well.

Stop 2
🦇 St Michael's Cave

The blue and purple lighting transforms the cave into something almost theatrical. The scale of the cavern, the thousands of stalactites and the sheer age of the limestone formations make this one of the most atmospheric places on the Rock.

Stop 3
⚔️ Great Siege Tunnels

Cannon emplacements carved into the Rock during the Great Siege — an extraordinary feat of military engineering. Walking through them gives a vivid sense of what the defenders of Gibraltar endured through nearly four years of siege.

Stop 4
🌍 Europa Point — with Africa across the Strait

The southernmost tip of Gibraltar with panoramic views south to Morocco. The Ibrahim-al-Ibrahim Mosque — one of the largest in Europe — sits here alongside the lighthouse. On a clear day, seeing another continent 14km away is properly unforgettable.

🗺️ One Day in Gibraltar — Our Itinerary

Here's how we'd play a full day if we were doing it again. Start early, wear proper shoes, bring your passport and finish with a pint somewhere appropriately British.

Morning — Cross the border & head for the Rock

  1. Get there early: Bus from the Costa del Sol, then cross into Gibraltar on foot at the border (passport at the ready).
  2. Book your Rock tour: Head straight for the Upper Rock — either by cable car or on a guided minibus tour.

Late Morning — Macaques, cave & tunnels

  1. Barbary macaques: First stop on the Upper Rock — watch respectfully, remember the Monkey Rule above.
  2. St Michael's Cave: Give yourself a proper look around — it's more spectacular than the photos suggest.
  3. Great Siege Tunnels: Military history carved into the limestone. Fascinating.

Midday — Europa Point & Morocco views

  1. Europa Point: Head to the southern tip of Gibraltar. On a clear day you can see Africa across the Strait — one of the great "wait, really?" travel moments.
  2. Lunch: Head down towards the town for lunch — plenty of options along the harbour or near Main Street.

Afternoon — Main Street & the town

  1. Main Street: Wander the pedestrianised shopping street — duty-free is genuinely good, and the cultural collision never stops being surreal.
  2. Optional: Detour to Catalan Bay on the eastern side if you want a change of scene.

Late Afternoon — Runway & the essential pint

  1. The airport area: Even with the Kingsway Tunnel now taking vehicle traffic, walking past the runway is still one of the strangest airport arrangements in the world.
  2. The pint: Find a British pub, order something cold, celebrate having done Gibraltar properly.
  3. Cross back into Spain: Catch the bus back along the coast at your leisure.
🚌 How to Get to Gibraltar from the Costa del Sol

Bus from the Costa del Sol — that's what we'd do every time

From Fuengirola: Direct bus, around 2 hours along the coast. This is what we did — straightforward, inexpensive, drops you near the border.

From Málaga: Bus/coach services or organised excursions run daily. Around 2 hours 30.

From Marbella or Estepona: Even quicker — very manageable as a day trip. Under 2 hours.

Driving: If you're driving down from the Costa del Sol, park on the Spanish side in La Línea and walk across the border. Bringing a car into Gibraltar is more hassle than it's worth for a day trip — parking is limited and expensive, and you don't need one once you're inside.

✈️ The Famous Gibraltar Runway
✈️🚗
Yes, the runway crossing was real — here's what's changed

For decades, Winston Churchill Avenue — the main road into Gibraltar from Spain — famously crossed the active runway of Gibraltar International Airport at a level crossing. When a plane was landing or taking off, barriers came down, traffic stopped and the plane passed a matter of metres in front of you. It became one of the most iconic quirks of world travel.

What changed: Since the Kingsway Tunnel opened in 2023, vehicle traffic now goes underneath the runway rather than across it. The old crossing is no longer the main route for cars and buses coming into Gibraltar. Pedestrians and cyclists can still cross the runway at ground level when planes aren't landing — so the experience isn't completely gone — but the classic "bus stops for airliner" scene is now history.

Why it still matters: The runway itself is still there, still active, and Gibraltar Airport is still one of the most extraordinary flight approaches anywhere. Standing near the runway and watching a plane land or take off from a strip of land wedged between the Rock and the Mediterranean is worth doing. And now you know the story — the famous crossing was completely real, and it lasted almost a century before the tunnel finally replaced it.

🇬🇧🇪🇸 So... why is Gibraltar British?

The quick history explainer

It's one of the first questions everyone asks when they visit — and the answer is genuinely interesting rather than politically fraught, so here's the short version.

Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory on the southern tip of the Iberian Peninsula. It has been under British sovereignty since 1713, when it was ceded to Britain under the Treaty of Utrecht at the end of the War of the Spanish Succession. Spain has long maintained a territorial claim, but Gibraltarians themselves have repeatedly voted (overwhelmingly) to remain British in referendums.

For visitors, that unusual status is part of what makes Gibraltar feel so distinctive — British institutions, English signs, pounds sterling, red phone boxes and pubs, all surrounded by southern Spain and looking across at Morocco. It's the geography and the history bumping into each other in one square-ish mile at the end of Europe, and it works.

❓ Gibraltar FAQ
Is Gibraltar worth visiting?
Absolutely — Gibraltar is one of the most fascinatingly unique places in Europe. A British Overseas Territory on the southern tip of Spain, with views across to Morocco, Barbary macaques roaming the Upper Rock, military tunnels carved into limestone, red phone boxes on Main Street and a famous runway crossing. There is nowhere else on earth quite like it.
How do you get to Gibraltar from the Costa del Sol?
The easiest way is the direct bus from Fuengirola, Málaga, Marbella or Estepona — from Fuengirola it takes around two hours along the coast. You cross into Gibraltar on foot at the border (bring your passport) and the Rock is right in front of you. If driving, park on the Spanish side in La Línea and walk across.
Why is Gibraltar British?
Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory on the southern tip of the Iberian Peninsula and has been under British sovereignty since 1713, under the Treaty of Utrecht. Spain has long maintained a territorial claim, but Gibraltarians themselves have repeatedly voted overwhelmingly to remain British. For visitors, that unusual status is part of what makes Gibraltar feel so distinctive.
Does traffic still cross the Gibraltar airport runway?
Vehicle traffic no longer routinely crosses the runway — since the Kingsway Tunnel opened in 2023, cars and buses now go underneath the runway. But the famous runway level crossing is still very much part of Gibraltar's identity: pedestrians and cyclists can still cross the runway when planes aren't landing, and it remains one of the world's most surreal airport arrangements to experience in person.
What are the Barbary macaques in Gibraltar like?
The Barbary macaques are Europe's only wild primates — brilliant to see but genuinely wild animals. Keep bags zipped, don't feed them (it's illegal and makes them aggressive) and don't deliberately encourage one onto you. They're much quicker than you are. Watch respectfully from a distance and everyone wins.
Can you see Morocco from Gibraltar?
Yes — on a clear day from Europa Point, the southernmost tip of Gibraltar, you can see the coast of Morocco across the Strait of Gibraltar. Africa is only about 14 kilometres away. Standing at the tip of Europe looking across at another continent is a genuinely extraordinary feeling.
What is St Michael's Cave in Gibraltar?
St Michael's Cave is a spectacular natural cavern inside the Rock of Gibraltar — dramatically lit in blues and purples, with extraordinary stalactite and stalagmite formations. The cave is used as a concert and performance venue and the acoustics are extraordinary. It's one of the most atmospheric places inside the Rock.
