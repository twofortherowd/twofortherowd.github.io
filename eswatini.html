🌍 Eswatini at a Glance

Know before you go

A tiny kingdom with a very distinct identity

Eswatini is a landlocked country in southern Africa, bordered by South Africa on most sides and Mozambique to the northeast. At roughly 17,364 km², it is one of Africa’s smallest countries, yet its scenery changes dramatically from the cooler mountainous Highveld to the warmer, drier Lowveld.

📜 A Little History

From Swaziland to the Kingdom of Eswatini

The modern kingdom has deep roots in Swazi history and the monarchy remains central to the country’s national identity. Eswatini became independent from Britain on 6 September 1968 under King Sobhuza II.

Today the country operates under a system in which the monarchy, traditional structures and parliament all play roles in national government. The 2005 Constitution is the supreme law, while the Tinkhundla system forms the basis of the country’s political and administrative structure.

In April 2018, King Mswati III announced that the country would officially be known as the Kingdom of Eswatini rather than Swaziland. The name means, broadly, “land of the Swazis” and marked a return to the country’s indigenous name. We happened to visit in 2018, so it was a particularly interesting time to be there — you will still see the old Swaziland name on older signs, maps and souvenirs.

Traditional ceremonies, music, dance, crafts and community structures remain highly visible parts of life in Eswatini. For visitors, understanding that these traditions are part of a living contemporary society — rather than simply tourist attractions — makes travelling here far more rewarding.

🧭 Before You Go

Practical Eswatini travel information

💰 MoneyThe local currency is the lilangeni (plural: emalangeni), code SZL. It is pegged 1:1 to the South African rand, and South African rand is also legal tender in Eswatini. That makes a combined South Africa–Eswatini trip particularly straightforward for cash.
💬 LanguagesiSwati and English are the official languages. English is widely useful for travellers, but learning even a greeting or two in siSwati is a lovely way to connect with people.
🚗 Getting aroundDriving is on the left, as in South Africa. Eswatini fits naturally into an overland southern Africa itinerary, but remember that crossing the border means entering another sovereign country: check passport, visa and vehicle requirements before travelling.
🌦️ ClimateDo not assume the whole country has one climate. The mountainous Highveld is cooler and wetter, while the Lowveld is warmer and drier. Layers are useful, particularly around Mbabane and at altitude.
🔌 ElectricitySouth African-style plugs are commonly used, so the same adaptor can generally serve both parts of a combined trip. Check your accommodation and current standards before departure.
🤝 CultureEswatini has strong living cultural traditions. Dress and behave respectfully at community, royal and cultural sites, ask before photographing people, and follow the guidance of local hosts.

Travel rules can change. Check current entry, health and border requirements with official sources before departure.

🇸🇿 The Story

A small kingdom with a very big heart

The visit that moved us most on our entire southern Africa journey.

"We expected Eswatini to be a stopover. It became one of the most profound travel experiences we have ever had. The children at that school — the joy, the energy, the warmth — it puts everything into perspective. We think about that visit all the time."

We crossed into Eswatini from South Africa in 2018 with one night planned in Mbabane and a loose itinerary for the following day. What unfolded over those two days ended up being among the most memorable of our entire African adventure.

Mbabane is a small capital city set in the beautiful Mdimba Mountains — unhurried, friendly and genuinely charming in a way that big cities rarely are. The markets, the local restaurants, the mountain setting and the warmth of the people we met made an immediate impression.

Our cultural visit was extraordinary. We were introduced to aspects of Swazi traditions, crafts, music and community life by local people who generously shared their knowledge with us. Looking back, what mattered most was not treating the experience as a performance or a photo opportunity, but listening, asking questions respectfully and appreciating that we were visitors being welcomed into someone else’s community.

But nothing prepared us for the primary school visit. The children were incredible. The joy, the curiosity, the energy, the warmth they showed to two Irish people turning up in their school — it was one of those moments in travel that strips everything back and reminds you what actually matters. We left with full hearts and a perspective on our own lives that we have never quite lost.

Eswatini is small. It is landlocked. It is not on most travel bucket lists. It absolutely should be.

Common questions

Eswatini FAQ

Is Eswatini worth visiting?
Absolutely — Eswatini is one of Africa's most overlooked destinations. It is a small landlocked kingdom with stunning landscapes, rich Swazi culture, welcoming people and a depth of cultural experience that is very hard to find elsewhere. If you are travelling through southern Africa it is absolutely worth making time for.
What is Eswatini like?
Eswatini — formerly known as Swaziland — is a small landlocked kingdom in southern Africa, surrounded by South Africa and Mozambique. It has beautiful highland and lowland landscapes, a proud and distinct Swazi cultural identity, and some of the most genuine and warm people we have encountered anywhere in Africa.
How do you get to Eswatini?
The easiest way to visit Eswatini is as part of a southern Africa trip — it is very accessible by road from South Africa and makes a brilliant stop between Johannesburg and the Kruger National Park or the Mozambique coast. We visited as part of a South Africa trip in 2018 and it was one of the highlights of the entire journey.
What is Mbabane like?
Mbabane is the capital of Eswatini — a small, characterful city set in the Mdimba Mountains with a relaxed atmosphere, good local markets and a genuine sense of Swazi culture and identity. It has a warmth and character that makes it a very pleasant base for exploring the kingdom.
What cultural experiences can you have in Eswatini?
Eswatini offers some of the most authentic cultural experiences in southern Africa — visits to traditional Swazi villages and homesteads, traditional crafts, music and dance, and the opportunity to spend time with local communities in a way that is respectful and genuinely enriching. We spent time with a local tribe and visited a primary school — both were among the most moving experiences of our time in Africa.
