Grand squares, late nights and three unforgettable excursions on Nicola's summer 2005 Interrail adventure.

Our Kraków story

History by day, Main Square by night

Kraków was another stop on my summer 2005 Interrail trip with friends. It had that wonderful combination of an incredibly beautiful historic centre and a lively, easy-going atmosphere.

We spent plenty of time eating and drinking around the enormous Main Market Square, surrounded by the Cloth Hall, church towers and constant activity. It was exactly the sort of place where you could wander without a plan and still feel that you were in the heart of the city.

We also used Kraków as the base for three very different excursions. Auschwitz-Birkenau was profoundly moving and impossible to forget. The chambers and underground spaces of Wieliczka Salt Mine were astonishing, while our trip to Wadowice brought us to the birthplace and family home of Pope John Paul II.

Kraków gave the trip its lighter evenings and some of its most serious moments. That contrast is a huge part of why the city stayed with me.

Nicola's memory

“Auschwitz was very moving. Then we had those brilliant evenings eating and drinking on Kraków's Main Square—it was a trip of real contrasts.”

Kraków & day trips

What to see

Kraków and its essential excursions

Give the city proper time before travelling beyond it. Kraków's centre, Kazimierz and Wawel easily fill two rewarding days.

We visited

Main Market Square

Rynek Główny is Kraków's magnificent centrepiece. See St Mary's Basilica, browse the Cloth Hall and stay into the evening when the restaurants and cafés bring the square to life.

Return by day and night
City essential

Wawel Hill & Royal Castle

Walk the Royal Route to Wawel Hill for the cathedral, castle courtyards and views above the Vistula. Interior exhibitions require separate tickets and deserve advance planning.

Allow half a day
City essential

Kazimierz

The historic Jewish quarter rewards unhurried exploration. Its synagogues, museums, squares, cafés and restaurants reveal a district with deep history and a strong present-day identity.

Explore respectfully
Our day trip

Auschwitz-Birkenau

A profoundly moving visit to the former German Nazi concentration and extermination camp. This is a place of remembrance: allow emotional space, follow site guidance and avoid treating it as an ordinary attraction.

Reserve well ahead
Our day trip

Wieliczka Salt Mine

Descend through chambers, tunnels and chapels carved in salt. The standard route reaches 135 metres below ground and involves roughly 800 steps, although the return is by lift.

Cool underground
Our day trip

Wadowice

Karol Wojtyła—the future Pope John Paul II—was born here in 1920. His family home is now a museum, making Wadowice especially meaningful for visitors interested in his life.

Around 50 km from Kraków

Eat & drink

What to try in Kraków

Pierogi

Poland's famous dumplings come with savoury or sweet fillings. Standard pierogi contain wheat, so coeliac travellers need a dedicated gluten-free option.

Obwarzanek krakowski

The twisted bread rings sold from street carts are a symbol of Kraków—but they contain gluten and are unsuitable for coeliacs.

Zapiekanka

Kazimierz is famous for this open-faced baguette loaded with toppings. Look for a venue offering safe gluten-free bread and separate preparation.

Gluten-free planning

Kraków has dedicated options, but always confirm cross-contamination. Carry the phrase: Mam celiakię. Muszę jeść bez glutenu.

Practical questions

Kraków travel FAQ

How many days do you need in Kraków?

Allow two full days for the city. Four or five days work better if you also want to visit Auschwitz-Birkenau, Wieliczka Salt Mine and Wadowice.

Can you visit Auschwitz-Birkenau from Kraków?

Yes. It is one of the most common excursions from Kraków. Reserve in advance, follow all site guidance and allow time afterwards to process an emotionally difficult visit.

Can Auschwitz and Wieliczka be visited on the same day?

Combined tours exist, but we would not recommend rushing them. The sites are very different and both take several hours; Auschwitz especially deserves an unhurried and respectful visit.

Was Pope John Paul II born in Kraków?

No. Karol Wojtyła was born in Wadowice in 1920. He later moved to Kraków, studied there and eventually became Archbishop of Kraków before his election as pope.

Is Wieliczka Salt Mine physically demanding?

The standard tourist route includes roughly 800 steps, with about 380 near the beginning, and descends to 135 metres. The underground temperature is around 17–18°C, so wear comfortable shoes and bring a layer.

Is Kraków suitable for coeliac travellers?

Yes, with preparation. Dedicated gluten-free venues exist, but traditional pierogi, obwarzanek and zapiekanka contain gluten. Explain that you have coeliac disease and confirm cross-contamination procedures.
