Our Warsaw story

A city I absolutely loved

Warsaw was part of my Interrail trip with friends in the summer of 2005, and I absolutely loved it. What struck me most was not simply how beautiful the historic centre looked, but the extraordinary story behind it.

Known as the Phoenix City, Warsaw rose again after the devastation of the Second World War. We took a city tour that helped us understand just how much had been lost—and how carefully the city had reconstructed its historic heart.

We explored Castle Square and the Royal Castle, visited the Museum of Warsaw and travelled out to Wilanów Palace and its gardens. We also visited the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes, a powerful and sobering part of the city's story.

It was one of those places that completely surpassed whatever expectations I had before arriving. Warsaw felt proud, resilient and full of life. Nearly two decades later, it remains one of the cities from that Interrail adventure that I remember most fondly.

What to see

The Warsaw essentials

The places that tell the story of Warsaw—from the reconstructed Old Town to royal residences and the history of the Jewish community.

We visited

Old Town & Castle Square

Warsaw's reconstructed historic centre is the natural starting point. Walk through Castle Square, follow the cobbled lanes and find the Mermaid statue in the Old Town Market Square.

Allow 2–3 hours
We visited

Royal Castle

Standing at the entrance to the Old Town, the Royal Castle was rebuilt after wartime destruction. Its restored interiors reveal Warsaw's royal and political history.

Book ahead in busy periods
We visited

Museum of Warsaw

Set across historic houses on the Market Square, the museum is an excellent place to understand the people, objects and events that shaped the capital.

Perfect history introduction
We visited

Wilanów Palace & Gardens

A magnificent royal residence beyond the centre, Wilanów rewards the journey with ornate rooms, landscaped gardens and a completely different side of Warsaw.

Allow half a day
We visited the monument

Monument to the Ghetto Heroes & POLIN

The monument was one of the most moving stops on our 2005 tour. The award-winning POLIN Museum now stands opposite and traces a thousand years of Jewish life in Poland.

Allow several hours
Add to your visit

Royal Route & Łazienki Park

Follow Krakowskie Przedmieście and Nowy Świat before slowing down among palaces, gardens and the Chopin Monument in lovely Łazienki Park.

Excellent on foot

Eat & drink

What to try in Warsaw

Pierogi

Poland's famous dumplings come with savoury or sweet fillings. Traditional fillings are not gluten-free, so coeliac travellers must ask specifically about safe alternatives.

Żurek

A distinctive sour rye soup, often served with sausage and egg. It normally contains gluten because of the fermented rye starter.

Milk bars

These no-frills cafeterias are a Warsaw institution and offer traditional Polish dishes at modest prices.

Gluten-free planning

Warsaw has dedicated gluten-free choices, but always confirm preparation and cross-contamination. Carry the Polish phrase: Mam celiakię. Muszę jeść bez glutenu.

Practical questions

Warsaw travel FAQ

How many days do you need in Warsaw?

Two full days let you cover the Old Town, Royal Route and one major museum. Choose three days if you want to add Wilanów Palace without rushing.

Why is Warsaw called the Phoenix City?

Much of Warsaw was destroyed during the Second World War. Its historic centre was painstakingly reconstructed, becoming a powerful symbol of the city's survival and rebirth.

Is Warsaw easy to explore without a car?

Yes. Central Warsaw is walkable, while trams, buses and metro services connect the wider city. Bus 180 is particularly useful for sights along the Royal Route towards Wilanów.

Is Warsaw suitable for a gluten-free traveller?

Yes, with planning. Warsaw has dedicated gluten-free restaurants and options, but traditional pierogi and żurek usually contain gluten. Always explain that you have coeliac disease and ask about cross-contamination.

Is Warsaw worth visiting as well as Kraków?

Absolutely. Kraków and Warsaw tell very different stories. Warsaw's reconstruction, modern energy, museums and royal sights justify at least two full days.
