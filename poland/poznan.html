Our Poznań story

The gorgeous one from our Interrail trip

Poznań was part of my summer 2005 Interrail trip with friends, and my strongest memory is simply what a gorgeous city it was. The centre felt colourful and welcoming, with grand façades and a much more relaxed pace than some of Europe's better-known city-break destinations.

The Old Market Square was the star. Its rows of brightly painted merchant houses and handsome Town Hall made it the kind of place where wandering around was an activity in itself. It was beautiful without feeling stiff or overly polished.

Poznań also felt easy and affordable—a proper lived-in Polish city rather than somewhere existing only for tourists. That balance of history, colour and atmosphere is what made it so memorable.

More than twenty years later, we are planning to return for the 2026 Christmas markets. This time Podge will get to see the city too, dressed in lights and hopefully every bit as lovely as I remember it.

What to see

The Poznań essentials

Poznań's story stretches from the beginnings of the Polish state to one of the country's liveliest modern city centres.

We visited

Old Market Square

Colourful merchant houses frame one of Poland's most attractive squares. Wander the lanes, sit outside with a drink and see the Town Hall's mechanical goats clatter their horns.

Goats at 12 pm and 3 pm
City essential

Town Hall & Museum

The Renaissance Town Hall dominates the square and houses the Museum of the History of the City of Poznań. Its celebrated goats appear high on the tower each day.

Arrive before the goats
City essential

Cathedral Island

Ostrów Tumski leads back to the beginnings of Poland. Poznań Cathedral dates to the tenth century and contains the burial places of Poland's first rulers.

Allow half a day
City essential

Porta Posnania

This modern heritage centre explains Cathedral Island and the formation of the Polish state using multimedia rather than a traditional collection of objects.

Excellent historical context
Add to your visit

Imperial Castle & Enigma Centre

Explore the monumental former Imperial Castle, then discover the Polish mathematicians whose work on Enigma helped change the course of the Second World War.

Strong rainy-day choice
Add to your visit

Lake Malta

A large recreational lake east of the centre with walking and cycling routes. It offers breathing room after a day among museums and historic streets.

Best in fair weather

Eat & drink

What to try in Poznań

St Martin's croissant

Rogal świętomarciński is Poznań's famous pastry, filled with white poppy seed, nuts and dried fruit. The protected traditional version contains gluten.

Pyry z gzikiem

A Greater Poland classic of potatoes served with seasoned cottage cheese. The basic dish can be naturally gluten-free, but always confirm ingredients and preparation.

Pierogi

Traditional Polish dumplings are widely available, although the standard dough contains wheat. Seek a dedicated gluten-free version if you are coeliac.

Gluten-free planning

Research safe venues before arriving and ask about cross-contamination. Carry the phrase: Mam celiakię. Muszę jeść bez glutenu.

Practical questions

Poznań travel FAQ

How many days do you need in Poznań?

Two full days suit a first visit. Add a third for more museums, Lake Malta, Citadel Park or a slower and more relaxed break.

When do the Poznań Town Hall goats appear?

The mechanical goats emerge from the Town Hall tower daily at noon and again at 3 pm, clattering their horns twelve times.

Is Poznań worth visiting?

Absolutely. It has a beautiful historic centre, important early Polish history, excellent food and a more relaxed atmosphere than many better-known European cities.

How do you travel from Poznań Airport to the centre?

Day buses 159 and 148 connect the airport with the city, while route 222 operates at night. The airport is only about five kilometres from central Poznań.

Are the 2026 Poznań Christmas-market dates confirmed?

We are planning our return for the 2026 markets, but exact dates and locations should be checked on the official tourism website before booking. Recent editions have used the International Fair, Plac Wolności and Old Market Square.

Is Poznań suitable for coeliac travellers?

Yes, with advance research. Remember that the protected St Martin's croissant and standard pierogi contain gluten. Always explain that you have coeliac disease and check cross-contamination procedures.
