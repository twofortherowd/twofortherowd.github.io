County Cavan might not be the first place that springs to mind when you're planning an Irish adventure, and that's exactly why it's worth discovering!

Famous for its lakes, rolling drumlins and peaceful woodlands, it's a county where slowing down is part of the attraction. You won't find tourist crowds at every turn. Instead there are ancient tombs, secluded lakeshores, historic castles, welcoming towns and walking trails that feel a world away from everyday life.

From the prehistoric landscape of the Cavan Burren to the quiet waters of Lough Oughter, Cavan is brilliant for anyone who loves nature, history and the lesser-known corners of Ireland.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Cavan, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🛂 Popping over the border?

West Cavan sits right beside County Fermanagh. The Marble Arch Caves and the Cuilcagh Boardwalk are in Fermanagh, not Cavan, but they make a great cross-border day trip. There are no border checks, but prices there are in pounds sterling (£) and speed limits are in miles per hour.

The Burren, lakes & castles
Geopark · Free

🪨 Cavan Burren Park

Near Blacklion in north-west Cavan, this is one of the county's most remarkable landscapes: limestone, ancient woodland, odd rock formations and prehistoric tombs, including the Calf House Dolmen.

Marked trails lead you between the monuments and some big views over Lough MacNean. It's part of the Cuilcagh Lakelands UNESCO Global Geopark.

Why visit? Archaeology, geology, walking and hidden gems.

🎟️ Admission is free, with seasonal access hours. Check the official website before you go.

Forest park · Lakes

🌲 Killykeen Forest Park

Woodland paths and lakeside views on the shores of Lough Oughter, with marked routes from short, easy loops to the longer Killykeen Way.

Why visit? Families, walking, cycling and relaxation.

Island castle

🏰 Clough Oughter Castle

A round tower castle on a tiny island in Lough Oughter, one of the most picturesque ruins in Ireland. It was an O'Reilly stronghold and saw dramatic events in the 1640s and 1650s.

Why visit? Castles, photography and history.

ℹ️ Enjoy it from the lakeshore or from the water on an organised trip. Don't assume you can land or go inside.
Museum

🏛️ Cavan County Museum

In Ballyjamesduff, covering archaeology, rural life and political history. Outside there's a remarkable replica First World War trench.

Why visit? History, culture and educational visits.

Lake system

🛶 Lough Oughter

A maze of waterways, islands and wooded shores, and one of Cavan's most distinctive landscapes. Enjoy designated walks, fishing and organised water activities.

Why visit? Nature, wildlife and peaceful scenery.

🌊 Lakes can have sudden depth changes and underwater hazards, and there's little supervised access.
Estate ruin

🏚️ Castle Saunderson

An atmospheric ruined castle in wooded grounds near Belturbet. The estate is also home to Scouting Ireland's international scout centre.

Why visit? Historic landscapes and photography.

ℹ️ Check which areas are open to the public before visiting.

Around the county
Forests, towns & ancient sites
Forest park · Family

🌳 Dún a Rí Forest Park

River views, woodland trails and estate history near Kingscourt.

Why visit? Families, nature and woodland walks.

Woods · Lake

🌲 Deerpark Forest, Virginia & Lough Ramor

Woodland and lakeside walks around the welcoming small town of Virginia. Lough Ramor is especially pretty on calm mornings and autumn evenings.

Why visit? Woodland walks and short breaks.

Town · Waterways

⛵ Belturbet

A historic town on the River Erne, a hub for boating and the surrounding lakes.

Why visit? Waterways, history and a relaxed stop.

Monastic site

🗼 Drumlane Abbey & Round Tower

Medieval church ruins and a round tower beside a lake near Milltown. Peaceful and very atmospheric.

Why visit? Monastic history and photography.

Myth · Source

💧 The Shannon Pot

A quiet pool on the slopes of the Cuilcagh Mountains, traditionally the source of the River Shannon, Ireland's longest river.

Why visit? Folklore, nature and unusual landmarks.

County town

⛪ Cavan Town

A good base with shops, restaurants and Cavan Cathedral. Handy for an overnight stay.

Why visit? Local culture, food and a convenient base.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

Legend

💧 Sionann and the well of wisdom

The goddess Sionann went looking for the wisdom of a magical well, surrounded by hazel trees whose nuts held all knowledge. The waters rose up and swept her away, creating the river that bears her name.

The Shannon Pot is the perfect place to tell the story.

Archaeology

🪨 The ancient stones of the Cavan Burren

Tombs, stone forts and rock art show that people lived and buried their dead in this limestone landscape thousands of years ago.

History

🛡️ The O'Reillys of East Breifne

The O'Reillys ruled East Breifne, a medieval Gaelic territory that broadly matches modern Cavan. Their story runs through the county's castles and towns.

History

🏰 Clough Oughter in the 1640s

In 1641 the Protestant Bishop William Bedell was held prisoner in the castle. Owen Roe O'Neill, the Confederate general, died there in 1649, and in 1653 it was one of the last strongholds to surrender to Cromwell's forces.

History

👑 The Kingdom of Breifne

Much of Cavan lay within the old kingdom of Breifne, a fascinating introduction to medieval Gaelic Ireland and the families who shaped it.

Common questions
County Cavan FAQ
Are the Marble Arch Caves in Cavan?
No. The Marble Arch Caves and the Cuilcagh Boardwalk (the 'Stairway to Heaven') are just over the border in County Fermanagh. They're part of the same cross-border geopark and make a great day trip from west Cavan, but prices there are in pounds sterling.
Is Cavan Burren Park free?
Yes, admission is free, but access hours are seasonal. Check the park's website before you go.
Can you visit Clough Oughter Castle?
You can see it from the lakeshore or on an organised trip on the water. Don't assume you can land on the island or go inside.
How many days do you need in Cavan?
Two or three days give you a good introduction to the lakes, forests and ancient sites.
