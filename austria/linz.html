Linz —
Austria's underrated city

Linz is Austria's most underrated destination. It doesn't have the immediate fame of Vienna or Salzburg, but it has something interesting happening. The Danube runs through it, the Hauptplatz is one of Austria's largest enclosed main squares, the Ars Electronica museum is world-class and the food culture is thriving. Linz has undergone a genuine cultural transformation and visiting rewards those who venture beyond Austria's famous triangle.

💫 About Linz

The city Austria forgot, until it transformed itself.

Linz doesn't get the tourist attention of Vienna, Salzburg or Innsbruck. Most visitors pass through or skip it entirely. That's changing and for good reason. The city has undergone a genuine cultural transformation over the last two decades and has become genuinely interesting to visit.

The Hauptplatz — the main square — is one of the largest in Europe at 8,000 square metres. It is surrounded by baroque facades and at its centre is the Trinity Column, erected in 1723 as a thanksgiving for the city's survival during plagues and wars. It is a genuinely beautiful square and a proper gathering place — locals sit here in cafés and on the steps, actually using the space rather than just passing through.

The Ars Electronica museum is one of the world's leading museums of digital culture. It sounds niche but it's genuinely fascinating even if you're not a tech person — the exhibitions explore how technology shapes our world, which is relevant to everyone. It is housed in a striking glass building on the Danube.

The Danube runs through Linz. There's a walking path along the riverbank, cyclists everywhere, real life happening. The river is the city's lifeblood in a way it might not be in other Austrian cities.

Linzer Torte is the famous cake from here — almond cake with raspberry jam. You'll find it in cafés and pastry shops throughout the city. It is genuinely delicious.

What to see in Linz

Hauptplatz — the main square, UNESCO World Heritage Site, beautiful to sit and watch. Ars Electronica — world-class digital culture museum. Lentos Art Museum — modern art on the Danube riverbank. St. Mary's Cathedral — the highest cathedral in Austria. Danube River walks — the riverside path is pleasant for walking. Mariendom Church — Gothic cathedral.

Practical information

Getting there: Linz has an airport served by some European destinations. It's also easily reached by train from Vienna (2 hours) or Munich (2.5 hours). Public transport: The city centre is compact and walkable. A single ticket costs €2.50. Where to stay: Around the Hauptplatz is central but pricey. Across the river in quieter neighbourhoods is cheaper. Budget €40-60/night for hostels, €60-100 for mid-range hotels. Best time to visit: Spring and autumn. Summers are warm. Winters can be cold and grey but the Christmas markets are good.

❓ FAQ
Is Linz worth visiting?
Yes. It's underrated and experiencing genuine cultural transformation. The Hauptplatz is beautiful, the Ars Electronica is genuinely fascinating, the Danube offers pleasant walks and the food scene is thriving. It's different from Vienna, Salzburg and Innsbruck — which is exactly why it's worth visiting.
How much time should you spend in Linz?
1-2 days. One day covers the Hauptplatz, a Danube walk and the Ars Electronica museum. Two days gives you time to sit in cafés, explore quieter neighbourhoods and really get a sense of what Linz is becoming.
Should you visit the Ars Electronica museum?
Yes. Even if you're not interested in digital culture specifically, the exhibitions are thought-provoking and beautifully presented. Entry is €13-15. It's one of the best uses of a couple of hours in Linz.
Is Linz expensive?
Budget €35-55/night for hostels, €55-90 for mid-range hotels. Food is reasonable — €8-12 for street food, €15-28 for casual meals. Coffee at a café costs €2-3. Linzer Torte costs €3-5 per slice. Less expensive than Vienna.
Can you day trip to Linz from Vienna?
Yes — it's 2 hours by train from Vienna. You could do it as a long day trip but you'd have less than 6 hours in the city. Better to stay at least one night if possible to actually experience Linz.
