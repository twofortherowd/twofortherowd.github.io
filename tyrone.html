County Tyrone —
Ancient Stones, Starry Skies & Spectacular Sperrins

Where beautiful mountains, remarkable history and some of Ireland's darkest skies create unforgettable adventures.

If you fancy escaping the busy tourist routes for a county full of history, mountains and outdoor adventures, Tyrone is a brilliant choice.

In the heart of Ulster, it's known for the Sperrin Mountains, peaceful forests and remarkable ancient monuments, and it's home to one of Ireland's most engaging heritage attractions, the Ulster American Folk Park.

And if you've ever fancied an evening under a sky full of stars, Tyrone has some of the darkest skies on the island.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: this is a researched guide rather than a diary of our own visits. When we explore and film in Tyrone, our own videos and first-hand tips will be added here, just like on our other county guides.

🛂 Crossing from the Republic?

There are no border checks, but prices are in pounds sterling (£), speed limits are in miles per hour, and it's worth checking your phone plan and car insurance cover before you go.

Start here
Folk park, Sperrins & ancient stones
Open-air museum

🏡 Ulster American Folk Park

Near Omagh, this open-air museum follows emigrants from Ulster to North America, through cottages, a ship and the streets of an American town. Costumed guides bring it all to life.

Why visit? Emigration history, families and education.

Mountains

⛰️ The Sperrin Mountains

Remote, rolling uplands stretching across Tyrone into County Derry, with quiet roads, walking routes and huge skies.

Why visit? Nature, hiking and photography.

⛰️ The uplands are exposed and the weather changes fast. Go prepared.
Prehistory · Free

⭕ Beaghmore Stone Circles

Seven Bronze Age stone circles, stone rows and cairns in a remote, atmospheric bogland setting.

Why visit? Archaeology and ancient mysteries.

Stargazing

✨ OM Dark Sky Park & Observatory

In Davagh Forest, an observatory and dark sky park where you can learn about astronomy and, on a clear night, see the Milky Way.

Why visit? Stargazing, science and families.

☁️ Clear skies are never guaranteed. Cloud and moonlight affect what you'll see.
Forest park

🌲 Gortin Glen Forest Park

Woodland walks, a forest drive and mountain views near Omagh.

Why visit? Walking, families and photography.

Visitor centre

🌿 An Creagán

A visitor and cultural centre near Creggan, with bog trails and exhibitions about the landscape and heritage of the area.

Why visit? Culture, countryside and families.

Around the county
O'Neill country & the east
Heritage · Views

👑 Hill of The O'Neill & Ranfurly House

In Dungannon, the hill where the O'Neills, the most powerful Gaelic dynasty in Ulster, had their stronghold. Ranfurly House tells their story, and the views are excellent.

Why visit? Gaelic history and views.

Inauguration site

🪨 Tullaghoge Fort

The hilltop enclosure near Cookstown where the O'Neill chiefs were inaugurated for centuries.

Why visit? Gaelic history and atmosphere.

National Trust · Linen

⚙️ Wellbrook Beetling Mill

A water-powered mill where linen was hammered to a sheen, a reminder of Ulster's great linen industry.

Why visit? Industrial heritage.

📅 Check current visitor arrangements.
Park

🦆 Dungannon Park

A lake, walking paths and green space.

Why visit? Families and relaxation.

Estate · Cycling

🚲 Blessingbourne Estate

A country estate near Fivemiletown with walking and mountain-bike trails.

Why visit? Cycling, nature and countryside.

Ancestral home

🇺🇸 Grant Ancestral Homestead

Near Ballygawley, the restored farmstead of the ancestors of US President Ulysses S. Grant.

Why visit? Emigration history and family roots.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

History

👑 The O'Neills

The O'Neills ruled Tír Eoghain, which gave Tyrone its name, for centuries. Their chiefs were inaugurated at Tullaghoge, and Hugh O'Neill led the Nine Years' War before the Flight of the Earls in 1607.

Archaeology

⭕ The mystery of Beaghmore

The circles were uncovered from the peat in the 1940s. Nobody is sure what they were for, though theories range from astronomy to ritual.

History

🚢 Leaving Ulster

Hundreds of thousands left Ulster for America in the 1700s and 1800s. Their stories, from the cottages they left to the towns they built, are at the heart of the Folk Park.

Folklore

⛰️ Sperrin stories

The mountains are rich in local lore, from fairy forts to stories of gold in the hills. Gold really has been found in the Sperrins!

A car is strongly recommended.

Common questions
County Tyrone FAQ
Where's the best place to stargaze in Tyrone?
Davagh Forest, home to the OM Dark Sky Park and Observatory. Go on a clear, moonless night.
Can you visit Beaghmore Stone Circles for free?
Yes, it's an open site. Wear sturdy shoes, as it's a remote bogland setting.
How long do you need at the Ulster American Folk Park?
Allow at least half a day. There's a lot to see, inside and out.
What currency is used in Tyrone?
Pounds sterling (£). There are no border checks if you're coming from Donegal or Monaghan.
Have Two for the Row'd visited these places?
