Dublin is the place most visitors start, and plenty never get much further. We get it: there's enough history, music and good pubs in the city centre to fill a week.

But County Dublin is more than the capital. Within half an hour on the DART you can be on a cliff path in Howth, wandering Malahide Castle's gardens or watching swimmers brave the Forty Foot.

We've filmed in Dublin a lot, from 1916 walking tours to medieval crypts, so this guide mixes our own videos with the research to help you plan the rest.

🧭 A quick note from Nicola and Podge: the places we've filmed ourselves are marked We filmed here, and our videos are in their own section below. Everything else is researched, and we'll keep adding to it.

🚧 Dublin Castle and the Chester Beatty are closed

The Dublin Castle campus is closed to the public for Ireland's EU Presidency and expects to reopen to visitors in January 2027. The Chester Beatty in its grounds is closed until the end of December 2026. Check dublincastle.ie before you plan around either.

Dublin city: history, cathedrals & museums
Heritage · Book ahead

📜 Trinity College & the Book of Kells

The Book of Kells, a ninth-century illuminated gospel book, is displayed at Trinity College, along with the soaring Long Room of the Old Library.

The Old Library is heading into a major conservation project. The Long Room stays open until the end of 2027, after which the Book of Kells moves to a new home on campus.

Why visit? Irish history, manuscripts and one of the world's great library interiors.

🎟️ Timed tickets sell out in busy periods, so book through Visit Trinity.
Visit Trinity ↗
History · Book ahead

⛓️ Kilmainham Gaol

A former prison where many of the leaders of the 1916 Rising were held and executed. The guided tour is one of the most powerful history experiences in Ireland.

Why visit? Irish independence history and the story behind modern Ireland.

🎟️ Entry is by guided tour, and tickets are released in advance and go quickly. Don't just turn up.

Cathedral · We filmed here

⛪ Christ Church Cathedral

Nearly a thousand years old, with one of the largest medieval crypts in Britain and Ireland. Down in the crypt you'll find some unexpected residents, including the famous 'cat and rat'.

Why visit? Medieval Dublin, architecture and a properly atmospheric crypt.

Cathedral · We filmed here

🍀 St Patrick's Cathedral

Ireland's largest cathedral, beside a well where tradition says St Patrick baptised converts. Jonathan Swift, author of Gulliver's Travels, was dean here and is buried inside.

Why visit? History, architecture and a peaceful park next door.

Free · Museum

🏺 National Museum of Ireland – Archaeology

On Kildare Street, home to prehistoric gold, Viking Dublin, the Ardagh Chalice and the eerie bog bodies of the Kingship and Sacrifice exhibition.

Why visit? Rainy days, ancient Ireland and a fantastic free museum.

🎟️ General admission is free.
National Museum ↗
Museum · We filmed here

📚 MoLI – Museum of Literature Ireland

In a beautiful Georgian house on St Stephen's Green, MoLI brings Joyce, Wilde, Beckett and Ireland's other great writers to life, with a lovely courtyard garden out the back.

Why visit? Book lovers and slow, rainy afternoons.

Museum

🚢 EPIC The Irish Emigration Museum

An interactive museum in the vaults of CHQ in the Docklands, telling the stories of the millions of people who left Ireland and what they did around the world.

Why visit? Families, interactive exhibits and anyone tracing Irish roots.

Landmark · We filmed here

🏛️ The Custom House

James Gandon's 18th-century masterpiece on the Liffey, burned during the War of Independence in 1921 and restored. Most people walk straight past it, and it deserves a proper look.

Why visit? Architecture, riverside walks and revolutionary history.

Visitor attraction

🍺 Guinness Storehouse

Seven floors of brewing history at St James's Gate, finishing in the Gravity Bar with a pint and a 360° view over the city.

Why visit? First-time visitors and anyone who wants the views.

Beyond the city centre
The coast, castles & Phoenix Park
Coast · Walking

🌊 Howth & the Cliff Walk

A fishing village at the end of the DART line, with a busy harbour, seafood and a cliff path looping around the head with views over Dublin Bay.

Why visit? Easy escape from the city, sea air and seafood.

⚠️ Stay on the marked path. The cliff edges are unfenced in places and dangerous in wind or wet weather.
Castle · Gardens

🏰 Malahide Castle & Gardens

A medieval castle that was home to the Talbot family for around 800 years, with walled gardens and parkland beside a seaside village.

Why visit? Families, gardens and an easy DART day out.

Seaside · Swimming spot

🏊 Dún Laoghaire, Sandycove & the Forty Foot

Walk Dún Laoghaire's East Pier, then follow the coast to Sandycove and the Forty Foot, where people have been swimming year-round for well over a century. James Joyce opens Ulysses at the Martello tower here.

Why visit? Coastal walks, sea swimming and literary history.

🌊 The Forty Foot is deep-water swimming off rocks, with no lifeguard. Only go in if you're a confident swimmer and conditions are calm.
Village · Views

⛰️ Dalkey & Killiney Hill

Dalkey is a charming village with a medieval castle, and a short climb up Killiney Hill gives one of the best views in Ireland, across Killiney Bay to the Wicklow Mountains.

Why visit? Views, independent shops and a seaside afternoon.

Park · Family

🦌 Phoenix Park & Dublin Zoo

One of Europe's largest enclosed city parks, with wild fallow deer, Áras an Uachtaráin (the President's residence) and Dublin Zoo, which has been here since 1831.

Why visit? Families, cycling and a green break from the city.

🦌 The deer are wild. Don't feed them, and keep well back during the autumn rut and the summer fawning season.

Hidden gems & quieter corners
Church · We filmed here

🕯️ St Audoen's Church

Dublin's oldest parish church still in use, around 800 years old, tucked behind High Street. A quiet, fascinating stop between the two cathedrals.

Walking tour · We did this

🇮🇪 1916 Rebellion Walking Tour

Historian Lorcan Collins leads this walk through the streets of the Easter Rising. We joined him and it brought 1916 to life in a way no museum could. See our video above.

Museum

🏠 The Little Museum of Dublin

A quirky, much-loved museum of 20th-century Dublin in a Georgian townhouse on St Stephen's Green, told through objects donated by Dubliners.

Museum · Rising

📮 GPO Witness History

Inside the General Post Office on O'Connell Street, the headquarters of the 1916 Rising, an exhibition tells the story of Easter week.

Market · Weekends

🥖 Temple Bar Food Market

Away from the late-night crowds, Meeting House Square hosts a weekend food market with local producers and street food.

Coast

🦭 Bull Island & Dollymount Strand

A sandy island in Dublin Bay with a long beach, dunes and birdlife, all within sight of the city.

Village · Coast

⛵ Skerries

A seaside town in north County Dublin with harbour walks, Skerries Mills and views out to the offshore islands.

Garden · Free

🌿 National Botanic Gardens

Free to visit in Glasnevin, with restored Victorian glasshouses. Glasnevin Cemetery, where Michael Collins and Daniel O'Connell are buried, is right next door.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & history

History and legend, clearly labelled.

History

🇮🇪 The Easter Rising, 1916

On Easter Monday 1916, rebels seized key buildings around Dublin and proclaimed an Irish Republic from the steps of the GPO. The Rising was defeated within a week, but the executions at Kilmainham that followed turned public opinion and changed Irish history.

You can still walk the route today, and the bullet marks on some buildings are still visible if you know where to look.

History

⚔️ Viking Dublin

The name Dublin comes from the Irish Dubh Linn, the 'black pool' where the River Poddle met the Liffey. Vikings founded a settlement here in the ninth century, and excavations at Wood Quay uncovered their streets and houses.

Legend

🐈 The cat and the rat of Christ Church

In the cathedral crypt lie a mummified cat and rat, found trapped in an organ pipe, frozen mid-chase. James Joyce mentions them in Finnegans Wake.

Legend

🔥 The Hellfire Club

On Montpelier Hill in the Dublin Mountains stands a ruined hunting lodge linked to an 18th-century club of wild young aristocrats. Stories of card games with the devil and a black cat have kept it notorious ever since.

It's a lovely woodland walk with views over the city, whatever you make of the legends.

Ready-made plans
Suggested days out

Realistic days, each focused on one part of the county.

🏛️ Historic Dublin

Ideal for: first-time visitors
  1. Morning: Trinity College and the Book of Kells (pre-booked).
  2. Midday: St Patrick's Cathedral, Christ Church Cathedral and St Audoen's.
  3. Afternoon: the Liberties, then Kilmainham Gaol (pre-booked).
  4. Tip: that's a full day. If it feels rushed, save Kilmainham for another morning.

🏺 Dublin museums

Ideal for: a rainy day
  1. Route: National Museum (Archaeology) → National Gallery → MoLI → EPIC.
  2. Note: the Chester Beatty is closed until the end of December 2026 for the EU Presidency, so check before you include it.

🌊 Coastal north Dublin

Ideal for: fresh air
  1. Morning: Howth harbour and the Cliff Walk.
  2. Afternoon: Malahide village and Malahide Castle and Gardens.
  3. Tip: both are on the DART line, and starting early gives you time for the walk.

🏖️ South Dublin seaside

Ideal for: a sunny day
  1. Route: Dún Laoghaire harbour → Sandycove and the Forty Foot → Dalkey village → Killiney Hill.

🦁 Dublin with children

Ideal for: families
  1. Route: Dublin Zoo → Phoenix Park (look out for the deer) → EPIC or the National Museum – Natural History.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
🚆 Getting around
DART, Luas, Dublin Bus and commuter rail. A car is unnecessary in the city centre.
🅿️ Parking
City-centre parking is expensive. Public transport is almost always easier.
🗓️ Best time
Spring and early autumn balance daylight and crowds. Summer has long evenings and busier attractions.
⏳ How long
Two or three days for the city, four or five with the coast and suburbs.
💶 Budget tip
The national museums, National Gallery and public parks are free.
🚧 Closures
Dublin Castle is closed until January 2027, and the Chester Beatty until the end of December 2026.

Opening times and booking rules change, so please check official websites before you travel. Useful links: Visit Dublin, Heritage Ireland, National Museum of Ireland, Transport for Ireland and Dublin Zoo.

Common questions
County Dublin FAQ
Is Dublin Castle open?
Not at the moment. The Dublin Castle campus is closed to the public while it hosts events for Ireland's Presidency of the Council of the EU, and is expected to welcome visitors back in January 2027. The Chester Beatty, in the castle grounds, is also closed until the end of December 2026.
Do I need to book Kilmainham Gaol and the Book of Kells?
Yes, book both in advance. Kilmainham is by guided tour only and sells out, and the Book of Kells uses timed tickets.
How many days do you need in Dublin?
Two or three days for the main city sights, and four or five if you want to explore the coast at Howth, Malahide, Dalkey and Dún Laoghaire too.
Do I need a car in Dublin?
Not in the city. The DART, Luas, buses and your feet will get you almost everywhere, and city-centre parking is expensive. A car only helps for the less-connected corners of the county.
Are Dublin's museums free?
Many are. The National Museum, the National Gallery and the National Botanic Gardens have free general admission, though special exhibitions may charge.
Have Two for the Row'd been to these places?
Some of them. We've filmed the 1916 Walking Tour, Christ Church, St Patrick's, St Audoen's, the Custom House and MoLI, and those are marked on the page. The rest of the guide is researched, and we'll add our own tips as we film more.
