County Cork —
Colourful Towns, Coastal Roads & Pure Adventure

Ireland's biggest county, with enough stories, scenery and surprises to fill several holidays.

There's no doing Cork properly in a single day.

Ireland's biggest county has everything: buzzing city streets, historic harbours, rugged Atlantic headlands, peaceful forests and some of the prettiest coastal villages in the country. You can kiss a famous stone, follow the Titanic's story and spend hours driving through spectacular scenery.

And just when you think you've seen enough, West Cork comes along with another beach, another village or another breathtaking stretch of coast. Whether it's a short city break or a long road trip, one visit probably won't be enough.

Cork city & harbour
City · Food

🧀 Cork City & the English Market

Cork city is full of character, history and that famous independent spirit. Start at the English Market, a historic covered food market packed with independent traders and local produce.

Then admire St Fin Barre's Cathedral, wander along the River Lee and soak up the lively streets. For something different, Cork City Gaol tells the stories of the people once held there.

Why visit? City breaks, food, architecture and local culture.

Castle · Gardens

💋 Blarney Castle & Gardens

One of Ireland's most famous attractions. Visitors from all over the world climb the castle to kiss the Blarney Stone, which is said to give the gift of the gab.

There's much more to the estate than the stone: woodland walks, landscaped gardens and the intriguing Poison Garden, home to potentially dangerous plants (look, don't touch!).

Why visit? Castles, gardens, folklore and history.

🧗 The climb to the Stone involves narrow, steep stairs. Expect queues at busy times.

Harbour town · Maritime history

🚢 Cobh

Colourful Cobh is one of Cork's most recognisable harbour towns, with steep streets, rows of brightly painted houses and a towering cathedral.

Its maritime history is remarkable. As Queenstown, it was the Titanic's last port of call in April 1912, a story you can explore at Titanic Experience Cobh, among other heritage attractions.

Why visit? Maritime history, photography, heritage and harbour views.

Island · Guided tour

🏝️ Spike Island

In the middle of Cork Harbour, Spike Island has been a monastery, a fortress and a prison. Its visitor experience brings that complex history to life through restored buildings, exhibitions and guided tours.

Why visit? Unusual history, island experiences and guided tours.

⛴️ Ferries leave from Kennedy Pier in Cobh, and a visit takes around three and a half hours. It runs daily from April to October, with weekend-only openings in the shoulder months, so book ahead.
Spike Island ↗
Family · Wildlife

🦒 Fota Wildlife Park

Near Cork city, Fota is a popular conservation-focused wildlife park where you can see a wide range of animals in spacious surroundings while learning about conservation.

Why visit? Families, wildlife and educational days out.

03
Head south-west
Kinsale, West Cork & the peninsulas
Town · Food

⛵ Kinsale

A colourful harbour town famous for its narrow streets, handsome buildings and food scene. It's rich in history, and it's a great starting point for exploring the coast west of Cork city.

Why visit? Food, history, pretty streets and coastal scenery.

Fortress · History

⭐ Charles Fort

Overlooking Kinsale Harbour, Charles Fort is a spectacular star-shaped fortress from the late 17th century. Explore its huge defensive walls and enjoy sweeping views over the water.

Why visit? Military history, architecture and coastal photography.

Scenery · Heritage

⛪ Gougane Barra

In the Shehy Mountains, Gougane Barra is one of Cork's most atmospheric spots. A small oratory stands on an island in a still lake, surrounded by wooded hills.

The place is closely linked with St Finbarr, Cork's patron saint, and there are lovely forest walks nearby.

Why visit? Peaceful landscapes, photography, religious heritage and walking.

Cliffs · Maritime

🌊 Mizen Head

On Cork's far south-western tip, Mizen Head serves up dramatic Atlantic scenery. The visitor experience includes the Signal Station and a spectacular footbridge across a chasm, with cliff-top viewpoints all around.

Why visit? Dramatic coastlines, maritime heritage and photography.

ℹ️ Mizen Head is Ireland's most south-westerly point, not its southernmost. That's nearby Brow Head. Access depends on weather and opening times.
House · Gardens · Seasonal

🏛️ Bantry House & Gardens

Overlooking Bantry Bay, Bantry House is a historic country house with impressive interiors and terraced gardens, offering a glimpse into the life of an influential Irish family against a stunning coastal backdrop.

Why visit? Historic houses, gardens and coastal scenery.

📅 Opens seasonally, on selected days. Check what's open before you travel.
Gardens · Boat trip

🌴 Glengarriff & Garinish Island

Glengarriff is a beautiful village on Bantry Bay, set among woodland. Just offshore, Garinish Island (Ilnacullin) is famous for its gardens, created in the early 1900s and thriving in a mild, sheltered microclimate.

You reach the island by boat, often passing a seal colony on the way.

Why visit? Gardens, coastal scenery, boat trips and nature.

⛴️ The gardens open seasonally, and boats depend on the weather. The boat fare is separate from garden admission.
Heritage Ireland ↗
Scenic drive · Cork & Kerry

🏔️ The Beara Peninsula

Some of Ireland's most spectacular, and still relatively quiet, coastal scenery. Winding roads lead through mountains, colourful villages and rugged Atlantic coastline. Castletownbere, Allihies and Eyeries are all worth exploring.

The Healy Pass is famous for its twisting road and dramatic views.

Why visit? Scenic drives, mountains, villages and quieter road trips.

🗺️ The Beara Peninsula is shared between Cork and Kerry, and the Healy Pass road crosses into County Kerry.

Hidden gems & unusual places

A mix of free outdoor places, towns and guided experiences, labelled so you can plan around them.

Hidden gem · Cliff walk

🗼 Ballycotton

A scenic fishing village in East Cork with a popular cliff walk and views out to the lighthouse.

Town · Music & food

🎶 Clonakilty

A lively West Cork town known for traditional music, history and great local food.

Beach · Free

🏖️ Inchydoney Beach

A beautiful sweep of sand near Clonakilty, with wide-open views along the coast.

Ruins · Free

🏚️ Timoleague Abbey

Atmospheric medieval friary ruins overlooking the water at the village of Timoleague.

Town

🏘️ Rosscarbery

An attractive West Cork town with historic streets and lovely coastal scenery close by.

Marine lake · Nature reserve

🛶 Lough Hyne

A remarkable saltwater lake near Skibbereen and Ireland's first marine nature reserve, famous for its unusual ecology. Specialist operators run guided night-time kayaking trips.

Headland

🧭 Brow Head

A dramatic headland near Crookhaven and the southernmost point of mainland Ireland.

⚠️ The terrain near the cliff edges is rough. Take care and stay well back from the edge.
Island · Ferry

⛴️ Sherkin Island

A peaceful island a short ferry hop from Baltimore, with beaches, walks and lovely sea views.

Landmark · Free

🤍 The Baltimore Beacon

A distinctive white navigation marker on the cliffs above Baltimore harbour, nicknamed 'Lot's Wife'. The views are terrific.

⚠️ It's an exposed cliff-top spot, so take care in wind.
Parkland · Free

🌳 Doneraile Estate

A lovely historic landscape in North Cork with woodland walks, parkland and heritage features.

Seaside town · History

🏰 Youghal

A historic seaside town with medieval landmarks, sandy beaches and a fascinating maritime past.

Woodland · Wetland

🌿 The Gearagh

An unusual, haunting woodland and wetland near Macroom, of real ecological importance. Much of it was flooded in the 1950s for a hydroelectric scheme, leaving tree stumps that emerge from the water.

History, myth & mystery
Myths, legends & mysteries

We've kept history and legend clearly labelled, because the best Irish stories are usually a bit of both.

Legend

💋 The Blarney Stone

Kiss the Blarney Stone, says tradition, and you'll be blessed with eloquence: the famous gift of the gab. There are all sorts of stories about how the stone came by its powers, from goddesses to saints to clever lords who could talk their way out of anything.

Its powers belong to legend, of course, but plenty of Cork people would say they never needed the help anyway.

History & ghost stories

👻 The ghosts of Spike Island

Spike Island's history is genuinely dark. It was a fortress, and in the 1800s it held thousands of convicts, many of them in harsh conditions. Later it was used again as a prison.

Unsurprisingly, it has gathered plenty of ghost stories over the years. Those are tales, not documented history, but the island leans into its spooky side with seasonal events. Exactly our sort of thing for Friday Night Frights.

History

🕯️ The Lusitania

On 7 May 1915, the ocean liner RMS Lusitania was torpedoed off the Old Head of Kinsale, with the loss of nearly 1,200 lives.

Rescue boats brought survivors and the dead into Queenstown, now Cobh, and many victims are buried in the town's Old Church Cemetery. A memorial on Cobh's waterfront remembers them.

Tradition

⛪ St Finbarr and Gougane Barra

Gougane Barra is closely associated with St Finbarr, Cork's patron saint, who tradition says founded a hermitage on the lake's little island before moving on to found the monastery that grew into Cork city.

Some legends go further, crediting the saint with banishing a monster from the lake. As always, enjoy the stories and don't take them too literally!

Ready-made plans
Four suggested itineraries

Realistic plans for a big county. The West Cork trip works best over two or three days.

🧀 Cork City & Blarney

Ideal for: first-time visitors
  1. Morning: the English Market and the city centre streets.
  2. Midday: head out to Blarney Castle.
  3. Afternoon: explore the castle gardens and Blarney village.

🚢 Cobh & Cork Harbour

Ideal for: maritime history
  1. Morning: Cobh, its cathedral and Titanic heritage.
  2. Afternoon: Spike Island, if sailing times allow.
  3. Tip: Spike Island takes around 3.5 hours including the ferry, so don't overpack the day.

⛵ Kinsale & the coast

Ideal for: food and history
  1. Morning: explore Kinsale town.
  2. Midday: Charles Fort.
  3. Afternoon: a coastal walk and a relaxed food stop back in Kinsale.

🏖️ West Cork road trip (2–3 days)

Ideal for: a slower road trip, best not rushed into a single day
  1. Day 1: Clonakilty, Inchydoney Beach and Rosscarbery.
  2. Day 2: Skibbereen, Baltimore (the Beacon and maybe Sherkin Island) and Lough Hyne.
  3. Day 3: Bantry, Glengarriff and Garinish Island, then a taste of the Beara Peninsula.
  4. Note: West Cork is a long way from Cork city, so base yourself out west rather than doing day trips from the city.

Before you go
Practical travel tips
👍 Best for
Road trips, beaches, historic towns, castles, islands, gardens, scenic drives and food.
🚗 Getting around
Cork city has good public transport, and the train runs to Cobh. A car makes a huge difference for West Cork and the Beara Peninsula.
🗓️ How long to stay
Two days for Cork city and nearby highlights; four to seven days to explore the county properly.
☀️ Best time to visit
Late spring to early autumn, especially for coastal trips and seasonal boats to Spike Island, Garinish and the islands.
💶 Budget ideas
Beaches, coastal viewpoints, historic town walks, parks and scenic drives.
☔ Rainy-day options
Cork City Gaol, museums, Titanic heritage in Cobh and other indoor attractions.

Important: County Cork is big, so don't try to combine Cork city and distant West Cork sights in one day. Opening times, ferries and access arrangements change, so please check official websites before you travel. For general visitor information, see Pure Cork and Discover Ireland.

Common questions
County Cork FAQ
How many days do you need in Cork?
Two days covers Cork city and nearby highlights like Blarney and Cobh. For West Cork, Kinsale and the peninsulas, allow four to seven days. Cork is Ireland's biggest county, so don't try to squeeze it in.
What's the best way to explore West Cork?
By car, slowly. Base yourself somewhere like Clonakilty, Skibbereen or Bantry for a few nights and explore an area each day, rather than driving back and forth from Cork city.
Is Mizen Head the southernmost point of Ireland?
No. Mizen Head is Ireland's most south-westerly point. The southernmost point of mainland Ireland is nearby Brow Head, near Crookhaven.
How do you get to Spike Island?
By ferry from Kennedy Pier in Cobh, which takes about 15 minutes. Tickets include a guided tour. It's open daily from April to October, with reduced openings in the shoulder months, so book ahead.
Is the Ring of Kerry in Cork?
No. The Ring of Kerry is in County Kerry. Cork's Beara Peninsula is shared with Kerry, though, and the Healy Pass crosses the county border.
